# Discussion: B4.1 停止规则的遗憾度量 — The Regret of Stopping

**Topic:** Online Marginal-Utility Estimation for Test-Time Reasoning — the regret of practical stopping rules (confidence / entropy / self-consistency) relative to an oracle stopping policy.
**Label:** [资源] · **Parent:** B4. 预算分配 / 推理经济学 · **Audit status:** KEEP (Tier-2, support). Cheap, mechanically verifiable, and time-sensitive: the same vendor charts that motivate it will pull the crowd in.
**Format:** 6 stages, each with an internal critic → refine pass.

## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

For a task instance x with a mechanically verifiable answer, let the model generate a reasoning trajectory segmented at reasoning-transition checkpoints k = 1, …, K (paragraph breaks, reflection tokens such as "Wait"/"Alternatively"). At each checkpoint k we can (in hindsight, via forced-answer probes) measure:

- **â_k** — probe accuracy: the verifier-checked probability that the model, if forced to answer at checkpoint k, answers correctly (estimated from N probe samples);
- **c_k** — cumulative compute spent up to checkpoint k (tokens).

Fix an accuracy–compute exchange rate λ (accuracy points per 1K tokens). Define the **utility of stopping at k** as

> U_λ(k) = â_k − λ · c_k.

The **oracle stopping point** is t*(x, λ) = argmax_k U_λ(k) — computable only in hindsight, from the full rollout.

A **stopping rule** π maps the *online-observable* history at checkpoint k — answer-span confidence, predictive entropy, self-consistency vote share among probe samples, their deltas, and c_k, but never the verifier label — to a stop/continue decision; it stops at t_π(x).

The **regret** of π on x at exchange rate λ is

> R_λ(π; x) = U_λ(t*(x, λ)) − U_λ(t_π(x)) ≥ 0,

and the paper's formal object is the **regret curve**: mean R_λ(π) over instances, as a function of λ and of task difficulty, together with its decomposition into **stop-too-early mass** (t_π < t*) and **stop-too-late mass** (t_π > t*, the quantified cost of overthinking). The core question (from the seed, near-verbatim): 继续思考的边际收益能否被在线估计？各停止策略相对 oracle 的遗憾是多少？— can the marginal benefit of continued thinking be estimated online, and what regret does each stopping rule incur relative to the oracle?

**Critic:** Four holes. (1) The oracle is a *construction*, not a ground truth: it depends on λ, on the checkpoint lattice (a coarser lattice mechanically shrinks measurable regret), and on â_k estimated from only N probes — and because t* is an argmax over noisy quantities, probe noise biases oracle utility upward and therefore inflates every rule's measured regret. The definition as written hides all three degrees of freedom. (2) Realized vs. expected regret is conflated: with stochastic decoding, both t_π and t* are random; regret must be defined per logged trajectory (the replayable object), with seed variance quantified separately, or the quantity is not well-defined. (3) The definition silently mixes the sequential axis (keep extending one chain) with the parallel axis (draw more samples): self-consistency vote share is used as an *online signal*, but drawing probe samples is itself compute — if probe cost is not charged into c_k for rules that consume the probes, the comparison is rigged in favor of self-consistency. (4) "Stopping rule" should be a policy class with calibrated thresholds, not a fixed heuristic — otherwise regret differences between rules are threshold-tuning artifacts.

**Refined:** Scope and instrument, made explicit. (i) Regret is defined **relative to the logged checkpoint lattice** — it is a lower bound on continuous-time regret, and a 2×-coarser-lattice ablation reports the discretization sensitivity. (ii) λ is swept over a log-spaced 7-point grid (~0.01–3 accuracy-points/1K tokens); every headline number is reported as a curve over λ, never at a single λ. (iii) â_k carries binomial noise ~±0.17 (1 s.e.) at N = 8; the oracle's max-of-noisy-means inflation is bounded empirically by recomputing t* with N = 32 probes on a 100-instance subsample and reporting the induced shift in mean regret as an instrument error bar. (iv) Regret is defined **per logged trajectory** (so all rule comparisons are exactly paired offline replays on identical logs), with seed variance measured by 3 repeated collections on a 200-instance subsample. (v) The study scopes to the sequential dimension; probe samples are a fixed, shared measurement overlay whose cost is charged identically to all rules (or excluded identically — both accountings reported), so no rule gets free parallel compute. (vi) Every heuristic rule's threshold is calibrated on a held-out dev split by minimizing dev regret at each λ; rules are compared as calibrated policy classes.

## Stage 2 — Motivation

Three layers, in order of citability:

1. **The immediate hook — [VERIFY, channel-relayed, do not cite without primary source].** Vendor-published charts circulating in mid-2026 reportedly show *negative marginal returns* at the top reasoning tiers of at least one frontier model family — the highest thinking budget scoring at or below the tier beneath it on public benchmarks. These numbers reached us through channel relays, not through a primary source we have inspected. They must not appear in the paper unless the primary chart/report is located and cited directly; until then they motivate the *project*, not the *paper*. If real, they are the first vendor-acknowledged instance of the inverted-U that the academic overthinking literature has documented for a year — and they convert "when to stop" from an efficiency question into a correctness question.
2. **The citable base.** The overthinking literature is now robust: shorter sampled chains outperform longer ones on reasoning benchmarks (arXiv:2505.17813 [VERIFY exact claims before citing]); overthinking measurably harms agentic-task outcomes (arXiv:2502.08235); a survey consolidates the efficient-reasoning subfield (arXiv:2503.16419); and error-accumulation theory predicts the inverted-U of accuracy vs. chain length (corpus: CoTLength, OpenReview:6QDFsYxtI1). Stopping rules exist in abundance (DEER, TrimR, CaTS, Best-of-∞ — Stage 3). What does not exist is an account of **how far any of them is from the achievable frontier**: every published rule is evaluated by end accuracy and token savings, never by distance to a per-instance oracle. A deployer choosing among stopping rules today cannot tell whether the remaining headroom is 2% or 40% of the achievable utility — i.e., whether the problem is essentially solved or barely started.
3. **The strategic clock.** The vendor charts, once verified and public, will pull many groups toward stopping-rule papers. B4.1's defensible position is not "another stopping rule" but the **measurement instrument the incoming wave will need**: a regret framework plus a replayable, verifier-labeled trajectory corpus against which any future rule can be scored. Resource/instrument papers benefit from arriving *before* the wave.

**Critic:** (1) The motivation leans on unverifiable vendor numbers in first position — a reviewer who cannot check them discounts the whole section, and if the primary source never materializes the hook evaporates. Rebuild with the citable literature first and the vendor story strictly as a deployment anecdote, or drop it. (2) "Deployers cannot tell how much headroom remains" asserts a user need without evidence anyone has this need — but note the need is *implied* by the existence of a dozen competing stopping papers with incomparable evaluations. (3) The urgency/crowd argument is planning logic, not scientific motivation; it must not leak into the manuscript. (4) Motivation layer 2 quietly assumes regret-vs-oracle is the right lens; a skeptic could say accuracy-per-token curves already answer the deployer's question. Answer that head-on: frontier curves compare rules to *each other*; only an oracle-referenced quantity says how much is left on the table, and only a per-instance oracle localizes *where* (difficulty, λ, stop-early vs stop-late) the loss concentrates.

**Refined:** Manuscript motivation order: (a) the overthinking phenomenon is established and costly — continuation can have *negative* marginal utility (2505.17813, 2502.08235, 2503.16419, CoTLength — each re-verified against its full text before citation [VERIFY tags resolved at writing time]); (b) a crowded stopping-rule literature evaluates by accuracy/tokens against each other, which cannot answer "how much achievable utility remains" or "where does my rule lose it" — that requires a per-instance hindsight oracle and a regret decomposition, which no published evaluation provides (claim to be re-verified against 2604.10739's evaluation section specifically — Stage 3); (c) deployment stakes: at scale, per-instance stopping is a continuous economic decision, and an unmeasured gap to the oracle is an unmeasured recurring cost. Vendor charts appear, if at all, as one sentence with a primary-source citation or not at all. The crowd-timing argument stays in this planning document only.

## Stage 3 — Related Work

**The critical adjacent — arXiv:2604.10739 (cost-aware evaluation with early stopping).** This is the closest published work and the one B4.1's delta must be sharpened against. As relayed, it proposes a *cost-aware evaluation framework* for reasoning models that incorporates early stopping — i.e., it already argues that accuracy-only evaluation is wrong and that stopping should enter the evaluation. **B4.1's delta, stated precisely:** (i) **regret against a per-instance hindsight oracle as the formal object** — 2604.10739 evaluates methods on accuracy–cost axes (comparing rules to each other under a cost-aware score); B4.1 evaluates rules against the *instance-level achievable optimum* reconstructed from full-rollout hindsight, which is what turns "rule A beats rule B" into "rule A leaves X% of achievable utility unclaimed, mostly by stopping too late on hard instances." (ii) **Online marginal-utility estimation from partial-trajectory signals** — 2604.10739's object is the evaluation score; B4.1's second object is the *estimation problem*: how much information about the correctness future is present in the mid-trajectory observable history, measured as signal AUROC per checkpoint and as the regret of a learned signed continuation-gain estimator. (iii) **A replayable artifact**: checkpoint-level verifier-labeled logs on which any future rule — including 2604.10739's own stopping procedure, which we include as an arm — can be regret-scored offline without model access. [VERIFY: obtain and read 2604.10739 in full before submission; if their framework already contains a per-instance oracle-regret construction, B4.1's (i) collapses and the paper must reposition on (ii)+(iii) alone — this is the single largest external risk to the framing.]

**The overthinking line (motivation, not competition).** arXiv:2502.08235 defines overthinking scores on agentic tasks (observed harm, no stopping-policy account); arXiv:2503.16419 surveys efficient reasoning; arXiv:2505.17813 shows preferring shorter sampled chains wins (a selection heuristic, evaluated by accuracy, not regret). [VERIFY all three against full texts; arXiv IDs relayed.]

**The stopping-rule zoo (the measured objects).** From the grounded corpus digest: DEER (OpenReview:NpU7ZXafRi) — confidence-threshold exit at reasoning transitions; TrimR (OpenReview:ofEkphaqg7) — verifier-based answer-stability trimming; CaTS (OpenReview:jrSc4RJXy1) — distilled calibrated-confidence head for adaptive sampling, whose own motivation concedes agreement thresholds "are not direct estimates of correctness"; Best-of-∞ (OpenReview:3qiCnLf3jf) — Bayes-factor stopping for parallel voting; JET (OpenReview:2u5ZRzDyS0) — RL-trained stopping (changes the policy; requires training access); AdaptiveThinking (OpenReview:2i6Rp0gCq6) — pre-decoding compute allocation; SAFER (OpenReview:kJmLmOvwLC) — conformal sampling-budget calibration (global, offline, coverage-not-regret); FirstImpression (OpenReview:2PP70tFY0S) — mechanistic diagnosis of overthinking. **Critic:** (1) The delta vs. 2604.10739 is asserted from a relayed summary — the paper has not been read; this is the section's load-bearing unverified claim and is at least honestly flagged, but the fallback framing (ii)+(iii) should be pre-drafted, not improvised on discovery. (2) Missing literature families: optimal stopping / secretary-problem and bandit formulations (the word "regret" imports expectations from online-learning theory — a reviewer from that community will ask whether R_λ is a regret in their sense; it is hindsight-oracle regret, a competitive-analysis quantity, and the paper must say so explicitly to avoid a definitional fight); prediction-with-expert-advice style baselines; calibration literature (confidently-wrong is a calibration failure); speculative decoding / early-exit-in-depth literature (different axis, worth one boundary sentence). (3) OpenReview corpus papers are proposals scraped from a local corpus — acceptance status and final claims unverified [VERIFY each before citing]. (4) The claim "none reports distance to a per-instance oracle" is an absence claim over a fast-moving field — soften to "we are not aware of," and re-search within two weeks of submission.

**Refined:** Add a "relation to optimal stopping theory" paragraph: R_λ is hindsight (prophet-style) regret with respect to a fixed trajectory realization, not no-regret-learning regret over rounds; cite classical optimal stopping and best-arm/bandit budget allocation as formal ancestors [VERIFY exact citations], and state why the classical machinery does not apply directly (the payoff process â_k is neither Markov nor observed — only proxies are). Add calibration literature for the confidently-wrong mechanism. ## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

- **SP1 — Constructing a credible oracle from full-rollout hindsight. [THE HARD PART.]** The oracle requires knowing â_k at every checkpoint, which requires *intervening* — branching a forced-answer probe ("…Final answer:") off the frozen trajectory at each checkpoint while the main trace continues. Two independent threats: (a) **intervention validity** — a forced answer at checkpoint k may systematically differ from what a genuinely-stopped model would say (prompt-format confound); (b) **probe noise** — â_k from N = 8 samples carries ±0.17 binomial s.e., and t* = argmax over ~20 noisy values inherits a positive utility bias, inflating everyone's regret. If SP1's error bars are larger than the between-rule regret differences, the instrument measures nothing. Everything else in the paper is downstream of this.
- **SP2 — A commensurable stopping-signal zoo.** Confidence, entropy, vote share, and answer stability must be logged *on the same checkpoints of the same trajectories* with identical probe overlays, so that rule comparisons are paired and signal comparisons are attributable to the signal, not to the harness.
- **SP3 — Regret estimation under the λ degree of freedom.** No single λ is defensible; the deliverable is regret *curves* and the stability (or instability) of rule *rankings* across λ — plus the stop-early/stop-late decomposition per λ, which is where the overthinking story becomes quantitative.
- **SP4 — Online marginal-utility estimation from partial-trajectory history.** The constructive sub-problem: train a small estimator (logistic / ≤2-layer MLP) mapping signal history at k to a signed predicted continuation gain, and measure its regret and its leave-one-benchmark-out / leave-one-model-out transfer. This is where "can marginal utility be estimated online?" gets a direct answer rather than an indirect one via heuristic proxies.
- **SP5 — Where regret concentrates.** Conditioning on difficulty deciles (baseline pass rate) and model family: does the oracle gap grow with difficulty, and does the dominant failure flip from stop-too-late (high λ) to stop-too-early (low λ)?

**Critic:** (1) SP1 is correctly named the hard part, but the plan under-specifies the validity check: comparing probe answers to natural final answers only validates the *final* checkpoint, where the model has converged — the confound matters most at *mid-trajectory* checkpoints where no natural comparison exists. Add a stronger check: on a subsample, actually terminate generation at checkpoint k (hard stop + answer elicitation, fresh decode) and compare with the branch-probe â_k — expensive but the only direct test at mid-trajectory. (2) SP4 risks scope creep into "yet another stopping rule"; keep it capped as one measured condition with a fixed tiny architecture, or the paper's identity (instrument, not method) blurs. (3) SP5 is analysis, not a sub-problem — but keeping it explicit forces the logging schema to carry difficulty labels from day one, so it stays. (4) Missing: a data-engineering sub-problem — 2.4×10^5 probe generations with prefix-KV reuse is the actual cost center, and if it slips, nothing else exists.

**Refined:** SP1 gains a two-pronged validity protocol: (a) branch-probe vs. natural-final agreement at terminal checkpoints (cheap, all instances); (b) branch-probe vs. true-termination â_k on a 100-instance × 5-checkpoint subsample (expensive, direct, mid-trajectory); plus the N = 32 re-probe bias bound from Stage 1. SP4 is frozen at two fixed architectures with no tuning beyond the dev split. A sixth engineering item (SP0: the logging harness with prefix-KV reuse and per-checkpoint schema {signals, verifier label, cost, difficulty}) is promoted into the build plan with week-1 priority, since every SP depends on it.

## Stage 5 — Methods

- **M1 — Task suites (mechanical verification is the [GIVEN] constraint).** Pilot: GSM8K (exact-match numeric), MATH-500 (expression equivalence), GPQA-Diamond (multiple choice) — ~500 instances each. Stage 2 adds AIME 2024+2025 (integer answers, all ~60 items, descriptive only) and LiveCodeBench (unit-test execution). Difficulty labels = per-instance baseline pass rates from the sampling model.
- **M2 — Trajectory logger.** Instrumented decoding: segment CoT at transition markers (paragraph breaks, "Wait"/"Alternatively" — following DEER's transition-point insight); at each of K ≈ 10–30 checkpoints, branch N = 8 forced-answer probes at temperature 0.6 off the shared prefix (KV reuse) while the main trace continues unmodified. Record per checkpoint: probe answers + verifier labels (→ â_k), mean answer-span log-prob (confidence), answer-span token entropy, modal-answer vote share (self-consistency), cumulative tokens c_k.
- **M3 — Stopping-signal zoo → rule bank.** Replayable rules over the logs: fixed-budget (stop at checkpoint j); confidence-threshold (DEER-style); entropy-threshold; vote-share threshold (the agreement family CaTS criticizes); answer-stability (TrimR-style: two consecutive modal answers agree); the 2604.10739 stopping procedure re-implemented as an arm [VERIFY implementability from their paper]; learned estimator (SP4). All thresholds calibrated per λ on a dev split.
- **M4 — Oracle construction via full-rollout hindsight.** t*(x, λ) = argmax_k [â_k − λ c_k] on the logged lattice; λ ∈ 7-point log grid. Instrument-error protocol from SP1 (N = 32 re-probe; true-termination subsample; 2× coarser lattice ablation).
- **M5 — Regret estimation.** Exactly paired offline replay: every rule on every logged trajectory; mean regret ± bootstrap 95% CI per (rule, λ, difficulty decile); normalized regret (÷ oracle-vs-never-stop gap); stop-early/stop-late decomposition; Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-instance paired differences with Holm–Bonferroni over rule pairs; Kendall's τ for ranking stability across λ.
- **M6 — Released corpus.** The full checkpoint-level logs packaged for offline replay — the [资源] deliverable: any future stopping rule becomes regret-scorable without GPU access.

**Critic:** (1) The probe overlay multiplies base decoding cost by K×N ≈ 160×; the mitigation (probe only at detected transitions, KV reuse, cap K on long traces, N = 4 on the largest model if vote-share correlation with N = 8 exceeds 0.95) must be validated in the week-1 dry run, not assumed. (2) Answer-inducing prompts can *change* downstream behavior if implemented carelessly — the design must guarantee the main trace never sees probe tokens (branch, don't inject); state this as an invariant with a test. (3) The 2604.10739 arm is listed but unread — it may require components (e.g., their cost model) that don't map onto the lattice; budget one week for adaptation or drop it with an explanation, don't let it silently vanish. (4) Entropy over the answer span is one of many entropy variants (token-level mean, max, trend); pre-register exactly which variants count as "the entropy rule" to avoid garden-of-forking-paths accusations. (5) No compute is budgeted for the true-termination validity subsample from SP1 — add it explicitly.

**Refined:** Week-1 dry run (50 instances, 1 model) validates: probe-cost projections with KV reuse, the branch-isolation invariant (unit test: main-trace tokens identical with probes on/off), N = 4 vs N = 8 vote-share correlation, and transition-detector checkpoint counts (target K median ∈ [10, 30]). Pre-registered signal definitions: one confidence (mean answer-span log-prob), two entropies (answer-span mean; last-64-token trend slope), one vote share, one stability rule — frozen before Stage-2 data. True-termination subsample budgeted at 100 instances × 5 checkpoints × 1 fresh decode ≈ 500 medium generations (noise-level cost). The 2604.10739 arm gets a hard decision date (end of week 2): adapted or dropped-with-note.

## Stage 6 — Experiment Design

**Arms (all replayed on identical logs — one collection pass, arms are free):** fixed-budget sweep; confidence-τ; entropy-τ (2 pre-registered variants); vote-share-τ; answer-stability; 2604.10739-style (conditional); learned estimator (2 architectures); oracle (upper anchor); never-stop (lower anchor).

**Scale & compute.**
- *Pilot (the paper for the deadline):* 1 model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) × 3 benchmarks × ~500 instances × K≈20 × N=8 probes ≈ 2.4×10^5 short probe generations + 1,500 full traces. With prefix-KV reuse: **~100–300 A100-hours ≈ 1 node-week.** Verification is mechanical; zero annotation.
- *Stage 2 (only if time permits; else post-deadline extension):* +1 open 32B-class model, +2 benchmarks, N=4 on the large model ≈ 600–1,000 additional GPU-hours. The API-model arm is cut from the deadline scope.
- Analysis is CPU-only offline replay; every additional rule, λ, or decomposition costs nothing after logging.

**Go/no-go gates.**
- *Week 1 (dry run):* probe cost within 2× of projection; branch-isolation invariant passes; checkpoint counts sane. Fail → fix harness before scaling; hard fail (cost >4× projection) → shrink to 2 benchmarks × 300 instances.
- *Week 2 (300-instance pilot slice):* (a) at least one signal's per-checkpoint AUROC vs. verifier label > 0.55 — else online estimation is vacuous and the paper dies (report as negative note, NO-GO for venue push); (b) oracle instrument error (N=32 re-probe shift) < smallest between-rule regret gap — else the instrument can't resolve its own question (NO-GO in current form; redesign probes); (c) no heuristic achieves regret statistically indistinguishable from zero across λ on ≥2 benchmarks — else the premise ("headroom exists") collapses and the paper becomes a short positive-surprise note (different, smaller venue).
- *Week 3:* freeze analysis plan; full pilot collection. *Weeks 4–5:* replay, analysis, writing. *Week 6:* internal review + polish.

**ICLR 2027 feasibility verdict (deadline Sep 25, 2026; today Aug 13 → ~6 weeks).** **CONDITIONAL GO.** The pilot-scale paper (1 model × 3 benchmarks, full regret framework, signal zoo, learned-estimator condition, released corpus) fits in 6 weeks *only if* collection starts within ~1 week and scope stays frozen — the replay design means all scientific iteration happens post-collection on CPU, which is what makes the timeline survivable. The known unknown is 2604.10739: reading it is a week-1 task, and the (ii)+(iii) repositioning fallback from Stage 3 is pre-drafted. Single-model scope is the paper's biggest reviewer vulnerability at ICLR; mitigate by shipping Stage-2's second model during the review period if accepted-with-revisions dynamics allow, and by leaning on the [资源] identity (instrument + corpus), where single-model pilots are acceptable. **Fallback venue:** ICML 2027 (abstract ~late Jan 2027 — comfortable, allows Stage 2 in full) or TMLR (rolling; well-suited to a measurement/instrument paper and indifferent to the crowd clock — but the crowd clock is exactly the reason to prefer ICLR).

**Critic:** (1) Six weeks with a 1-week harness assumption has zero slack for the usual vLLM/KV-branching engineering surprises; the dry run *is* the slack — if week 1 slips to week 2, the verdict flips to fallback, and that trigger should be written down now, not renegotiated under deadline emotion. (2) Gate (c) treats "heuristics are near-oracle" as a failure, but a robust near-zero-regret finding would itself be publishable and important — reframe: it is a *pivot*, not a death ("online stopping is solved: an audit" is a legitimate short paper). (3) The compute estimate ignores probe-elicitation for the *entropy/confidence* signals at checkpoints where no probe branches — confirm all signals derive from the same 8 probe branches (they do: answer-span statistics are computed on probe branches), and say so to avoid double-counting. (4) Review-period model additions are not allowed at ICLR (no new experiments in rebuttal by policy in recent years [VERIFY current ICLR 2027 rebuttal policy]) — don't build the mitigation on it; instead pre-emptively frame single-model scope in Limitations with the corpus as the extensibility argument. (5) The go/no-go AUROC threshold 0.55 is arbitrary; tie it to the decision-relevant quantity — minimum signal information that yields detectable regret separation at the pilot's n — computable in the week-2 analysis rather than fixed a priori.

**Refined:** Hard fallback trigger, written now: **if the harness dry run has not passed by Aug 24, retarget ICML 2027/TMLR and expand scope to Stage 2 — no exceptions.** Gate (c) reframed as a pivot to an audit-style short paper rather than a no-go.
