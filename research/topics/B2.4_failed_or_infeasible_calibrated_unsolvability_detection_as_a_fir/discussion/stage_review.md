## Stage 3 — "Method" (Analysis/Finding variant: the analytical apparatus)

**STAGE 3 VERDICT: PASS**

**Why:** Each construct is defined precisely *before* it is used — the oracle's two-condition certification (required + no-substitute), the false-blame/cop-out rates, and the design-versus-ceiling dissociation are all given in Problem Definition/Methodology ahead of any claim that invokes them. The inferential chain is licensed at its one load-bearing hinge, and the draft states it explicitly rather than asserting the dissociation by fiat: "calibrating a reader directly on the ground truth constrains only what it learns on its training instances, not whether that signal generalizes to held-out traces... if even this privileged reader cannot discriminate above chance on held-out instances, no attribution-system redesign could either, since the limiting factor is the trace's information content, not the reader's construction" (Methodology, Oracle-Calibrated Ceiling Probe). This is exactly the step that makes the ceiling probe non-tautological — a real methodological risk `pipeline.md`'s Stage 5 critique round surfaced and the refiner pass addressed — and the draft carries that reasoning through rather than dropping it. **Note for Stage 4:** the discharge test here isn't ablation (that's the method-paper bar) — it's whether the finding is falsification-tested, not merely correlational. This is the check that fired below.

## Stage 4 — "Experiments" (Analysis/Finding variant: falsification, not correlation)

**STAGE 4 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the first assembled draft:** The one question this stage asks in every archetype — is the claimed cause isolated from its strongest alternative — is well-served structurally: RQ4 is a genuine falsification design (an oracle-calibrated probe that could, in principle, come back showing no ceiling gap at all, not a test rigged to confirm the paper's premise), and RQ1's oracle-soundness check is a real validity test the whole benchmark depends on. But the first assembled draft's RQ subsections stopped at an "**Expected outcome**" line (e.g., RQ3: "for at least 3 of the 5 attribution strategies, FBR exceeds CoR by at least 10 percentage points") without a separately stated line describing what result would *refute* that expectation. Per the Analysis/Finding bar: "the strongest finding papers construct a case where, if the claim were false, the result would differ... demand at least one such test," stated as an explicit check, not left implicit in the expected-outcome wording. The underlying `paper.md` material already had this discipline (its own per-hypothesis "Fallback" clauses in Step 6), and it needed to survive into the assembled `paper_draft.md` as its own labeled line, not be folded silently into "Expected outcome."

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit **Falsification condition** line to all six RQ subsections, stating the specific, concrete statistical condition that would refute each expected outcome — not a restatement of the outcome's negation in vague terms, but a named threshold or comparison (e.g., RQ4's falsification condition distinguishes *two* distinct refutation directions — an information-ceiling refutation and a no-meaningful-gap refutation — rather than a single generic "if this doesn't happen" line), mirroring the rigor already present in `paper.md`'s own Step 6 fallback clauses.
