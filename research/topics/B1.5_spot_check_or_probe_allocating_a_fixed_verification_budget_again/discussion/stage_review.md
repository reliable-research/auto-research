**Why, quoting the draft:** The four threads in `paper_draft.md`'s Related Work are genuinely argued, not a citation dump — each ends on a problem-tied limitation (e.g., single-channel oversight "can characterize how much auditing a given safety level requires, but none can express the question of which composition... minimizes miss rate at fixed cost"), and the nearest-neighbor honesty check passes: the first thread (AI Control / Control Tax / CTRL-ALT-DECEIT) is unambiguously the closest prior work — it is the one reproduced as the actual baseline in Experiments ("reproduced at matched call budget from the closest prior budgeted-oversight work"), not merely cited in passing. **THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add a fifth Related Work thread naming the combinatorial-test-design literature that the $H$ flavor's construction technique is drawn from, and stating the delta plainly: that literature optimizes coverage completeness against unstructured faults under a static specification, with no notion of a strategic pass-rate-optimizing adversary, no cross-flavor cost meter, and no allocation-aware attacker — it supplies the construction technique, not an answer to the composition question.

## Stage 4 — "Experiments" (Analysis/Finding variant: falsification, not correlation)

**STAGE 4 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the draft:** RQ3 and RQ5 in particular are genuine falsification designs in structure (RQ3 pits mixed-vs-pure degradation under an adversary that adapts; RQ5 strips targeting structure while holding novelty fixed, to rule out the "it's just novelty" alternative explanation). But the first assembled draft's RQ subsections stopped at **"Expected outcome"** for all five RQs, without ever stating the converse — what result would *refute* each expectation. Per the Analysis/Finding bar: "the strongest finding papers construct a case where, if the claim were false, the result would differ... demand at least one such test," stated as a check, not implied. An "Expected outcome" alone is a hope; a stated falsification condition is what makes each RQ a pre-registered, breakable claim. This discipline already existed upstream — `paper.md`'s own Step 6 spells out an explicit **Fallback** clause for H1 ("If H1 fails, the paper's claim becomes: 'Under a fixed verification budget, uniform random spot-checking... is already cost-competitive...'"), for H2, and for H3/H3b, plus a re-attribution clause for the B-alt (novelty) control — but none of that fallback language survived into the first assembled `paper_draft.md`'s RQ1–RQ5 subsections, which carried only "Expected outcome" / "Hypothesis (plain language)" / "Metrics" / "Result: [placeholder]."

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit **Falsification condition** line to all five RQ subsections in Experiments (both `paper_draft.md` and `paper_draft.tex`), stating the specific observation that would refute each expected outcome — reusing the concrete fallback logic already present in `paper.md`'s Step 6, not inventing new content.
