# Capability, Policy, or Format? An Identification Framework for Black-Box Attribution of LLM Version Drift

## Abstract

When a deployed language model is upgraded, downstream behavior changes — but *why*? Did the model get smarter or dumber (capability), did its alignment policy move (policy), or did only its output style drift (format)? The distinction determines the correct response: fix parsers, re-review compliance, or re-benchmark. Existing longitudinal audits report blended drift numbers that conflate the three, and the field's most-cited audit misbooked a markdown-fencing change as a capability regression. We show that the naive "variance shares of an upgrade" question is unidentifiable as stated — shares are properties of the measurement instrument, and each source can masquerade as another — and we make identifiability itself the deliverable. Our framework consists of (i) a generalizability-theory measurement model over a pre-registered three-axis probe battery, in which axis shares are defined relative to a declared universe of admissible observations and reported with leakage, residual, and noise-floor terms; (ii) an explicit identification-assumption ledger A1–A5, each assumption paired with an empirical test or bound, including the calibration-transfer assumption that is only partially testable; (iii) calibration on construction-known control pairs with *measured* purity, mixed-dose recovery, and quantified masquerade tests that reproduce documented mis-attributions under controlled conditions; and (iv) overidentification checks — split-half agreement, scoring-rule swaps, and symmetric self-intervention controls that run on the commercial pairs themselves. A sequential pre-registered gate decides the outcome: a validated black-box drift-attribution instrument if the gate passes, or a quantified measurement critique — how many points of reported "capability drift" are reproduced by pure format or policy interventions — if it fails. Both outcomes correct how blended drift audits are read. Estimated cost: ~600k cached generations across ≥6 commercial and open-weight version pairs plus ~15 control pairs, $2–5k API + ~300 GPU-hours.

## 1. Introduction

Language-model APIs evolve through frequent version upgrades, and providers rarely disclose what a version bump changed. For applications built on these APIs, an upgrade can alter outputs even when prompts and downstream code are unchanged: regressions can outweigh aggregate gains and impede adoption (Lai et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2022), and enterprises now maintain dedicated prompt-migration processes to survive model evolution (Tripathi et al., 2025). Recent supply-chain work formalizes deployer-side governance of silent updates with risk-category test suites spanning functionality, formatting, and safety regressions — and explicitly lists "how to detect and *explain* model drift" as an open challenge (arXiv:2604.27789, 2026). Detection is largely solved: black-box change-detection tests exist at practical query budgets (Gao et al., ICLR 2025; arXiv:2602.11083; arXiv:2604.27405). Explanation — *attribution of the change to a cause* — is not.

The canonical longitudinal audit (Chen, Zaharia & Zou, 2023) illustrates the stakes. It reported code-generation "regressions" between GPT snapshots that were partly caused by newly added markdown fencing breaking an executability check: a **format** change scored as a **capability** loss, in the field's most-cited drift paper. The converse masquerades are equally real: a policy tightening scored as capability loss when refusals count as errors; a capability gain read as policy loosening when a model that now knows an answer stops refusing. An integrator told "format" when the truth was "policy" is worse off than with a blended alarm.

We ask the question these audits skip: **under what conditions is a black-box decomposition of version-pair drift into capability, policy, and format components identifiable at all?** Three observations shape our answer. First, variance shares are instrument properties, not model properties: double the format items in the battery and the format share moves. Any honest decomposition must therefore be *instrument-relative*, with the battery pre-registered as the declared universe of admissible observations — the move generalizability theory (Cronbach et al., 1972) made sixty years ago for exactly this problem. Second, the axes are latent causes observed through a many-to-one map; without assumptions the three-cause model is unidentifiable from behavioral deltas alone. We therefore write the assumptions down as a ledger (A1–A5) and pair each with a test or bound. Third, no ground truth exists on the commercial pairs of interest, so identifiability must be earned on construction-known control pairs and then *checked* on real pairs via overidentification tests that need no ground truth — most importantly, symmetric self-intervention controls applied to the commercial endpoints themselves.

The contribution is an **identification framework — assumption ledger, construction-known calibration with measured purity, masquerade falsification tests, and overidentification checks — for black-box drift attribution, instantiated on three axes.** The framework, not the axis trinity, is the claim; the basis is explicitly extensible if the residual is structured. The design pre-registers a sequential gate with two publishable outcomes: (a) if calibration passes, the field gains the first validated black-box drift-attribution instrument, upgrading longitudinal audits from "something changed" to "here is what kind of change, in what proportions, with what uncertainty"; (b) if it fails as the masquerade analysis predicts it might, the study delivers quantified masquerade evidence — how many percentage points of reported "capability drift" a pure format intervention reproduces — a measurement critique that directly corrects how audits like Chen et al. are read, plus the minimal access conditions (e.g., logprobs, paired system-prompt control) under which identification would be restored. Outcome (b) is not a consolation prize; it is the identifiability-honest version of the same contribution.

Why attribution rather than just format-robust scoring everywhere? Robust scoring removes format's contamination of capability *measurement*, but cannot report that format itself drifted — which is exactly what breaks parsers downstream. Format drift is a first-class outcome, not only a nuisance.

## 2. Related Work

**Drift phenomena and their mis-measurement.** Chen, Zaharia & Zou (arXiv:2307.09009) measure task-level drift, refusal changes, and code-format errors across GPT snapshots but report blended deltas; their markdown/executability incident seeds our motivation and their per-task dashboard is our naive baseline. Model-update regression is studied at instance level with negative-flip metrics and mitigations — distillation, ensembles, gated fusion, compatible adapters (Xie et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2022; Lai et al., 2023; Echterhoff et al., 2024) — treating drift as undifferentiated error to suppress, not attribute. Prompt-migration case studies (Tripathi et al., arXiv:2507.05573) document operational breakage across GPT upgrades.

**Detection, not attribution (2025–2026 neighbors).** Model Equality Testing (Gao et al., ICLR 2025) casts "is the API serving the claimed model?" as two-sample testing; B3IT (arXiv:2602.11083) detects API model changes token-efficiently from outputs alone; within-model reliable-change detection (arXiv:2604.27405) separates change from noise. These answer *whether* behavior changed; we consume such tests as our drift-vs-noise gate and answer *what kind* of change. "Test Before You Deploy" (arXiv:2604.27789) governs silent updates with risk-category test suites over functionality/formatting/safety — the same trichotomy we study — but tests categories independently without asking whether cross-category attribution is identifiable; it names drift explanation as open. Model-substitution auditing (arXiv:2504.04715) resolves a binary identity question; logprob-based update tracking (arXiv:2512.03816) yields change-points but requires logprob access, violating our black-box floor.

**Attribution with white-box access (boundary).** Abliteration separates refusal from capability in code LLMs by removing the refusal direction (arXiv:2606.05396) — our policy/capability distinction, but requiring weights; training-stage attribution via influence functions and per-token KL similarly needs pipeline or logit access. These works validate that the constructs are separable *given access*; our question is what survives at the black-box floor.

**Single-axis constructs the batteries build on.** XSTest (Röttger et al., 2024) and OR-Bench (Cui et al., 2025) separate over-refusal from genuine safety; they also warn that policy and surface lexical form are coupled in training — a caution against assuming probe purity. IFEval (Zhou et al., 2023) provides verifiable instruction-following items; EvalPlus (Liu et al., 2023) provides execution-robust capability scoring; length/style bias in judged evaluation (WildBench-line evidence) is why our policy judge sees style-normalized responses.

**Instrument dependence and variance decomposition.** FormatSpread (Sclar et al., 2024), template ensembles (Voronov et al., 2024), and multi-prompt evaluation (Mizrahi et al., 2024) show scores depend strongly on format choices — instrument sensitivity for one model at one time, not cross-version attribution. Statistical treatments add error bars, benchmark-variance accounting, and mixed-effects partitioning of evaluation-pipeline factors (Miller, arXiv:2411.00640; Madaan et al., 2025; Messing, arXiv:2604.11581; Biderman et al., 2024): they partition *evaluation* variance, not *model-change* variance, and none binds shares to identification tests. Schaeffer et al. (NeurIPS 2023) attribute claimed emergent abilities to metric choice — the same move we make against blended drift scores. Classical generalizability theory (Cronbach et al., 1972) named and solved "shares depend on the facet design" by declaring the universe of admissible observations; we import its formalism directly, and blind-source-separation identifiability results supply the formal analogy for "latent causes from mixtures need assumptions."

**Gap.** No prior work performs a capability/policy/format decomposition of black-box version-pair drift with construction-known controls as ground truth, an explicit identification-assumption ledger, and overidentification tests on the target pairs themselves. The closest works attribute with white-box access, detect without attributing, or treat style as a nuisance rather than an attributed component.

## 3. Problem Setup and Measurement Model

**Setting.** A version pair \(p=(M_p^-, M_p^+)\) from one provider, both queryable black-box: no weights, logits, training data, or changelog trust. A pre-registered probe battery \(B\) with axes \(a\in\{\text{cap},\text{pol},\text{fmt}\}\), items \(i\) nested in axes (declared counts \(\approx\) 1,500/1,000/500), and \(k\ge 5\) replicates per item per version under fixed decoding, queried in interleaved windows. Scored per-item paired deltas \(y_{i}\) arise from format-robust capability scorers, three-way comply/refuse/hedge policy classification, and format-feature extractors.

**G-theory decomposition.** We model \(y_{i}\) with a hierarchical variance-component (generalizability) model whose facets are *axis* (fixed), *item* (random, nested in axis), and *replicate* (random):
\[ y_{ir} = \mu + \delta_{a(i)} + u_i + \varepsilon_{ir}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(y) = \sum_a \sigma^2_a + \sigma^2_{\text{item}} + \sigma^2_{\text{rep}}. \]
The **axis share** of drift is the variance component attributable to axis \(a\)'s battery *in excess of the within-version noise floor* \(\sigma^2_{\text{rep}}\) (estimated from repeats and interleaved same-version windows), normalized on the simplex together with two first-class remainder terms: a **leakage share** (cross-axis regression: capability deltas explained by the item's format-feature deltas; policy classifications explained by surface lexical cues) and an **unattributed residual**. Per pair we report \((\text{cap}\%, \text{pol}\%, \text{fmt}\%, \text{leak}\%, \text{resid}\%, \text{noise floor})\) with 1,000-resample bootstrap CIs over items. Because the battery is the declared universe of admissible observations, shares are instrument-relative *by construction* — G-theory makes the dependence explicit rather than hidden — and share sensitivity to battery reweighting is a reported analysis, not a vulnerability.

**The masquerade matrix.** Before any experiment, we derive on paper which (true cause → measured axis) confusions are possible under which scoring rules: format→capability under strict parsing (the Chen et al. incident); policy→capability under refusal-as-error; capability→policy under knowledge-aware refusal; policy→format via refusal boilerplate loading on style features. These four named masquerades become experiments (Section 5, E4) that the pipeline must demonstrably defeat — or the paper quantifies precisely why it cannot.

### 4.1 Assumption ledger (each assumption paired with its test)

- **A1 (probe-axis alignment).** Each battery loads primarily on its axis; cross-axis leakage bounded by \(\lambda\). *Test:* empirical 3×3 confusion matrix and leakage shares on control pairs (E1); requirement: mean measured leakage lower-bound < 15%. Leakage is estimated by linear cross-axis regression on declared surface features and is therefore a **lower bound**; a random-forest cross-axis predictor provides a nonlinear upper-bound probe, and the gap between bounds is reported as part of the uncertainty.
- **A2 (additivity).** Axis effects combine approximately additively at item level. *Test:* interaction terms and dose-response linearity \(R^2\) on mixed-dose controls.
- **A3 (noise stationarity).** Within-version variance stable across query windows. *Test:* interleaved windows; same-version window-to-window drift must sit inside the repeat-based floor.
- **A4 (basis adequacy).** Residual share small and unstructured. *Test:* residual size + clustering of item-level residuals; a structured residual factor (e.g., verbosity/reasoning-style) triggers basis extension, never silent absorption into the nearest axis.
- **A5 (calibration transfer).** Leakage bounds estimated on open-weight controls transfer to commercial pairs. *Declared as the untestable-in-full assumption.* *Partial test:* calibrate on 7–8B controls, validate on the largest available open-weight pairs (70B-class), report leakage as a function of scale; if leakage grows with scale, commercial attributions carry intervals widened by the worst measured leakage.

### 4.2 Instrument design for approximate orthogonality

Capability items are scored format-robustly (fence-stripping, normalization, execution via EvalPlus-style harnesses) so format cannot move the capability score. Format items come in two strata: a pre-verified both-versions-near-ceiling set (so capability cannot move format features) and a **mid-difficulty stratum** with capability matched statistically post hoc, because the at-ceiling pre-pass under-samples exactly the region where format and capability interact. Policy items are stratified (safe-but-seemingly-unsafe / pseudo-harmful / genuinely-harmful, drawing on XSTest and OR-Bench constructions) so tightening and loosening separate. The policy judge sees style-normalized responses (boilerplate stripped, length-truncated); judge-swap agreement between two judge models is reported, with human validation on a 600-item stratified sample per version; policy shares are claimed only where judges agree. Residual leakage is *measured, never assumed zero*. The probe bank is shared with sibling topic B3.1 (one corpus, disjoint claims: B3.1 predicts breakage, we attribute composition).

### 4.3 Construction-known control pairs with measured purity

Controls are labeled **dominant-axis-with-measured-purity**, not "pure," on a hardness ladder:
- *Format:* same weights + chat-template swap / JSON-forcing / markdown-forcing decoding (clean end), then style-SFT (entangled end).
- *Policy:* same weights + system-prompt policy tightening (cheapest, purest lever), then safety-SFT per published recipes (entangled: ContrastiveOverRefusal-style coupling of policy and surface form is expected and measured).
- *Capability:* math/domain SFT (gain direction); quantization as a labeled **stress case** outside headline statistics (it measurably changes style too).

For every control we first *measure* its side-effect deltas on the other two axes (e.g., the boilerplate style shift induced by a policy prompt) and grade recovery against the **measured** dominant axis. **Mixed-dose pairs** compose two known changes at 2–3 relative strengths; the pipeline must recover shares tracking constructed dose — pure-pair recovery is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. A simulation-based power check sizes the control count before any SFT run; the simulator is a design tool only and contributes no evidence.

### 4.4 Overidentification checks on every pair — including real ones

Because ground truth is absent exactly where it matters, every attribution ships with internal-consistency checks that a valid decomposition must pass and an artifactual one will fail:
1. **Split-half battery agreement:** disjoint item halves must yield the same simplex point (ICC ≥ 0.7).
2. **Scoring-rule swap:** strict vs robust parser must flip the *naive* attribution but not the model's.
3. **Self-intervention control (co-primary, runs on the commercial pairs themselves):** the *identical* format-constraint wrap (and separately a system-prompt-policy wrap) is applied to **both** versions, with attribution tested on the *differential* response; a no-op wrap (whitespace/benign prefix) serves as placebo so that version-asymmetric prompt sensitivity loads on the placebo and is subtracted. A valid pipeline moves only the intended axis's share.
4. **Paraphrase stability** of shares.

### 4.5 Verdict logic

Attribution for a pair is **licensed** only if the sequential gate (Section 5, G1) passed in calibration *and* the pair's own overidentification checks pass; otherwise the pair receives a **quantified nonidentification verdict** listing which assumption's test failed and by how much. Nonidentification is a first-class, publishable outcome — the framework never forces shares.

## 5. Experimental Design

**E1 — Identifiability calibration (gate experiment, runs first).** 9–12 construction-known control pairs (≥3 per axis on the hardness ladder) + 4–6 mixed-dose pairs, all on open-weight 7–8B models with 70B-class transfer anchors (A5). Blind dominant-axis recovery, full 3×3 confusion (masquerade) matrix, leakage lower/upper bounds, dose-response \(R^2\). Everything downstream is conditional on E1.

**E2 — Real-pair decomposition.** ≥6 version pairs, minimum viable configuration ≥2 commercial vendors (snapshot-pinned endpoints with both versions live at study start, queried interleaved and immediately, all raw outputs cached for release) + 2 open-weight families (e.g., Llama-3→3.1, Qwen2→2.5) carrying the replicability burden. Output: axis-share simplex per pair with full uncertainty accounting.

**E3 — Self-intervention controls (co-primary).** For every real pair, the symmetric wrap + placebo design of §4.4(3): attribution-validity evidence *on the pairs of interest themselves* — the only check that does not rest on A5.

**E4 — Masquerade quantification (publishable regardless of G1).** Run the format-only control through a naive strict-parsing harness and quantify the fake "capability drop" (the Chen et al. incident reproduced under controlled conditions); the policy-only control through refusal-as-error scoring; the capability-gain control against refusal metrics. Then show the identified pipeline shrinks each masquerade below the noise floor — or report by how much it fails.

**E5 — Exploratory cross-vendor structure.** Descriptive simplex comparison, Kendall's W rank agreement with permutation CIs, clustering by vendor. Explicitly exploratory unless ≥10 real pairs materialize; the seed's cross-vendor question survives as structure-*description*, not hypothesis-*confirmation*.

**Baselines.** Blended aggregate delta (Chen-et-al.-style single number); refusal-rate-only audit; benchmark-delta-only audit; two-way ablation (capability+policy, no format axis — does the third axis change attributions at all?).

**Scale and cost.** ~3,000 probe items × 2 versions × 5 repeats ≈ 30k calls per pair; 6 real + ~15 control/dose pairs ≈ 400–600k generations. $2–5k API + ~250–350 GPU-hours (open-weight inference + ~12 small SFT controls) + ~40 annotator-hours; 1–2 researchers, 2–3 months. Feasibility risk #1 is snapshot deprecation mid-study; mitigation: pairs selected only if both endpoints are live at start, immediate interleaved querying, full caching.

**Sequential pre-registered gates.**
- **G1 (identifiability gate, after E1):** blind recovery ≥8/9 on the *clean-ladder* controls (binomial vs \(p_0=1/3\); safety-SFT and quantization excluded as labeled stress cases, reported on the full ladder), mean leakage lower-bound <15%, split-half ICC ≥0.7, dose-response \(R^2\) ≥0.6; thresholds carried with CIs and sized by the pre-registration power simulation. *Pass* → E2/E3 at full scale. *Marginal* (recovery 6–7/9 or leakage 15–25%) → "bounded attribution": interval-only claims. *Fail* → pivot, no full-scale E2.
- **G2 (real-pair signal gate):** on ≥4 of 6 real pairs, at least two axes exceed the noise floor and E3 is axis-specific; failure is reported as "drift is noise-dominated or A5 broken," not hidden.
- **G3 (structure gate):** evaluated only if ≥10 pairs; otherwise E5 stays descriptive.

**Pivot on G1 failure (the internal risk made productive).** The paper becomes the measurement critique: black-box three-way drift decomposition is not identifiable under A1–A5; here is the masquerade matrix (E4) quantifying how format changes are booked as capability changes in current audit practice; and here are the minimal access conditions (logprobs, paired system-prompt control, provider-attested changelogs) under which identification would be restored. Portfolio decision rule: strong G1 pass + E3 specificity → standalone mechanism paper consumed by B3.3/B3.4; marginal → merge as B3.3's mechanism section; fail → compact critique or cautionary section with released control-pair recipes.

## 6. Limitations and Threats

*Instrument-relativity is irreducible:* shares are claims about a declared battery, and we report sensitivity to reweighting rather than pretending otherwise. *A5 is the load-bearing untestable assumption:* the leakage-vs-scale ladder bounds but cannot verify transfer to frontier models; commercial attributions therefore carry widened intervals, and E3 supplies the only direct real-pair evidence. *Leakage estimates are lower bounds* (linear feature regressions); the nonlinear upper-bound probe brackets but does not close the gap. *Judge-mediated policy scoring* imports judge bias; style normalization, judge-swap agreement, and per-version human validation mitigate but cannot eliminate it. *Basis incompleteness* is a live risk: a structured residual triggers taxonomy growth, and forcing three axes on higher-dimensional change would guarantee mis-attribution — which is why the residual is a first-class reported term. *Snapshot availability* constrains E2; the minimum viable configuration and the open-weight anchors are pre-registered so the study cannot silently shrink.

## 7. Conclusion

The scientific center of gravity of drift decomposition is identifiability, not decomposition. The naive variance split is unidentifiable — format/capability/policy masquerades are real and documented in the field's most-cited audit — so we deliver an identification framework: an assumption ledger with tests, construction-known control pairs with measured purity, mixed-dose recovery, masquerade falsification, and overidentification checks that run on the commercial pairs themselves. A pre-registered gate converts the topic's internal risk into a two-outcome design: a validated black-box drift-attribution instrument that upgrades longitudinal audits from "something changed" to "here is what kind of change," or a quantified measurement critique that corrects how blended drift numbers are read. Either way, downstream auditors get what they currently lack: an honest account of what a version bump changed — and of when that question cannot be answered from the outside.
