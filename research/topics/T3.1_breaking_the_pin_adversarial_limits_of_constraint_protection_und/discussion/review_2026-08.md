## 2. Novelty verdict

**Overall: somewhat-novel / novel-at-margin — earned on the measurement rows (C2 strength normalization; C4 cost-ratio estimand; C5 boundary+ordering), NOT on the object of study.** The paper is an Analysis/Finding archetype; its 🟢 lives on the finding row (locating and pricing a break boundary), not on a technique row.

**Decisive finding — the nearest neighbor is uncited [major].** The draft's "a prior result already shows an optimization-based injection can break every tested model's pinning defense" is **Governance Decay** (arXiv 2606.22528, June 2026), which the draft never names — it sits under the "citation-provenance caveat (pending re-harvest)". Governance Decay is far more than a "prior result that pinning breaks":
- it **introduces** compaction-induced governance decay (the exact phenomenon and estimand);
- it **introduces** the Compaction-Eviction Attack (adversarial content biasing the summarizer to drop a policy) — the draft's attack premise;
- it **proposes Constraint Pinning**: a pinned buffer *exempt from compaction*, re-injected verbatim, with per-step integrity checking — i.e. the draft's `d_slot` (structural slot), `d_reinj` (verbatim re-injection) and `d_cksum` (checksum-verified) variants are GD's own defense, decomposed;
- it **reports the defender cost** (≈47 tokens, <0.5% of a ≥10k-token history) — half of the draft's "dual-currency cost accounting";
- it **names the residual failure mode**: pinning is defeated by *operator impersonation in recent context* — which is precisely the draft's template-role-forgery attack family `a_bb`.

So Governance Decay anticipates the object of study (the pinning taxonomy), the attack premise, the defender-cost half of C4, and the very failure mode C5 exploits. This does not sink the paper — GD's headline is "pinning **works** (0% at negligible cost)", whereas T3.1's contribution is to locate **where on a continuous strength axis that success turns into failure and at what attacker/defender ratio**, a legitimately different estimand — but it **caps the verdict until conceded**. A reviewer who knows GD (and GD is the field-defining June-2026 paper on this exact topic) will reject a draft that presents GD's defense taxonomy as its own object without naming GD.

**Named neighbors for every stance:**
- **Governance Decay (2606.22528)** — nearest overall; originates phenomenon + attack + pinning defense + defender-cost. (C1, C4, C5)
- **IH-Benchmark (2607.25987)** — nearest oracle instrument (predicate DSL + category-scoped LLM judges over 44 constraint families, tool-mediated conflict). Anticipates C3's two-tier structure. [moderate]
- **Security–Fidelity Tradeoffs (2606.30783)** — nearest cost-of-defense neighbor for C4. [moderate]
- **SlotGCG (2606.05609)**, **ChatInject (2509.22830)** — the two attack families (now WebSearch-verified as real ICLR-2026 arXiv records; the draft's "pending re-harvest" caveat on these is stale and partially dischargeable).
- **TensorTrust, LightMem** (draft's existing named neighbors) — remain valid as evaluation-design / mechanism analogues but are **not** the nearest neighbor; the draft currently mis-ranks them ahead of GD.

**Prescribed candidate neighbors — all dismissed as NON-neighbors** (different estimand: reward/test-suite hacking or failure-attribution, not adversarial constraint retention under compaction):
- EvilGenie (2511.21654), SpecBench (2605.21384), ImpossibleBench (2510.20270) — reward/test-case hacking in coding agents. Non-neighbor.
- Hardening Agent Benchmarks (2606.08960) — hacker-fixer verifier robustness; an attack/defense-loop *evaluation design* but on outcome verifiers, not constraint pinning. Non-neighbor.
- SWE-Marathon (2606.07682) — long-horizon SWE capability benchmark; touches "long-horizon compaction" motivation only. Non-neighbor.
- Who&When Pro (2607.09996), CHIEF (2602.23701) — failure attribution in multi-agent systems (B2 cluster). Non-neighbor.

**Cross-topic (T3.x) distinctness — CONFIRMED distinct.**
- **T3.2** (soft/hard decay mechanism attribution): estimand is the *decay-gap mechanism* (salience/framing/prior) under benign long context — not adversarial break boundary. Distinct.
- **T3.3** (adversarially robust *identification* of which spans are rules): attacks the *identification* step (evasion/spoofing of binding force vs register); T3.1 attacks the *retention* step under compaction. Different estimand (miss-pin/false-pin rates vs break-through boundary). Distinct, adjacent.
- **T3.4** (token overhead vs violation at *scale*, benign constraint-count sweep): shares "defender cost of pinning" but is benign-scale, not adversarial-strength. Distinct estimand.
- T3.1's estimand (adversarial break-through boundary + attacker/defender cost ratio under compaction) stays distinct from all three.

**Temporal check (t_audit = 2026-08-15).** All neighbors are prior/concurrent: Governance Decay 2606 (Jun 2026), IH-Benchmark 2607 (Jul 2026), Security–Fidelity 2606, SlotGCG 2606, ChatInject 2509. This is a **crowded, fast-moving cluster** — the durability/cost delta is real today, but at current velocity (GD + IH-Benchmark + cost-tradeoff all within ~2 months) the half-life is short; the boundary+ratio sweep should be run and staked promptly.
