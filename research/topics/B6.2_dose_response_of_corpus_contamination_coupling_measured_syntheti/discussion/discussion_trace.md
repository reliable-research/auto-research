## Seed brief

- **One-sentence description:** Measure the causal dose-response relationship between the exactly-known fraction of synthetic (LLM-generated) text mixed into a fixed-size training corpus and the resulting degradation in downstream capability and output diversity, determine whether a genuine safety threshold exists or degradation is monotonic, and quantify how much that verdict shifts when the synthetic fraction is estimated by a black-box AI-text detector instead of known exactly.
- **Apparent inputs:** a fixed pretrained backbone checkpoint; a provenance-guaranteed pre-2022 human reference corpus; a synthetic corpus generated by an instruction-tuned LLM and distribution-matched to the human corpus; a log-spaced grid of exactly-known mixing ratios; a fixed continued-pretraining token/compute budget; a black-box AI-generated-text detector.
- **Apparent outputs:** per-metric dose-response curves with seed-level uncertainty; a statistical verdict per metric among {flat, linear, convex power-law, change-point}; a quantification of the detector-induced shift in the estimated safety threshold.
- **Closest research areas:** model-collapse theory (Shumailov et al. 2024; Gerstgrasser et al. 2024); mixing-ratio optimization theory (PreventCollapseMixing); pretraining data-mixture optimization (DoReMi); benefit-framed synthetic-data pipelines (SyntheticCPT, NotAllLLMData); black-box AI-text detection (DandR); controlled-pretraining intervention infrastructure (TrainOnceAnswerAll).
- **In scope:** controlled continued-pretraining experiments measuring the dose-response of a within-corpus synthetic fraction on capability/diversity/tail metrics, plus coupling that measurement to detector-estimation error. **Out of scope:** full pretraining-from-scratch at production scale; multi-generation recursive collapse dynamics; fine-tuning/RLHF or multimodal settings; building a new, better detector.
- **Confidence: high.** As with the B5.5 exemplar, the "repo" here is a prior reverse-digest plus a pre-registered spec (`digest.md` + `paper.md`) that is already internally cross-referenced (N-node ↔ C/M-component ↔ RQ ids) and already carries its own asymmetry/taste self-check (`paper.md` Step 0/3). Reconstruction risk is low; the risk is redundancy with `paper.md`'s own Step 5/6 — Stage 5/6 below are framed as validating the **measurement instrument's properties** (confound isolation, metric validity, threshold identifiability, detector-coupling direction, scale transfer), not re-deriving `paper.md`'s hypotheses from scratch.

## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (proposer):
- `summary`: Given a fixed pretrained language-model backbone and a training corpus that can be exactly partitioned by provenance into human-authored and synthetic (LLM-generated) text, determine the causal function relating the synthetic fraction ρ mixed into a fixed-size continued-pretraining corpus to the resulting degradation in downstream capability and output diversity, and determine whether this function has a genuine threshold (below which contamination is statistically indistinguishable from harmless) or whether degradation is monotonic from the first synthetic token — including how this verdict shifts when ρ is estimated by a black-box AI-text detector rather than known exactly.
- `input`: (1) a fixed pretrained backbone checkpoint θ₀; (2) a provenance-guaranteed human reference corpus; (3) a synthetic corpus generated by an instruction-tuned LLM; (4) a log-spaced grid of exactly-known mixing ratios; (5) a fixed continued-pretraining token/compute budget; (6) a black-box AI-generated-text detector.
- `output`: (1) per-metric dose-response curves (capability, diversity, tail) with seed-level uncertainty; (2) a statistical verdict per metric among {flat, linear, convex power-law, change-point}, plus a TOST-certified "harmless" low-dose region if one exists; (3) a quantification of how much the threshold/curve-shape verdict shifts when ρ is replaced by a detector-estimated ρ̂.
- `constraints`: the synthetic fraction must be exogenously injected and exactly known (not estimated) for the causal dose-response estimation; the backbone and all training hyperparameters must be held fixed across every mixing ratio; the study is scoped to a fixed continued-pretraining budget, not full pretraining-from-scratch.
- `objective`: a curve-family verdict (flat/linear/convex/change-point) is accepted only if it wins a stated model-comparison criterion over the seed-level data and the corresponding statistical test achieves significance at a pre-registered α, with a TOST equivalence margin defining "harmless" — a mere point-estimate degradation without this adjudication does not count as a verdict.
- `study_type`: `empirical` — stated explicitly, not silently inferred: digest N12 already settles this ("what N9 would validate is a set of measured dose-response curves and statistical verdicts about an existing phenomenon... the same shape as prior best-paper controlled empirical studies... no new system, dataset, or benchmark is the headline contribution").

## Stage 2 — Motivation

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1:**
- `summary`: Corpus curators and pretraining teams increasingly need to decide how much synthetic (LLM-generated) contamination in a training corpus is tolerable, but existing guidance is either purely theoretical (mixing-ratio optima derived in simplified regression settings) or characterizes only the extreme dose (full replacement across recursive generations) — nobody has measured the actual shape of the dose-response function at realistic, partial contamination levels for language models.
- `why_it_matters`: As synthetic text becomes a de facto, largely uncontrolled component of web-scraped pretraining corpora, curators need more than a binary "collapse happens under recursive full-replacement" warning — they need to know whether there is a tolerable ceiling (a genuine safe region) or whether harm accrues from the first synthetic token, and whether a black-box AI-text detector's fraction estimate can be trusted as a policy input at all, since detector estimation error propagating into the estimated safety threshold in an uncharacterized way means a curation policy calibrated on detector output could be silently miscalibrated in a specific, predictable direction — not just noisy.
- `who_cares`: teams curating pretraining corpora at scale who must set a tolerable contamination ceiling; researchers relying on the model-collapse literature's mixing-ratio theory to justify practical thresholds outside the linear-regression regime it was derived in; developers and adopters of AI-text detectors who market fraction estimates as content-safety signals without characterizing what estimation error implies for a downstream harm decision.
- `evidence`: `[PreventCollapseMixing, OpenReview:9RdhTvYbX0]`'s own motivation restricts its analysis to "low-dimensional or simplified settings"; `[Shumailov et al., 2024]` and `[Gerstgrasser et al., 2024]` contrast only two regimes (replace vs. accumulate) at the far end of the dose axis, never a continuous fraction; `[DandR, OpenReview:FiMZSxo4DO]` stops at per-document detection and never aggregates to a corpus-level contamination estimate, let alone couples it to training harm — together these show that a measured, LM-scale dose-response-to-harm coupling has never been produced.

**critique:**
- summary: Stake-specific, evidence-backed motivation, but coverage of Stage 1's plural output is incomplete.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "why_it_matters", anti_pattern: "partial_output_coverage", comment: "Stage 1 output (1) explicitly promises curves 'per capability, diversity, and tail metric' — plural and metric-specific, not a single aggregate number. why_it_matters motivates why a threshold matters in the aggregate, but never explains why a curator should care whether the verdict differs *across* metric families. Without that, a reader could reasonably ask: why not just report one clean threshold number and be done? This is exactly the kind of unaddressed gap the base rubric asks about."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `why_it_matters` (extended): "...moreover, a threshold calibrated on one endpoint (say, few-shot benchmark accuracy) is only actionable if it also holds for the properties curators actually ship to users. If diversity and tail behavior erode well before benchmark accuracy visibly moves, a curator who monitors only capability metrics would certify a corpus as 'safe' at a contamination level that has already measurably narrowed what the model can say — silently trading away the property nobody was watching. Tracking the verdict per metric family, not just in the aggregate, is what turns a single threshold number into an actionable monitoring policy rather than a false sense of precision."
- `changes_made`: added the metric-family coverage argument closing the partial-output-coverage gap.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 2.**

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (5 sub-problems, from digest N5's C1–C5):

- **`sp_confound_isolation`** — *description:* Isolate the causal effect of the synthetic fraction ρ from confounds of token budget, compute, data ordering, and generator-quality/distribution shift. *why_it_is_a_problem:* without this, an observed degradation could reflect a distribution-shift or budget artifact rather than syntheticness itself, invalidating any dose-response claim before it is made. *technical_challenge:* every non-dose axis (token count, hyperparameters, starting checkpoint, shuffling policy) must be held bit-for-bit identical across every mixing ratio, and the synthetic pool's document-length/topic distribution must independently be verified to match the human pool's — synthetic generation processes naturally drift from the reference distribution unless explicitly corrected, so matching is a real filtering problem, not a default property. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no cited work solves corpus-matching-as-confound-control for an LM-scale synthetic/human dose axis.
- **`sp_metric_validity`** — *description:* Define capability and diversity metrics that are comparable, sensitive, and non-self-fabricating across a fine dose grid at small model scale. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a dose-response curve is only interpretable if the measuring instrument doesn't itself manufacture or dissolve an apparent threshold. *technical_challenge:* few-shot accuracies are noisy and near-floor at sub-1B scale, so a discontinuous metric can produce a false change-point that a continuous metric on the same underlying behavior would not show. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_closerlook_diffusion_domain"]` — CloserLookCollapse's entropy indicator is diagnostic for image-diffusion collapse but "not connected to language-model capability benchmarks"; this sub-problem is the LM-side generalization of closing that gap.
- **`sp_threshold_identifiability`** — *description:* Distinguish a genuine statistical change-point (a hard safety threshold) from gradual monotonic degradation under realistic seed-level noise. *why_it_is_a_problem:* "small but non-zero effect at low dose" and "true zero effect below a threshold" are statistically adjacent but decision-theoretically opposite claims — one implies a genuine safe ceiling exists, the other that none does. *technical_challenge:* requires a log-spaced low-dose grid, multiple seeds, pre-specified equivalence margins, and explicit model comparison across nested and non-nested curve families; no amount of eyeballing a scatter plot resolves this. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_gerstgrasser_two_regime_not_continuous"]` — Gerstgrasser's two-regime contrast cannot in principle distinguish threshold from gradual decline, since it never sweeps a continuous dose.
- **`sp_dose_axis_measurement_error`** — *description:* Determine how much the dose-response threshold verdict shifts when the mixing fraction is estimated by an imperfect black-box detector rather than known exactly. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a dose-response law derived under known, injected ratios is only actionable for a real curator if it survives being applied through the noisy detector they would actually have to use. *technical_challenge:* the detector's error structure as a function of true ρ is unknown a priori and must be measured empirically by re-running the entire curve-fitting pipeline on detector output rather than ground truth — an errors-in-variables problem, not a simple sensitivity check. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_dandr_detection_only"]` — DandR-style detectors stop at per-document classification and never characterize corpus-level fraction-estimation error, let alone its propagation into a downstream harm threshold.
- **`sp_scale_generalization`** — *description:* Determine whether the fitted dose-response curve shape and dose-coefficient magnitudes are stable across backbone scale and synthetic-text generator, given that the whole protocol is a small-model, fixed-backbone continued-pretraining proxy. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a dose-response law measured at one scale/generator combination is a case study, not a policy input, unless there is evidence the same functional family holds elsewhere. *technical_challenge:* requires an actual multi-cell factorial design (multiple backbones × multiple generators), and even a full match across tested cells only supports a bounded, honestly-scoped transfer claim, never an unconditional one. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no cited work tests transfer of an empirical LM dose-response law across scale/generator; PreventCollapseMixing's scale-invariance is asymptotic-regression-theoretic, not an empirical claim requiring an empirical check.

**critique:**
- summary: Coverage checks out (solving all five would jointly deliver Stage 1's objective), but two sub-problems risk reading as the same underlying issue restated.
- comments:
  - `{severity: minor, field: "sp_threshold_identifiability / sp_dose_axis_measurement_error", anti_pattern: "overlap", comment: "Both invoke 'change-point vs. continuous/gradual' language, which can read as the same sub-problem twice. They are genuinely distinct — one is about statistical identifiability under sampling noise on the *response* (given the true dose axis), the other is about measurement error on the *covariate itself* (the dose axis being mismeasured by an imperfect instrument) — but the descriptions don't make that response-noise-vs-covariate-noise distinction explicit enough."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `sp_threshold_identifiability.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is a **response-noise identifiability** problem: separating signal from sampling noise given that the true dose ρ is known exactly. It persists even with a perfect detector."
- `sp_dose_axis_measurement_error.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is a distinct **covariate (errors-in-variables) measurement** problem: even with infinite seeds and zero sampling noise on the response, the dose axis itself is mismeasured whenever ρ is read off a real detector instead of known by construction — no amount of additional seeds fixes it, unlike the response-noise problem above."
- `changes_made`: made the response-noise-vs-covariate-noise distinction explicit in both `technical_challenge` fields.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

## Stage 5 — Method Design

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (5 methods):

- **`m_matched_corpus_harness`** → targets `sp_confound_isolation`. *proposed_approach:* Build a provenance-controlled corpus pair (human pool from a fixed pre-2022 corpus slice; synthetic pool from an instruction-tuned LLM prompted to regenerate/continue documents from a disjoint slice, filtered by rejection sampling to match the human pool's document-length and topic-cluster distributions), mix at document granularity into a log-spaced grid of exactly-known ratios, and train every ratio from an identical starting checkpoint with an identical token budget, hyperparameters, and shuffling-seed policy. Additionally run a budget-matched control in which the "contaminant" is human text oversampled until its measured entropy matches the synthetic pool's. *key_insight:* Matching-then-freezing turns "confound-free" from an assumption into a checkable gate, but matching alone cannot rule out that *any* distributionally-narrow text (not specifically synthetic text) would produce the same curve — an entropy-matched all-human control is the one design element that can tell "synthetic" apart from "merely narrow." *hypotheses:* ["A budget-matched human-text control whose distributional entropy is oversampled to match the synthetic pool's measured entropy will show a dose-response curve statistically distinguishable from the synthetic-fraction curve (non-overlapping fits, interaction p < 0.05) — demonstrating the measured effect is attributable to syntheticness specifically, not to generic distributional narrowing."]
- **`m_paired_metric_battery`** → targets `sp_metric_validity`. *proposed_approach:* Measure every checkpoint on a pre-registered battery pairing each discontinuous capability metric (few-shot accuracy) with a continuous counterpart (held-out perplexity as the sole pre-registered primary endpoint), plus diversity (distinct-n, Self-BLEU, embedding coverage) and tail/entropy endpoints (rare-token recall, tail-decile perplexity, nearest-neighbor differential entropy) known to be collapse-sensitive from the image-diffusion literature. *key_insight:* A discontinuous metric evaluated on a coarse grid can manufacture an apparent change-point that is a property of the metric's resolution, not the underlying model; pairing every discontinuous endpoint with a continuous sibling on identical checkpoints is what lets a later analysis attribute an apparent threshold to the model or to the metric. *hypotheses:* ["Diversity and tail/entropy endpoints will show a significantly earlier onset dose (first Holm-significant deviation from ρ=0) than capability endpoints, and any apparent change-point detected on a discontinuous few-shot-accuracy metric will fail to replicate (Davies test non-significant) on its paired continuous metric evaluated on the identical checkpoints."]
- **`m_curve_adjudication`** → targets `sp_threshold_identifiability`. *proposed_approach:* Fit four candidate response-shape families (flat, linear, convex power-law, segmented change-point) to seed-level per-metric data via mixed-effects regression, compare families by AICc, test change-point existence with a Davies test, test monotone trend with Jonckheere–Terpstra, and declare a dose "statistically harmless" only via a pre-registered TOST equivalence test against ρ=0 with a margin fixed before the confirmatory grid is run. *key_insight:* "Small effect" and "true zero effect" are statistically adjacent but decision-theoretically opposite claims; the only way to tell them apart is to require the equivalence test to positively certify the null rather than merely fail to reject it, and to require the change-point family to win a model-comparison contest rather than being assumed from a plot. *hypotheses:* ["On the primary endpoint, the convex power-law family will win the AICc comparison (weight ≥ 0.5) with fitted exponent γ̂ > 1 (95% CI excluding 1), the Davies change-point test will be non-significant (p ≥ 0.05, Holm-corrected), and the TOST-certified harmless region will be non-empty but confined to the lowest 1–2 grid doses — a soft, resolution-limited safe region rather than a genuine mechanistic threshold."]
- **`m_detector_coupling`** → targets `sp_dose_axis_measurement_error`. *proposed_approach:* Run two independent black-box AI-text-detector instruments over a fixed document sample from every mixture, aggregate per-document scores into an estimated corpus-level fraction ρ̂ via a calibration curve fit on the two pure pools, then re-run the `m_curve_adjudication` pipeline substituting ρ̂ for the known ρ and report the shift in estimated threshold location and curve-shape verdict. *key_insight:* Detector error on the dose axis is an errors-in-variables problem, not simple additive noise on the outcome, because the detector is calibrated to separate individual documents rather than estimate an aggregate rate — re-fitting the same adjudication pipeline on ρ̂ is what turns the resulting bias direction and magnitude into an empirical finding rather than a theoretical worry. *hypotheses:* ["Re-fitting on detector-estimated ρ̂ will attenuate the fitted dose-response slope on the primary endpoint (slope ratio b̂(ρ̂)/b̂(ρ) ≤ 0.8) and will widen the TOST-certified harmless region by at least one grid step relative to the ground-truth fit, with the calibration error e(ρ) = ρ̂(ρ) − ρ negative (under-estimating) at low ρ — i.e., detector noise biases a contamination policy toward permissiveness, not merely toward imprecision."]
- **`m_factorial_transfer_check`** → targets `sp_scale_generalization`. *proposed_approach:* Run the confirmatory dose grid as a factorial design crossing two backbone scales and two independent synthetic-text generators on a coarser dose sub-grid, fit the interaction of dose with scale and with generator identity in a pooled mixed-effects model, and check whether the AICc-winning curve family (not just its coefficients) is identical across all four backbone-by-generator cells. *key_insight:* curve-shape transfer and coefficient transfer are different claims with different policy consequences — a curve that keeps the same shape but different steepness across scale is still a usable law with a scale-adjustment factor, whereas a curve whose winning family itself flips means no single law transfers and per-regime calibration is unavoidable. *hypotheses:* ["The AICc-winning curve family on the primary endpoint will be identical across all four tested backbone-scale × generator cells, while the fitted dose-coefficient magnitude will differ significantly by scale (dose × scale interaction p < 0.05)."]

**critique:**
- summary: Every method has a real mechanism and a falsifiable hypothesis, but two issues need attention before moving on: one hypothesis bundles three conjuncts without saying what a partial failure means, and one method's directional prediction isn't yet derived from anything.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "m_curve_adjudication.hypotheses", anti_pattern: null, comment: "The hypothesis ANDs three separate conditions (AICc family win, non-significant Davies test, non-empty TOST region). If one conjunct fails while the others hold — e.g., the change-point family wins instead — what does that mean for the paper's headline claim? As written, the hypothesis reads as unfalsifiable-by-parts rather than a single coherent falsifiable claim with a stated consequence for each partial failure."}`
  - `{severity: minor, field: "m_detector_coupling.key_insight", anti_pattern: "weak_key_insight", comment: "The key_insight explains *why* detector bias could be non-trivial (an errors-in-variables argument), but not *why the specific direction* (permissive/under-estimating at low ρ) follows from anything, rather than being an assumed direction. The hypothesis predicts a signed bias; the key_insight should derive the sign, not just the existence of bias."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass
