## N1/N2 Motivation (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.motivation`)
Pre-training data detection for large language models aims to determine whether a given text was included in a model’s training corpus using only black-box or grey-box access. Existing approaches largely adapt membership inference heuristics from smaller-scale models, relying on raw likelihoods, losses, or calibrated variants thereof. However, large-scale evaluations show that these heuristics perform inconsistently in realistic LLM settings, often degrading to near-random performance when training and non-training data are closely matched, as demonstrated in @duan2024mimir.

The strongest reference-free baseline, Min-K%, introduced in @shi2024mink, aggregates the lowest-probability tokens under the hypothesis that unseen texts contain rare outliers. While effective empirically, this approach lacks a principled connection to the LLM training objective and remains sensitive to context-dependent probability scales. At the same time, reference-based calibration methods achieve stronger performance but require auxiliary models or external signals, as highlighted in @carlini2021extracting, limiting practicality and interpretability.

This gap matters because pre-training data detection is increasingly used to audit memorization, copyright risk, and benchmark contamination. A detector grounded in the optimization geometry of LLM training itself would offer greater robustness and explanatory power. The opportunity, therefore, is to derive a detection criterion directly from maximum likelihood training, avoiding ad-hoc heuristics and external references while remaining compatible with discrete, autoregressive LLMs.

## N6 Methodology (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.method`)
We propose Min-K%++, a theoretically grounded, reference-free method that detects pre-training data by identifying whether tokens form local maxima of an LLM’s conditional distribution. The core idea is to reinterpret maximum likelihood training through implicit score matching and operationalize local maximality in discrete token spaces via normalized comparisons to the full conditional categorical distribution.

1. **Implicit Local-Maxima Characterization**: Building on the equivalence between maximum likelihood and implicit score matching established in @hyvarinen2005score and @koehler2022score, we interpret training samples as points where first-order gradients vanish and curvature is negative. This motivates treating training tokens as local maxima along each input dimension, providing a principled target for detection.

2. **Token-Level Distributional Z-Score**: For each token, we compute its log-probability relative to the mean and standard deviation of the entire conditional distribution. This replaces raw likelihoods used in @shi2024mink with a normalized score that reflects whether the token is a mode or near-mode, inspired by calibration insights from @guo2017calibration.

3. **Mode-Sensitive Calibration Without References**: Unlike reference-based calibration in @carlini2021extracting, the normalization uses only statistics internal to the target model. This yields an input-adaptive calibration that accounts for varying entropy and sharpness of conditional distributions, aligning with memorization-induced peakedness observed in @dong2024memorization.

4. **Min-K% Aggregation for Robustness**: We retain the Min-K% aggregation strategy from @shi2024mink, averaging the lowest-scoring fraction of token-level z-scores. This aggregates local evidence across a sequence and mitigates noise from easy or generic tokens.

Together, these components form a coherent system: the theoretical characterization defines the signal of interest, the normalized token score measures proximity to that signal, and the Min-K% aggregation yields a stable sequence-level detector. The result is a robust, interpretable, and practical method for pre-training data detection.
