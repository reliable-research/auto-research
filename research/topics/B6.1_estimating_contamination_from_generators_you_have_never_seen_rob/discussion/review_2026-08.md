## 5. Feasibility + staleness findings (se-paper-reviewer lens)

### N1 [major] — The named nearest neighbor is in the wrong modality, because the text-modality neighbors were missed

**Where:** Abstract ("address unseen-generator degradation only in the image modality"); Introduction ¶3 ("The closest work by far is a detector-generalization study in the image modality"); Related Work ¶1 and the "Detector generalization to unseen generators" paragraph.

**Evidence:** MAGE (Li et al., arXiv:2305.13242, ACL 2024) builds an explicit out-of-distribution testbed for machine-generated **text** detection across unseen generators and unseen domains. RAID (Dugan et al., arXiv:2405.07940, ACL 2024) is a shared benchmark evaluating detector robustness across multiple generator families **crossed with adversarial attacks including paraphrase** — i.e., it already occupies both of B6.1's stress axes, in text. Neither appears in `refs.bib`. `experiment_design.md` line 14 nonetheless names "MGTBench- and RAID-style" collections as the study's synthetic-side datasets — the work is used as an instrument while being denied in the claim space.

**Consequence:** the sentence "addresses unseen-generator degradation only in the image modality" is false, and R2 (domain/related-work) would flag it as a hidden neighbor. Per the SE reviewer survey, hiding a neighbor is worse than conceding it: conceding RAID *strengthens* B6.1, because RAID demonstrates the degradation is real at the per-document level and thereby makes the aggregate-level question urgent rather than speculative.

**Fix:** concede RAID and MAGE by name; narrow the delta to the estimand (per-document accuracy vs. corpus-wide proportion) and to the identification design (neither runs a capability-matched swap, neither runs a quantification correction, neither reports an aggregate-proportion error).

### N2 [major] — The estimand-row nearest neighbor is mis-identified; the canonical non-cooperative corpus-proportion estimator is uncited

**Where:** Related Work ¶1 (concedes only arXiv:2607.00224, a cooperative-watermark sample-complexity analysis, on the estimand dimension); Introduction ¶3 ("A calibration layer ... its target remains a per-passage label, not a corpus-wide ratio").

**Evidence:** Liang et al., *Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale* (arXiv:2403.07183, ICML 2024) fits a distributional maximum-likelihood mixture to estimate the **fraction** α of AI-modified content in a corpus, with **no watermark and no per-document labels**, and deploys it on ICLR/NeurIPS review pools; the companion *Mapping the Increasing Use of LLMs in Scientific Papers* (arXiv:2404.01268) applies the same framework at scale. This is precisely the class of estimator B6.1 says it is stress-testing, and it is exactly the case the draft's own framing requires: its reference distributions are built from a **fixed, known generator set**, which is the assumption B6.1 exists to break.

**Consequence:** the draft asserts an estimand-level gap that a widely-known ICML 2024 paper already occupies on the estimator side. Left uncited, C5 and the Related Work opening both fail against work the paper *should* have cited — the exact failure mode the Afzal ICLR-corpus study identifies as the most common cause of an overstated-novelty verdict.

**Fix:** name Liang et al. as the primary **target-of-study** estimator class rather than a competitor, and state the delta: it reports a proportion but never varies the calibration generator set, never sweeps paraphrase, and reports no error bound off-calibration.

### N3 [moderate] — The quantification-learning literature is inherited silently

**Where:** Methodology §"Quantification-Correction Contrast" ("a quantification-corrected estimator (a prior-shift / adjusted-count correction)"); Problem Definition ("Aggregating many per-document readings into one corpus-level ratio is a known source of systematic bias under distribution shift").

**Evidence:** `refs.bib` contains zero quantification-learning references. "Adjusted count" and "prior-shift correction" are named techniques from a 20-year literature; "known source of systematic bias under distribution shift" is a claim sourced to nobody. González, Moreo & Sebastiani (arXiv:2310.04565) is the direct experimental study of binary quantification under dataset shift; Vaz, Izbicki & Stern (arXiv:1807.03929) is the ratio-estimator treatment of quantification under prior-probability shift.

**Fix:** cite both at first use; state the delta explicitly (that literature studies shift in the label prior, not shift in the *generator identity* that produces it — which is why an off-the-shelf correction is a candidate, not a solution).

### F1 [moderate] — The paraphrase axis lacks both its canonical priors and its scope boundary

**Where:** Methodology §"Paraphrase-Strength Sweep"; RQ2.

**Evidence:** `experiment_design.md` line 15 names a "DIPPER-style paraphraser" as the instrument. DIPPER is Krishna et al., *Paraphrasing evades detectors of AI-generated text, but retrieval is an effective defense* (arXiv:2303.13408, NeurIPS 2023) — uncited. Sadasivan et al., *Can AI-Generated Text be Reliably Detected?* (arXiv:2303.11156, TMLR) established the paraphrase-defeats-detectors result — uncited. Without them, RQ2's expected outcome ("error increases with paraphrase strength") reads as re-deriving a settled per-document finding, and a reviewer will score RQ2 as a confirmation rather than a measurement.

Second half of this finding: B6.1's paraphrase parameter is a **fixed number of non-adversarial rewrite rounds**, not a budget-bounded or directionally coordinated adversary. The draft never says so. A reviewer — and, at portfolio level, anyone reading B6.5 alongside it — will ask why the adversarial case is absent.

**Fix:** cite both; state the delta for RQ2 (at the aggregate-proportion level an averaging argument predicts per-document errors partially cancel, so the per-document result does **not** determine the aggregate slope — that is why the sweep is informative rather than confirmatory); and scope the axis explicitly as non-adversarial, deferring the adversarial case.

### F2 [moderate] — Units-of-analysis mismatch between the power declaration and the actual test

**Where:** Experiments §"Power and inconclusive cells"; `experiment_design.md` RQ1 ("95% CIs via nonparametric bootstrap resampling *generators* ... not documents").

**Evidence:** The pre-declared power floor is stated **per document** (≥1000 documents per cell, 0.03 AE effect floor). But RQ1's inferential unit is the **generator** — the CI is generator-resampled and the LOGO fold count equals the pool size. Document-level power says nothing about generator-level power, and a generator-resampled bootstrap over a pool of 12 has at most 12 distinct support points.

**Arithmetic (this pass):** for a two-sided t-test at α = 0.05 with 80% power on the per-generator LOGO gap, the required standardized effect is d ≈ 0.89 at n = 12 generators, so detecting the pre-declared 0.05 absolute-error gap requires the **between-generator SD of the per-generator gap to be ≲ 0.056**. At the walking skeleton's n = 3, the requirement is d ≈ 3.26, i.e. SD ≲ 0.015 — implausible, which correctly confirms the skeleton is a smoke test, not an inference. No between-generator variance assumption appears anywhere in the draft, so RQ1's headline test is currently unpowered-by-unknown-amount.

**Fix:** add the generator-level MDE to the power paragraph, state the between-generator-SD assumption it rests on, and extend the INCONCLUSIVE pre-declaration to the generator axis (if the realized between-generator SD exceeds the assumed bound, RQ1 is reported as INCONCLUSIVE rather than null).

### F3 [major] — RQ7 certifies invariance by failing to reject a null, so low power buys certification

**Where:** Methodology §"Generator-Invariance Probe"; RQ7 Setup ("testing that cross-cell AUROC variance is not significantly greater than zero ... Holm-corrected across the feature families tested").

**Evidence:** the criterion is `fail-to-reject(H0: cross-cell variance = 0) AND per-cell AUROC ≥ 0.70`. Failing to reject is not evidence of flatness. Two consequences follow mechanically, both in the wrong direction:

1. **Holm correction makes certification easier.** Holm raises the bar for *rejecting* H0; since the paper certifies invariance on non-rejection, applying Holm across feature families **increases** the number of features certified as invariant. The multiplicity correction, correctly applied everywhere else in the draft, is inverted here.
2. **A noisier, thinner grid certifies more features.** The fewer documents per cell, the wider the per-cell AUROC noise... but also the lower the power of the variance test, so an underpowered study is *more* likely to declare a generator-invariant feature exists.

This directly threatens RQ7, one of the paper's two headline deliverables, and it is not covered by the existing INCONCLUSIVE rule: that rule keys on per-cell document counts and AE confidence-interval width, not on the **flatness test's own power**.

**Fix:** recast the flatness leg as an **equivalence test** — pre-declare an invariance-relevant maximum cross-cell AUROC range (an equivalence margin), and certify only when the confidence bound on the cross-cell spread falls *inside* that margin (TOST-style), which requires evidence rather than the absence of it. Retain the 0.70 per-cell floor unchanged. Pre-declare that a flatness test lacking the power to exclude the margin is scored **INCONCLUSIVE**, never PASS.

### F4 [minor] — RQ3's 30% reduction threshold is unmotivated

The pre-registered "≥30% reduction in Δ_LOGO" has no stated derivation (unlike the 0.05 AE floor, which is tied to a decision-relevance argument). Not a revision trigger on its own; noted for the supervisor. **No revision manufactured for this finding.**
