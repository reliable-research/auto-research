# Undocumented by Design? Measuring Silent Behavioral Change in LLMs against Vendor Changelogs

## Abstract

Deployed large language models change: vendors ship new versions, update hosted snapshots, and silently modify the models behind floating aliases. A decade of recent work can now detect *that* an API's behavior changed; no published instrument measures *what fraction of detected change the vendor's own changelog accounts for*. We define and estimate the **behavioral-change disclosure gap** ρ: among cross-version behavioral differences detected by a frozen, pre-registered black-box probe battery, the fraction with no vendor-authored changelog claim whose stated scope covers them. Because a single threshold on "documented" is gameable in both directions, disclosure is scored on a three-level specificity scale — absent (S0), blanket (S1), specific (S2) — and ρ is always reported as a **strict/lenient interval** (strict: only specific claims cover; lenient: sufficiently scoped blanket claims also cover), with the gap between the two quantifying how much disclosure is boilerplate. Detection uses held-out-confirmed, sample-only tests with a calibrated same-version null and deterministic complete-link individuation into countable change units; alignment maps changelog claims and detected changes into a frozen six-axis scope lattice via a calibrated automated encoder audited against blinded human dual-coding; inference uses crossed-block bootstrap with full-pipeline reconstruction. Estimates are produced **separately and never pooled** across three version-pair regimes with different evidence value: retrospective open-weight releases (headline, fully reproducible), concurrently served dated API snapshots (realistic, vendor-selected), and prospective monitoring of mutable aliases (unbiased, calendar-dependent case study). The instrument, a released changelog claim corpus, seeded-replay and simulation validations, and per-regime ρ estimates turn "changelogs are incomplete" from an anecdote into a calibrated, partially identified, reproducible measurement.

## 1. Introduction

Large language models are now consumed as externally managed dependencies. The party that controls the change — the vendor — is also the sole author of the public record of the change: the changelog, release note, or model card. Downstream integrators deciding whether prior evaluations remain valid, safety auditors asking whether refusal-behavior changes are disclosed at the same rate as capability improvements, and regulators operationalizing post-market monitoring obligations under the EU AI Act's GPAI provisions all currently have to treat that record as complete. No published number says how complete it is.

The ingredients for producing that number exist. Chen et al. [1] showed that the "same" GPT service drifts substantially between dated snapshots. A 2024–2026 line of black-box auditing work detects change ever more cheaply and rigorously: linguistic-feature two-sample tests [2], anytime-valid sequential shift tests [3], rank-based equality tests against a reference model [4], log-probability tracking of production endpoints [5], and token-efficient change detection under tight query budgets [6]; within-model reliable-change methods separate genuine shifts from stochastic evaluation noise [7]; and substitution audits ask whether you get the model you pay for [8]. All of this answers *whether and where* behavior changed. None of it aligns detected change with the vendor's disclosure text. Conversely, the transparency literature — model cards [9], the Foundation Model Transparency Index and its follow-ups [10, 11, 12], transparency reports [13], documentation-structure analyses [14], and audit-trail proposals [15] — scores what vendors *say*, without an arm that measures what models *do* across versions. The accountability question in between is open: **when a black-box probe battery detects a behavioral difference between two versions, does the vendor's changelog contain a claim whose stated scope covers that difference?**

This paper builds the instrument that answers it and reports the resulting estimand. Three design commitments distinguish a measurement from an anecdote:

**(i) The estimand owns its conditionality.** ρ is defined relative to a frozen probe suite P and a pre-registered analysis configuration θ (significance level with multiplicity control, effect-size floor, coverage rules). It estimates the silent fraction *of suite-visible changes*; it says nothing about changes the suite cannot see. The defensible claim is a lower bound on the *count* of undisclosed changes — "at least this much measured change went undisclosed" — never an estimate of the total. Because our hand-written probes are mined from changelog vocabulary itself, the suite is *better* at detecting the kinds of change vendors talk about, which biases ρ downward; the conservative direction for an accountability claim.

**(ii) "Documented" is a spectrum, not a bit.** Vendors write both "improved model quality and safety" and "fixed over-refusal on medical dosage questions." A lenient matcher lets one blanket sentence document everything (ρ→0 by construction); a strict one documents nothing (ρ→1). We therefore code every claim's specificity (S0 absent / S1 blanket / S2 specific) and report ρ_strict (only S2 covers) and ρ_lenient (S1∨S2 covers) jointly; ρ_strict − ρ_lenient is itself a finding — the boilerplate share of disclosure.

**(iii) Regimes are evidence classes, never pooled.** Retrospective open-weight version pairs are perfectly re-queryable and anchor the headline; concurrently served dated API snapshots are realistic but vendor-selected (the vendor chose to date them, plausibly biasing toward better-documented transitions — reported as a caveat, and the cross-regime contrast is a named secondary finding); prospective monitoring of mutable aliases is unbiased but cannot be scheduled, so it is pre-declared as a case study whose absence does not invalidate the study. Every estimate is produced per regime with dependence-aware uncertainty.

**Contributions.** (1) The ρ estimand and its identification statement: a partially identified, strict/lenient-bracketed, per-regime disclosure-gap rate for LLM version transitions, with a σ companion (silent diffs per probe) that remains defined when few diffs are detected. (2) A two-arm instrument: a behavioral-change ledger that converts held-out-confirmed black-box test results into countable change units without logits or a reference model, and a changelog claim corpus + frozen scope lattice that reduces claim–change alignment to auditable containment decisions, executed by a calibrated automated encoder audited against blinded human dual-coding. (3) Regime-specific crossed-block inference with full-pipeline reconstruction, validated on seeded replays and a pre-registered dependence simulation grid, with honest failure labels (`non-estimable`, `cluster-dominated`). (4) Artifacts: the frozen ~1,400-probe battery, the dated multi-vendor changelog claim corpus (which exists nowhere as a dataset today), raw response archives, and per-regime ρ estimates with sensitivity grids.

## 2. Related Work

**Behavioral drift and black-box API auditing.** Chen et al. [1] established cross-snapshot drift for GPT-3.5/4; Ma et al. [16] reframe evolving-API regressions as a testing problem. Detection machinery has since matured: two-sample tests on linguistic features [2], sequential anytime-valid shift audits with tolerance [3], rank-based uniformity testing against a trusted local reference [4], continuous log-probability tracking of deployed endpoints [5], and token-efficient change detection [6]; Cacioli et al. [7] separate reliable within-model change from evaluation noise. Substitution auditing [8] asks the binary identity question — is the served model the advertised one — and concludes software-only verification is fragile, motivating TEEs. **Boundary:** this entire line stops at detecting, localizing, or verifying change. None of these works ingests vendor disclosure text, classifies disclosure specificity, or estimates what fraction of detected change is covered — the quantity ρ that this paper defines. We *reuse* the line: [5] as boundary detection for the prospective arm, [3]'s anytime-valid machinery for continuous monitoring, and detection-only pipelines as our baselines. (Pre-submission blocking check, pre-registered: full-text audit of [5] and [6] for any informal detected-vs-announced comparison; if one exists, our delta statement narrows to the calibrated alignment arm, the specificity taxonomy, and the ρ estimand, and the text will say so.)

**Adversarial scope.** Zhang et al. [17] show fingerprint-based audits can be spoofed by an aware vendor. A vendor that actively conceals change defeats any black-box audit; our estimand targets the *non-adversarial but under-documenting* vendor, which the incident record suggests is the common case. Broad behavioral batteries are also harder to spoof than narrow statistical fingerprints, and our headline regimes (open-weight pairs, publicly dated snapshots) are ones where adversarial concealment is implausible. A measured non-adversarial ρ is the prerequisite for arguing the adversarial case matters.

**Transparency and documentation.** Model cards [9] and the Foundation Model Transparency Index [10, 11, 12] assess disclosure practice; transparency reports [13], the AI Transparency Atlas [14], and audit-trail proposals [15] structure it. These score documents against checklists, not against measured behavior. Our work adds the missing behavioral arm and, conversely, inherits their finding that disclosure practice is heterogeneous — which motivates the specificity scale.

**Governing model updates.** Chishti et al. [18] propose deployer-side compatibility rules and pre-deployment tests for the LLM supply chain; Yang et al. [19] catalogue latent requirements that make silent change costly; safety patching [20] shows vendors themselves iterate between releases. Reproducibility work cautions that infrastructure alone shifts outputs: numerical nondeterminism [21] and single-sample evaluation noise [22] are exactly the null our detector must be calibrated against. Willing-but-unable analyses [23] show refusal and capability can move independently, motivating our separation of refusal-family from capability-family endpoints when attributing change categories.

**Documentation fidelity in software engineering.** Release-note studies and generators (e.g., ARENA [24]) established, for traditional software, that shipped changes routinely fail to surface in release notes — the closest conceptual precedent for documentation fidelity as a measurable quantity. We port the question to a setting where the "diff" is only observable behaviorally.

**Alignment machinery.** Claim–change matching is instantiated as structured containment plus calibrated entailment, positioned against NLI/fact-verification pipelines [25]. The novelty claimed is the estimand, the scope-lattice representation, and the calibration protocol — **not** a new entailment model; the matcher is deliberately off-the-shelf.

## 3. Problem Definition

Let R = {ow, api, alias} index the three regimes. For regime r, let V_r be its version transitions, P its frozen probe battery (shared across regimes; frozen and hashed before any post-transition measurement), and θ = (α, multiplicity control, effect floor, coverage rules) the pre-registered analysis configuration. For transition v = (v⁻, v⁺) and probe p, the observation is the pair of N-sample response sets X_{r,v,p} = (Y_{r,v⁻,p}, Y_{r,v⁺,p}). A reproducible detection procedure Δ (Section 4.1) maps these to a finite set of detected, individuated behavioral changes D_r. The estimand is **conditional on (P, θ, Δ)** and concerns detected changes, not latent vendor-internal changes.

Let C_r be the vendor-authored public claims applicable to r, each with specificity s(c) ∈ {blanket, specific} and a coded scope. M(c, d) ∈ {0,1} indicates whether claim c's stated scope covers detected change d (Section 4.2). Each d gets a disclosure class with precedence specific > blanket > absent:

L_r(d) = specific if ∃c: M(c,d)=1 ∧ s(c)=specific; blanket if no such specific claim but ∃c: M(c,d)=1 ∧ s(c)=blanket; absent otherwise.

With π_{r,k} the fraction of D_r in class k:

- **ρ_r^strict = π_{r,absent} + π_{r,blanket}** — fraction not covered by any *specific* claim;
- **ρ_r^lenient = π_{r,absent}** — fraction covered by no claim at all.

Reported output per regime: O_r = (π_{r,absent}, π_{r,blanket}, π_{r,specific}, ρ_r^strict, ρ_r^lenient, dependence-aware intervals), plus the unconditional companion **σ_r = (# silent changes)/|P|**, which is defined for every transition. Transitions with |D_r| < 10 are reported descriptively (full diff list + labels) and excluded from rate estimation. The non-pooling constraint is hard: O_r is estimated separately for every r; no cross-regime average is ever formed.

**Identification statement (verbatim in the paper):** ρ estimates the silent fraction of *suite-visible* changes. It is neither an upper nor a lower bound on the true silent fraction of all changes; the claim it supports is a lower bound on the *count* of undisclosed changes. Because the probe battery is partly mined from changelog vocabulary (Section 5.1), detection power is tilted toward disclosable-kind changes, biasing ρ downward — conservative for an accountability finding.

**Challenges.** (i) D_r must be inferred from stochastic black-box outputs whose "same-version" variability includes load-dependent routing, batching, and inference-stack nondeterminism [21, 22] — the null must be estimated from same-version resamples, not assumed. (ii) M(c,d) must bridge wildly different granularities ("safety improvements" vs a measured +9pt over-refusal shift on dosage questions) reproducibly — if humans cannot agree on S0/S1/S2 (κ < 0.6), the metric has no floor. (iii) Probes sharing templates, transitions sharing lineages, and repeated samples are dependent; item-level intervals would be anti-conservative. (iv) The ideal object — the same alias before/after a silent update — is ephemeral; every acquisition strategy trades realism against availability, which is exactly why regimes are separated.

## 4. Method

*Figure 1: The two-arm disclosure-gap instrument: probe-battery arm (detection → ledger), changelog-corpus arm (claims → scope lattice), calibrated alignment into absent/blanket/specific coverage, and per-regime strict/lenient ρ estimation with pilot gates.*

### 4.1 Arm 1 — Behavioral change ledger

**Probes and acquisition.** ~1,400 probes across six categories — factual QA, refusal/safety, instruction-following/formatting, reasoning/math, tool-calling, latency/cost — drawn as stratified subsets from MMLU, GSM8K, TruthfulQA (factual/reasoning), XSTest + HarmBench (refusal and over-refusal), IFEval (instruction/formatting), BFCL (tool-calling), plus ~200 hand-written probes targeting behaviors changelogs actually describe (persona/tone, formatting defaults, refusal templates, JSON-mode strictness), mined from the changelog corpus so that ρ_strict < 1 is attainable. Probes are organized into paraphrase-grouped families; each probe = (prompt, locked decoding config, response-feature extractors). N = 25–50 samples per probe per version at the version's default decoding; features: exact answer, refusal flag (classifier), format-compliance flags, response length, and first-token top-k logprobs where returned. The battery is frozen and hashed before any post-transition measurement.

**Null calibration (gate A0).** Same-version null pairs — resamples of one endpoint spread across days and load conditions — estimate the detector's false-positive rate. Because a silent update *during* the null window would poison it, null windows are themselves monitored with a logprob/fingerprint tracker [5]; any discontinuity voids that window. Detector FPR on ≥ 200 null probe-pairs must be ≤ 1.5×α, and the measured FPR is reported next to every headline number.

**Detection and confirmation.** Per (transition, probe family, endpoint): blocked permutation tests (blocking by paraphrase group and repeat) with Benjamini–Yekutieli control at q = 0.05 for retrospective and snapshot pairs; anytime-valid e-process change-point tests with e-BH at q = 0.05 for fixed-cadence alias monitoring [3]. A detection additionally requires an effect floor |Cliff's δ| ≥ 0.33 (pre-registered in θ; sensitivity at 0.2 and 0.5). Candidates rejected on discovery samples are re-tested on held-out fresh samples; only held-out-confirmed cells enter individuation.

**Individuation (confirm-then-cluster).** Each confirmed cell is a node keyed by (regime, lineage, interval, probe family, endpoint, effect direction) carrying standardized signed effects over paraphrase groups. A frozen endpoint-equivalence map declares which endpoints are redundant indicators of one construct. Nodes are hard-compatible only if regime, lineage, interval, and direction match and endpoints share an equivalence class; similarity = cosine over jointly applicable paraphrase groups (≥ 2 overlap required); deterministic complete-link agglomeration at threshold 0.80 (ties broken lexicographically; no post-hoc manual splits/merges; no transitive bridge merges). Resulting clusters are the countable changes in the ρ denominator; sensitivity denominators at thresholds 0.70/0.90 and unclustered singletons.

### 4.2 Arm 2 — Changelog claim corpus and scope-lattice alignment

**Corpus.** We scrape and archive (with Wayback snapshots for historical states) release notes, changelogs, model cards, and release blog posts of the target vendors; segment them into dated claim units; and annotate each claim's category and specificity (S1 blanket / S2 specific; S0 is the absence of an eligible claim). The corpus is released as an artifact — no such dataset currently exists.

**Scope lattice.** Claims and ledger records are encoded on a frozen six-axis lattice: vendor/model, effective interval, behavior family/endpoint, effect direction (primary axes); task/domain, affected population (secondary axes). Categorical nodes are sets of terminal values; containment is a subset test; interval containment requires the claim's stated window to contain the ledger interval; parent–child relations, synonyms, and terminals are frozen before matching.

**Coverage rules (deterministic given encodings).** *Specificity:* absent if no model-and-time-eligible claim exists; blanket if eligible claims exist but none states a behavioral predicate; specific if one does. *Strict coverage:* a single specific eligible claim states the same non-top node (frozen synonyms allowed) on every primary axis, contains the interval, and matches every secondary axis on which the record is non-top. *Lenient coverage:* a single eligible claim contains the record under the descendant relation (top values allowed only on secondary axes); a blanket claim counts only if its evidence span explicitly scopes it to all outputs/behaviors of the matching model and interval. A contradiction on any stated axis rejects coverage; multiple claims combine by per-claim OR — fields from different claims are never unioned into a synthetic match.

**Calibrated encoding (automation-first, human-audited).** Encoding every claim and record by hand does not scale and is not the contribution. An LLM/NLI encoder maps claim texts and structured "diff cards" (category, direction, effect size, example response pairs) into lattice fields with quoted evidence spans. Calibration: a stratified ~250-pair claim–change dev set is blind dual-coded by two human encoders with adjudication (pilot gate: κ ≥ 0.6 on S0/S1/S2; target κ ≥ 0.75 on lattice fields); the automated encoder is accepted only if its field-level agreement with adjudicated human codes reaches the human–human level and end-to-end coverage decisions reach macro-F1 ≥ 0.8 on a held-out split; disagreement-flagged and low-confidence cases (target ≤ 15%) are routed to human adjudication. A keyword/BM25 matcher and an unconstrained free-text NLI judge are retained as baselines to quantify how much the structure matters. All encodings, evidence spans, and adjudications ship with the artifact.

### 4.3 Regime-specific gap inference

For each regime r and rule q separately: ρ̂(r,q) = (# clusters uncovered under q)/(# clusters), with numerator decomposition by specificity class and per-category rates ρ_c under pre-registered category weights (not raw probe counts). Uncertainty must respect dependence: probes cluster in families, changes cluster in transitions, repeats travel together. The bootstrap frame is frozen before testing: for ow/api, resample lineages, then transition blocks within lineages, crossed with independently resampled probe-family blocks, applying Cartesian multiplicities to raw acquisition cells; for alias, resample stationary time blocks crossed with probe-family blocks. **Every replicate re-runs the full pipeline** — testing, confirmation, clustering, alignment (automated encoder only), ρ — from the resampled raw cells, so change-selection and individuation uncertainty propagate; original clusters are never resampling units.

Primary intervals are 95% percentile intervals from B = 1,000 outer replicates. The nested percentile-t (studentized) variant — outer × inner with per-replicate standard errors — is computationally prohibitive against real acquisitions and is therefore evaluated in the simulation study (Section 6, RQ5) and applied to the real cohort only if percentile coverage proves inadequate there; this weakening is disclosed rather than hidden. A replicate is valid only if it detects ≥ 1 change; if < 95% of replicates are valid, the interval is reported **non-estimable** — never silently replaced by an item-level interval. Leave-one-transition-out diagnostics flag **cluster-dominated** estimates (any single deletion moving ρ̂ by > 0.10).

## 5. Measurement Cohort, Budget, and Gates

### 5.1 Three regimes, sized to reality

- **R-OW (retrospective, open-weight; headline).** 6–8 transitions with both endpoints downloadable and a dated vendor disclosure text (release blog + model card), candidates: Llama-3.1→3.3, Qwen-2.5→Qwen-3, DeepSeek-V3→V3.1, Mistral versioned releases, Gemma-2→3 — each pair's disclosure text verified to exist and be dated **before the cohort freeze**. Endpoints served locally (vLLM): sampling fully controlled, infinitely re-queryable. Note the disclosure genre here (model cards, blogs) is richer than API changelogs — one reason regimes are never pooled.
- **R-API (retrospective, dated snapshots).** 4–6 pairs of concurrently served dated snapshots from ≥ 2 vendors, aligned to release notes for the inter-snapshot window; snapshot availability and deprecation dates verified at freeze (snapshots get retired). Selection caveat reported: the vendor chose to date these transitions.
- **P (prospective, alias monitoring; case study).** Daily fingerprint/logprob tracking [5] plus a weekly 150-probe mini-suite against floating aliases of 2–3 vendors, with anytime-valid sequential tests so continuous monitoring does not inflate error [3]. A diff is attributed to a window only if bracketed by fingerprint-stable stretches; otherwise "boundary-uncertain" and excluded from ρ. Any boundary caught before freeze becomes a case study; its absence is pre-declared as non-blocking.

### 5.2 Budget and compute

Open-weight: ~14 endpoints × 1,400 probes × 50 samples ≈ 1.0M short generations (~0.5B tokens); ≈ 600–900 A100-hours dominated by 2–3 large pairs (fallback: drop 70B-class pairs, run ≤ 14B — the estimand is disclosure fidelity, not capability). API arm capped at **$4K**: N = 25 samples/probe, pairs capped at 4 if needed, rate-limited polite querying. Annotation: ~60–80 hours across two encoders + one adjudicator, front-loading changelog specificity labeling (not diff-dependent) before the full runs. Raw responses, fingerprints, and the changelog corpus are frozen and released (~tens of GB).

### 5.3 Pilot gates (go/no-go)

Pilot = 1 open-weight transition (8B-scale) + 1 API snapshot pair, 300-probe mini-suite, N = 25. **GO requires:** (i) A0 detector FPR ≤ 1.5×α on ≥ 200 same-version null pairs; (ii) ≥ 15 held-out-confirmed diffs across the two transitions, **or** a demonstrably calibrated detector (A0 pass) with high power on synthetic injected diffs — a quiet-transition world measured by a calibrated instrument is a publishable negative result ("disclosure completeness is high for these pairs"), not a failure; (iii) two annotators reach κ ≥ 0.6 on S0/S1/S2 over ≥ 50 diff–claim pairs. **NO-GO fallback:** descope to a measurement-instrument paper (detector + corpus + calibration + open-weight-only ρ) and retarget the next venue window, letting the prospective arm mature into a genuine longitudinal arm.

## 6. Experiments

Validation experiments (RQ1–RQ5) exercise the instrument on constructed data where ground truth exists; RQ6 and the headline analysis run on the real cohort. All comparisons are paired on identical inputs; all randomness pre-seeded.

**RQ1 — Enumeration validity (seeded replay).** Replay 360 blinded seeded behavioral mutations + 360 matched nulls (120 each per regime; synthetic, so scale is cheap), stratified by change family and effect size, concealed manifest scored one-sided. Success: regime-balanced seed recall ≥ 0.80 at empirical FDP ≤ 0.10, and recall ≥ 0.15 above an acquisition-matched aggregate-shift detector with leave-one-probe-family-out localization (paired permutation test, Holm-corrected).

**RQ2 — Individuation reproducibility.** 90 acquisition targets (30/regime, ≥ 20 endpoints), duplicate independent acquisitions with fresh stochastic samples. Success: duplicate-clustering ARI ≥ 0.75 and ≥ 0.15 above one-pass per-endpoint testing (cluster-level bootstrap).

**RQ3 — Alignment validity (held-out).** 300 held-out claim–change pairs (100/regime), stratified over specificity and coverage polarity, gold = 3-expert blinded consensus. Success: three-class specificity macro-F1 ≥ 0.80; balanced accuracy ≥ 0.85 under each rule; each ≥ 0.10 above unconstrained free-text NLI and above keyword/BM25 (paired stratified bootstrap). This N is chosen to fit the annotation budget; per-class standard errors (~0.05) are reported with the results.

**RQ4 — Encoding reliability.** 150 pairs, 4 trained encoders, randomized structured-vs-free-form crossover with washout. Success: Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.75 on lattice fields and specificity; structured − free-form α ≥ 0.10; adjudication rate ≤ 15%.

**RQ5 — Interval calibration (simulation).** Pre-registered grid: 3 regimes × 2 rules × ICC ∈ {0, .1, .25, .5} × 3 cluster-size templates (parameterized from the real cohort) × true ρ ∈ {.2, .5, .8} × 3 disclosure mixtures; 2,000 sims/cell. Success: crossed-block coverage ≥ 0.90 in every cell (percentile and percentile-t variants both evaluated; the better-calibrated one is pre-committed for the real cohort), while the item-level bootstrap drops below 0.90 in ≥ 1 cell with ICC ≥ 0.1.

**RQ6 — Robustness of real-cohort estimates.** On the real cohort (6–8 ow transitions, 4–6 api pairs, alias case study): leave-one-transition-out ρ deviations per regime × rule; report max deviation and label any estimate moved > 0.10 by a single deletion **cluster-dominated** rather than stable. Sensitivity grid over θ: α ∈ {0.01, 0.05}, |δ| ∈ {0.2, 0.33, 0.5}, clustering threshold ∈ {0.70, 0.80, 0.90, singleton}, encoder-vs-human-adjudicated labels.

**Headline analysis.** Per-regime π (absent/blanket/specific), ρ_strict/ρ_lenient with crossed-block intervals, σ companion, per-category rates ρ_c (refusal/safety vs capability categories contrasted), the boilerplate share ρ_strict − ρ_lenient, and the named secondary finding: are API changelogs sparser than open-weight release notes? Detector FPR from A0 printed beside every headline number.

## 7. Limitations, Ethics, and Threats

**Partial identification.** ρ is conditional on (P, θ, Δ); the paper's claims are phrased so the instrument cannot be misquoted as "X% of all changes are secret." **Adversarial vendors** are out of scope by design [17]; the estimand targets under-documentation, not concealment. **Genre confound:** open-weight "changelogs" are model cards and blogs; cross-regime comparison is reported as a contrast between disclosure regimes, never pooled. **Infra-vs-model change:** some detected diffs may reflect serving-stack changes rather than weight changes [21]; the ledger records this ambiguity, and the alias arm's fingerprint bracketing partially controls it. **ToS/legal:** rate-limited polite probing of public endpoints only; findings at vendor-aggregate and per-category granularity in the main text with per-vendor detail in an appendix; safe-harbor context for good-faith evaluation [26] cited; legal review before de-anonymized release. **Annotation scale:** human labels bound the alignment arm's validity; all κ, F1, and adjudication statistics ship with the artifact, and the automated encoder is never trusted beyond its measured agreement.

## 8. Conclusion

Everyone can now detect that a deployed model changed; this work measures whether anyone was told. By freezing the probe suite, bracketing "documented" between strict and lenient readings, refusing to pool acquisition regimes, and calibrating both the detector (same-version nulls) and the aligner (blinded human dual-coding), the disclosure gap ρ becomes a number a regulator, integrator, or auditor can cite — with uncertainty, conditionality, and failure labels attached. The changelog claim corpus and probe battery are released so vendors' future disclosure fidelity can be tracked longitudinally; the prospective alias arm, pre-declared here as a case study, becomes the natural long-term instrument.
