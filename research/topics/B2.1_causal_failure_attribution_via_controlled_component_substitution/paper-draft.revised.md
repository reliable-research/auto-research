# CapsuleBench: Mechanically Certified Fault Injection for Causal Failure Attribution in LLM Agent Pipelines

> Revised draft (2026-08-15), reconciled with `discussion.md`. Changes vs the autodiscussion draft: (1) the injectable-fault taxonomy is frozen at three classes {plan, tool, observation} with the executor as the held-fixed substrate, resolving the four-class contradiction; (2) the paper's identity is restored to *benchmark instrument + method-zoo evaluation* (Defects4J lineage), with the crossover diagnoser kept as the interventional reference method; (3) certification uses the stochasticity-tolerant control lattice (identity / no-op / fault / bypass, majority-over-k) instead of absolute determinism; (4) B2.3 cross-backbone axis reports label survival first-class; (5) B2.2 replay-budget axis keeps nested budgets plus the k-sensitivity check; (6) pilot gates, powered-cell rule, and descope ladder are pre-registered.

## Abstract

Failure attribution in plan–executor–tool LLM agent pipelines determines which component engineers repair, replace, or monitor, yet the field's ground truth is almost entirely human- or LLM-judge-annotated: benchmark gains measure agreement with annotators, not causal correctness. Intervention-based approaches, including recent injected-fault datasets, assume without measurement that failure follows the injected or substituted component. We present CapsuleBench, a benchmark whose labels are *mechanically certified by construction*. Typed fault capsules — plan corruptions, tool breakages, observation pollutions — are injected into reproducibly succeeding base runs and earn a label only by passing a four-arm control lattice: the outcome flips under k replays of the fault (majority-failure), a class-matched no-op substitution does not flip it, a zero-edit identity replay is stable, and bypassing the capsule restores success while a lineage certificate ties the observed failure to the injected source. The first result is a validity measurement no prior work reports: per-class flip-rate above matched no-op controls — the field's shared substitution premise, tested. On the certified corpus we mechanically score an attribution-method zoo (LLM-judge strategies, a frozen surface heuristic, do-then-verify, mutation-kill, and an outcome-blind bidirectional crossover diagnoser as the interventional reference) at component level (primary) and step level (secondary, directly comparable to the literature's ~14% step numbers). Two absorbed axes complete the design: certified labels are re-certified on swapped executor backbones, reporting label survival and diagnosis stability, and interventional methods are metered under nested replay budgets, reporting accuracy per replay. The corpus, harness, and re-certification scripts are released as a versioned instrument.

## Introduction

Large language model (LLM) agents decompose tasks into plans, executor steps, tool calls, and observations [ReAct: Yao et al., arXiv:2210.03629; Plan-and-Act: Erdogan et al., arXiv:2503.09572; Toolformer: Schick et al., arXiv:2302.04761]. When such a pipeline fails, the first engineering question is *where*: was the plan wrong, did a tool break, or was an observation corrupted before it reached the executor? Failure attribution is now the largest and fastest-growing subtopic of agent reliability, yet the crowd shares one load-bearing weakness: its ground truth is annotation. Who&When's responsible-agent and decisive-step labels are human-annotated, and its best methods reach ≈53.5% agent-level and ≈14.2% step-level *agreement with annotators* [Zhang et al., arXiv:2505.00212]; TRAIL's error taxonomy labels are expert-annotated [Deshpande et al., arXiv:2505.08638]; judge-based scoring of judge-like methods is circular — a plausible narrative can agree with a plausible annotation while both miss the causal locus. Nobody in this literature can currently answer "is the blame *correct*?", only "is the blame *agreed with*?".

Injection-based work moves closer but stops short of certification. Who&When Pro constructs 12,326 failed trajectories by replaying successful prefixes and injecting erroneous actions, calling the injected step a "golden label" [Liu et al., arXiv:2607.09996] — but the label is assigned *by convention*: nothing verifies that the observed failure tracks the injected change rather than intrinsic stochasticity, an edit-pathway artifact, or a dormant natural weakness in the base run. AgenTracer uses programmed injection to mint reinforcement-learning supervision for a trained tracer [Zhang et al., arXiv:2509.03312]; AgentDebug tests earliest-critical-error hypotheses by single corrective re-rollouts [Zhu et al., arXiv:2509.25370]; DoVer verifies one blame hypothesis per failure by rewind–edit–replay [Ma et al., arXiv:2512.06749]; Causal Agent Replay estimates step-level do()-effects, validated on synthetic causal models [Shah, 2026]. Every one of these silently assumes the same premise: *that failure follows the substituted component*. None measures it.

Software engineering solved the analogous problem a decade ago by fixing the ground truth instead of the technique: Defects4J [Just et al., ISSTA 2014] outlived every fault-localization method evaluated on it because each of its real bugs is certified by a deterministic triggering test. Agent pipelines have no such test for natural failures — which is precisely why ground truth must be manufactured *forward*, by injection, and why the injection itself must be certified. This paper builds that instrument. A fault capsule earns a causal label only by passing a four-arm control lattice executed under a pinned, replayable harness: (i) *flip* — the outcome flips from majority-success to majority-failure under k replays of the fault; (ii) *no-op null* — a class-matched no-op substitution traversing the same edit pathway does not flip the outcome; (iii) *identity stability* — the zero-edit replay is stable within a preregistered stochasticity bound; (iv) *bypass and lineage* — disabling the capsule restores success, and a lineage certificate links the injected tag to the violated oracle condition, separating the injected source from downstream symptoms. The label is explicitly scoped: it certifies the injected fault as a *sufficient difference-maker relative to a certified-reproducible base run* — a Lewis-style counterfactual contrast, not a metaphysical unique cause; residual latent-cause masking is registered as a threat, not defined away.

Three design decisions follow from the design document and are load-bearing. First, the taxonomy is frozen at **three injectable classes** — plan, tool, observation — because these are the pipeline's own component slots; the **executor is the held-fixed substrate**, and executor variation appears where it is well-defined: as the cross-backbone axis, which swaps the executor backbone and measures whether certified labels *survive* (a fault certified on backbone A may be routed around by backbone B — non-transfer is an expected, measured outcome, not a failure of the benchmark). Second, the paper is an *instrument*, not a method: its headline deliverables are the certified corpus and the certification protocol; the evaluation scores an attribution-method zoo — LLM-judge strategies, a frozen surface heuristic floor, do-then-verify, mutation-kill step credit, and an outcome-blind bidirectional crossover diagnoser we contribute as the interventional reference — with every method also scored on Who&When natural failures and the gap reported. Third, interventional attribution consumes replays, so the benchmark meters them: accuracy is reported per unit of replay budget under nested budgets, and the labeling protocol reports its own k-sensitivity.

## Contributions

- **A certification protocol that turns fault injection into ground truth** (CO2): the four-arm control lattice (flip / class-matched no-op / identity stability / bypass + lineage certificate) under a pinned replayable harness, with outcome-blind admissibility and abstention. The protocol's validity is itself measured (RQ1): per-class flip-rate above matched no-op controls — the first test of the substitution premise the entire intervention-based literature assumes.
- **A certified injected-fault corpus** (CO1): version-tiered (`corpus-v1-<scaffold>-<backbone>`) certified trials across plan / tool / observation faults with recorded injected step s*, released with harness and re-certification scripts; two-tier labels (component primary, step secondary) make results directly comparable to the annotation-based literature.
- **A mechanical method-zoo evaluation** (CO3): LLM-judge (three context strategies), frozen surface heuristic B0-b, ablation/leave-one-out, DoVer-style do-then-verify, mutation-kill step credit, random/majority floors, and the bidirectional crossover diagnoser — scored against certified labels, with abstention scored, calibration and directional mis-blame reported, budget-matched across access regimes.
- **Two absorbed robustness axes**: cross-backbone label survival and diagnosis stability (B2.3); replay-budget efficiency curves and certification k-sensitivity (B2.2). No existing artifact reports either.

## Problem Definition

Let \(\mathcal{K}=\{\mathrm{plan},\mathrm{tool},\mathrm{obs}\}\) be the injectable component classes of a plan–executor–tool pipeline; the executor is held fixed within a corpus tier and indexed separately by backbone \(b\in\mathcal{B}\). A failing trajectory \(\tau\) records the plan, executor messages, tool calls, returns/observations, memory writes, and the deterministic oracle outcome. Two access regimes define two tasks and are never conflated: the *post-hoc regime* (trajectory text only — what judge methods consume) and the *interventional regime* (trajectory plus a checkpointed harness with replay access, metered by budget \(C\)). Methods are compared only within a regime or under explicit budget matching.

An attribution method \(m\) outputs a blamed component \(\hat c\in\mathcal{K}\) (primary), optionally a blamed step \(\hat s\) (secondary) and a confidence \(\hat p\in[0,1]\); "cannot localize" is a legal, scored output.

**Certified trials.** The benchmark's atomic unit is the certified substitution trial
\[(\text{base task},\ \text{scaffold},\ b,\ \text{checkpoint},\ \text{template id},\ c,\ s^{*},\ k\ \text{replay outcomes},\ \text{control outcomes},\ \ell=c),\]
where fault operator \(\delta_c\) was mechanically injected at recorded step \(s^{*}\) into a reproducibly succeeding base run. The label exists iff the certification lattice holds:

1. **Flip:** \(\mathrm{FlipRate}(\delta_c)\) over \(k\) replays yields majority-failure from a majority-success base;
2. **No-op null:** the class-matched no-op \(\delta_{\varnothing}^{c}\) (same edit pathway, no fault) satisfies \(\mathrm{FlipRate}(\delta_{\varnothing}^{c})\le 0.10\);
3. **Identity stability:** the zero-edit replay \(\delta_{\mathrm{id}}\) satisfies \(\mathrm{FlipRate}(\delta_{\mathrm{id}})\le 0.05\);
4. **Bypass + lineage:** disabling the capsule restores majority-success, and the lineage certificate links the injected tag to the violated oracle condition.

No human judgment is in the label path; humans appear only in a post-hoc audit of the instrument (κ ≥ 0.7 on a 200-trajectory sample). Non-flipping injections are discarded, never mislabeled (inject ≠ cause). Natural failures without provenance form a separate contrast set (Who&When) and are excluded from causal-accuracy estimates.

**Measures.** \(\mathrm{Acc}(m)=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}[m(\tau)=\ell(\tau)]]\) over certified trials, cluster-robust at base-task level (trials sharing a base task are one cluster). Secondary: step accuracy (strict and tolerance-w), 3×3 directional confusion \(P(\hat c=j\mid \ell=i)\) with a named upstream-blame bias statistic, calibration (ECE, selective-risk curves), efficiency (accuracy vs replay budget \(C\)), and stability (label survival across \(b\); diagnosis agreement). Two nested questions organize everything: **RQ-V** (validity): does failure track the substituted component above the no-op control at all — a measurement no prior work reports; and **RQ-A** (accuracy): given certified labels, how accurate are existing attribution methods, mechanically scored.

### Substrate and injection mechanism

Tasks must be (i) replayable — tool calls return identical results on re-execution; (ii) checkpointable — mid-trajectory state restorable; (iii) outcome-testable — deterministic pass/fail oracle; (iv) tool-use-rich (≥5 steps typical). This selects containerized file-system/CLI tasks, local-API tool suites, and GAIA-style questions [Mialon et al., arXiv:2311.12983] served from snapshotted tool backends (frozen search corpus, pinned API fixtures). Target: ~200 base tasks in five families entering the reproducibility gate; ≥60 surviving (pre-registered floor 30).

Faults are injected as **orchestrator-message-level edits** (DoVer-style), never weight or hidden-state surgery: plan faults edit the plan message (delete/reorder/replace a step, infeasible sub-goal, corrupted planner prompt); tool faults rebind the tool to a faulty implementation (error stub, empty/malformed return, wrong-signature swap); observation faults rewrite the return payload en route to the executor (contradiction, noise, truncation, schema-valid falsehood). The library deliberately includes *signal-free* templates — schema-valid falsehoods and semantically wrong plan steps that crash nothing — so the challenge tier is populated by design. One fault per trajectory, injected at recorded step \(s^{*}\), replayed forward from the checkpoint k = 4 times at temperature 0. Severity knobs are fixed before scoring, never tuned on results. Memory-write and environment-state faults are named as the corpus's v2 extension surface, out of scope here.

Each capsule additionally carries the mechanics inherited from the autodiscussion draft, now as *components of* the lattice rather than replacements for it: a decidable trigger predicate over the boundary state, a deterministic transformation, and a **lineage tag** whose tag-to-oracle certificate records how the injected fault propagates to the observable failure — this is what separates the causal component from downstream components that merely process corrupted state, and it is what the bypass arm toggles.

### Certification: the four-arm control lattice

Certification runs in a version-pinned runtime with seeded decoding and snapshotted tools, but does **not** assume bit-exact determinism: LLM serving does not guarantee it, so every gate is a majority/threshold over k replays. The four arms per candidate trial:

| Arm | Edit | Certifies |
|---|---|---|
| \(\delta_{\mathrm{id}}\) identity | none (zero-edit replay) | intrinsic stochasticity below bound (FlipRate ≤ 0.05) |
| \(\delta_{\varnothing}^{c}\) class-matched no-op | same pathway, no fault (paraphrase-equivalent plan step; identically-bound tool; re-serialized observation) | the edit pathway itself is inert (FlipRate ≤ 0.10; generators audited: κ ≥ 0.7, ≥95% rated equivalent) |
| \(\delta_{c}\) fault | the capsule transformation | sufficiency: majority-failure from majority-success base |
| bypass + lineage | capsule installed, trigger disabled | failure depends on the transformation, not instrumentation; tag-to-oracle certificate holds |

A no-op arm that flips outcomes makes every label noise — this is where the benchmark lives or dies, so the no-op generators are themselves audited artifacts. The reproducibility gate (base run majority-success under temperature-0 replay) is what makes "relative to this base" a strong baseline: a run that succeeds 4/5 times has no *active* natural fault.

### Crossover Evidence Pack: the interventional reference diagnoser

We retain the autodiscussion draft's substitution machinery as the strongest interventional method in the zoo (it is a contribution, but it is *scored by* the instrument, not the instrument itself). Each episode is a checkpointable typed state machine; candidate events are enumerated in topological order from a provenance-free log (capsule metadata hidden). For each aligned event, clean and faulty **component twins** — the boundary-transition implementation plus component-owned serialized prestate, excluding donor inputs/outputs — are constructed from paired clean and faulted snapshots. *Fault transfer* restores the clean recipient's pre-event checkpoint and swaps in the faulty twin; *clean repair* is the reverse. Admissibility is decided **before consulting the task oracle**: shared task/event keys, interface invariants, a capability sandbox bounding reads to declared inputs, hash-fixity of non-target state, and a same-run self-transplant that must reproduce the native next outcome under the identity-tier bound (majority-match, not hash-exactness). Inadmissible arms are recorded unavailable, never replaced by easier ones. A boundary exhibits **bidirectional crossover** when fault transfer flips clean-pass→failure and clean repair flips failure→pass on the preregistered matched seeds; the blinded diagnoser selects the earliest such boundary or abstains. Requiring both directions guards against interventions that merely disrupt execution or introduce a generally stronger replacement — the reason repair-only "validation" (the DoVer signal) is not causal evidence.

### Cross-backbone axis (absorbed B2.3)

Swapping the executor backbone changes the reachable trajectory distribution, so "the same benchmark across backbones" is not well-defined by fiat. The design answer is **per-backbone certified tiers**: every Stage-1 certified trial is re-certified on a pinned open-weights backbone (the tier the release-longevity plan requires anyway), and we report, per class, (i) the **label survival rate** — does \(\delta_c\) still flip on backbone B? — with non-transfer as an expected, interpreted outcome (stronger backbones route around weaker faults); (ii) the per-method accuracy delta on the surviving intersection; and (iii) one diagnosis-stability confusion table (same blamed component?). Minimal fixed design, two numbers per class plus one table; no open-ended exploration.

### Replay-budget axis (absorbed B2.2)

Interventional methods consume replays-from-checkpoint; judges do not. The budget unit is **replays from checkpoint** (matching the mutation-kill sibling's budget C), with token cost as a secondary column. Replay-consuming methods are swept at nested budgets \(C\in\{0,2,4,8,16\}\) (smaller budgets are prefixes of larger, so gains are attributable to added evidence, not different arm selection); the budget-matched judge control gives the post-hoc regime the same budget, passively consumed. The labeling protocol reports its own **k-sensitivity** (k ∈ {2,4,8} on a 50-trial subsample): is k = 4 enough for stable labels?

### Pilot gates, preregistration, and descope ladder

A **walking skeleton** precedes any scale-up (≈$21–105, <1 day): 20 GAIA-style tasks, four arms (reproducibility gate target ≥12 survivors; tool-break injection k=3; class-matched no-op k=3; identity k=3). Pass iff yield ≥ 12 ∧ no-op crash ≤ 10% ∧ FlipRate(tool-break) − FlipRate(no-op) ≥ 0.20 ∧ identity flips ≤ 1 ∧ ≥10 labeled trajectories. Failure routes by tier: no-op crash → harness bug; identity instability → raise k once, then the pre-named negative-result pivot ("the field's substitution premise does not hold" — itself a publishable finding). Cells with <80 certified trajectories or simulated cluster-robust power <0.8 are pre-declared INCONCLUSIVE. The descope ladder is fixed in advance: D1 Stage-2 scale drops to the 1,500-trial floor; D2 the budget sweep shrinks to k-sensitivity + one budget pair (4 vs 16); D3 the backbone tier shrinks to tool-class only; D4 the FAPO/CRM-style localizer leaves the zoo. The never-cut core: validity arm + controls, zoo on the certified tier, B0-b floor, human audit.

## Experiments

**Setup.** ~200 base tasks, 5 families, one scaffold (scoped claim; the versioned-tier schema makes further scaffolds config extensions). Primary backbone (pinned API model) for certification; second pinned open-weights backbone for the cross-backbone tier; optional third backbone if budget allows — three, not five: each added backbone multiplies the panel and the discussion document's minimal design requires one. Stage 1 targets n≈300 certified trials (~100/class); Stage 2 scales to n≈1,500–3,000. All statistics cluster-robust at base-task level; paired tests wherever the same trials are scored under two methods or budgets. Compute: ≈15,000–30,000 agent runs ⇒ $2k–6k API at Stage-2 scale; the backbone tier runs on local GPUs; the budget sweep reuses certified trials (~1,200 extra replays).

**Method zoo (with access regime and budget).** LLM-judge × {all-at-once, step-by-step, binary-search} (post-hoc); frozen deterministic surface heuristic **B0-b** — blame the component hosting the last step before the first logged error signal, regex/status-code rules, no LLM (post-hoc floor); ablation/leave-one-out (interventional); DoVer-style do-then-verify (interventional); mutation-kill step credit (interventional, budget-bounded); Crossover Evidence Pack bidirectional diagnoser (interventional reference); random and majority-class floors. Controls: budget-matched judge; contamination control (≥30 held-out/paraphrased base tasks scored separately); Who&When natural-failure contrast set scored for every method with the gap reported.

**RQ1 — Instrument validity (the headline).** Per class, is \(\mathrm{FlipRate}(\delta_c)-\mathrm{FlipRate}(\delta_{\varnothing}^{c})\) positive and large? Arms: ≥100 injection + ≥100 no-op per class + ≥100 identity replays (Stage 1), ~3× at Stage 2. Success: gap ≥ 0.20 per class with cluster-robust 95% CI excluding zero; identity tier within bound. A partial result (some classes valid, some not) triggers the pre-registered certified-tier partition rather than silent exclusion. *This is the measurement the intervention-based literature (Who&When Pro, AgenTracer, DoVer, AgentDebug, CAR) assumes and has never reported.*

**RQ2 — Certificate robustness to nuisance variation.** Every certified capsule receives 10 pre-registered semantics-preserving nuisance variants (benign paraphrase, independent-context reordering, formatting, equivalent tool-arg encodings) with the injected mechanism fixed; matched success-to-failure-only injections (Who&When-Pro-style, controls ablated) run the same variants. Success: ≥90% of certified capsules preserve trigger + certificate on all variants and exceed the matched ablation by ≥20 pp — certification tracks a mechanism, not a brittle failure instance.

**RQ3 — Method-zoo accuracy against certified labels.** Component-level top-1 accuracy (primary), per class, with abstention scored; step-level strict and tolerance-w accuracy (secondary) on the same trials — the causally certified counterpart of Who&When's 14.2%. Reported alongside: B0-b's **heuristic-sufficiency rate** as a headline number (if the certified tier is symptom-readable, we say so and release the residual signal-free slice as the challenge tier); the 3×3 directional confusion matrix with the upstream-blame bias statistic; ECE and selective-risk curves; and the Who&When natural-failure gap per method. Floors: random (1/3), majority-class, B0-b. Success is honest measurement, not a threshold: the headroom check pre-commits that if the best method exceeds 0.90 on the certified tier, the challenge-tier release becomes the framing.

**RQ4 — Value of bidirectional interventional evidence.** On certified trials, the crossover diagnoser vs repair-only (DoVer-signal ablation) vs trace-only. Success: bidirectional exceeds repair-only by ≥0.10 macro-F1 (3-class, abstain = error) with paired cluster-robust CI excluding zero, and earlier-boundary dual-flips ≤10% of trials (localization uniqueness). This isolates *why* repair success is not causal evidence — quantitatively.

**RQ5 — Replay-budget efficiency (B2.2).** Accuracy-vs-budget curves for the interventional zoo at C ∈ {0,2,4,8,16} (nested), accuracy-per-replay, token cost secondary; budget-matched judge as the regime control. Saturation check: does the 8-replay score lie within 0.03 of 16? Plus certification k-sensitivity (k ∈ {2,4,8}, 50 trials): label stability under cheaper certification. Paired hierarchical bootstrap clustered by base task.

**RQ6 — Cross-backbone stability (B2.3).** Full certified Stage-1 set re-certified on the pinned open-weights backbone (~300 trials × k=4). Report per class: label survival rate; per-method accuracy delta on the surviving intersection; diagnosis-agreement confusion table. Expectation is asymmetric by design: plan faults should survive backbone swaps more than observation faults (stronger executors recover from polluted observations); the asymmetry itself is a finding about what injected-fault benchmarks measure.

## Related Work

**Annotation-labeled attribution benchmarks.** Who&When [Zhang et al., arXiv:2505.00212] established automated failure attribution with 127 human-annotated failure logs; best methods reach ≈53.5%/14.2% agent/step agreement. TRAIL [Deshpande et al., arXiv:2505.08638] contributes an expert-annotated error taxonomy over GAIA/SWE-bench substrates; MAST [Cemri et al., arXiv:2503.13657] a failure taxonomy from human analysis of 1,600+ traces; "Seeing the Whole Elephant" [Chen et al., 2026] argues for full-observability traces; MP-Bench [In et al., 2026] represents attribution ambiguity via multiple plausible labels. All supply taxonomies or agreement labels; none certifies that a labeled component caused the failure. We use Who&When as the natural-failure contrast set and its judge strategies as baselines.

**Injection-labeled datasets — the nearest neighbors.** Who&When Pro [Liu et al., arXiv:2607.09996] is the closest prior work: 12,326 trajectories built by injecting an error after replaying a successful prefix, labels assigned by the decisive-error convention. The boundary is exactly the certification lattice: no no-op arm, no identity tier, no bypass/lineage verification, no measurement that failure tracks the injected change — its golden labels are what our RQ1 tests, and its label-assignment is our RQ2 ablation (success-to-failure-only injection). AgenTracer [Zhang et al., arXiv:2509.03312] uses programmed injection plus counterfactual annotation as *training supervision* for a learned tracer — injection as a training trick, not a certified, controlled, released measurement instrument for all methods.

**Attribution methods (the zoo we score).** RAFFLES' iterative judge–evaluator [Zhu et al., arXiv:2509.06822]; StepFinder's temporal-semantic trajectory structuring [Zhu et al., 2026]; causal-inference attribution with Shapley credit [Ma et al., 2026]; DRIFT's claim-support span auditing [Wang et al., 2026]; conformal step-set prediction [Feng et al., 2026]. All are evaluated against annotation labels; each is a candidate consumer of certified ground truth.

**Intervention-based debugging.** DoVer [Ma et al., arXiv:2512.06749] verifies blame hypotheses by rewind–edit–replay and counts recovered runs — but repair can succeed without the hypothesized component being the cause; AgentDebug [Zhu et al., arXiv:2509.25370] tests earliest-critical-error hypotheses with single corrective re-rollouts, no budget bound; Causal Agent Replay [Shah, 2026] defines an intervention algebra with do()-resampling, validated on synthetic SCMs with planted ground truth. Their shared, unmeasured premise — failure follows the substituted component — is our RQ1; their techniques enter the zoo as scored methods.

**SE lineage.** Defects4J [Just et al., ISSTA 2014] certifies each real bug backward via a deterministic triggering test; agent pipelines lack such tests, which is why CapsuleBench manufactures ground truth forward and certifies the manufacture. The no-op arm inherits mutation analysis's equivalent-mutant discipline; the minimal-edit framing inherits delta debugging [Zeller & Hildebrandt, TSE 2002]. LLM-judge fragility [Wang et al., "LLMs are not Fair Evaluators", ACL 2024] motivates excluding judges from the label path entirely.

## Threats to Validity

**Construct.** The label certifies a sufficient difference-maker relative to a reproducible base run; latent-cause masking (a dormant natural weakness the injection triggers) is bounded by the reproducibility gate but not eliminated (registered threat t3). Class purity under severity control: a polluted observation is operationally adjacent to a broken tool, and certification selects for outcome-flipping severity — the κ ≥ 0.7 two-rater audit with adjacent-class-only confusion tolerance is the acceptance test. **Internal.** The no-op generators are the load-bearing control; they are audited artifacts with published equivalence ratings, and a failing no-op tier halts certification (pilot gate), it does not soften it. **External.** Certified ground truth is currently manufacturable only where replay is controllable — deterministic, snapshotted, single-scaffold tasks. This is stated as the field's asymmetry, not an apology: the corpus claims *validated labels for method evaluation*, never *representative faults* ("realistic" is deleted from the claim vocabulary). Every method's Who&When natural-failure gap is reported so readers can see what transfers. One scaffold, temperature 0, three fault classes: all scoped on the title page; memory/environment faults and additional scaffolds are the versioned corpus's v2 surface. **Statistical.** Cluster-robust inference at base-task level everywhere; powered-cell rule pre-declares underpowered cells INCONCLUSIVE; multiple comparisons Holm-corrected within RQ families.

## Conclusion

The attribution literature is measuring agreement, and its injection-based branch is assuming causation. CapsuleBench replaces both with a certificate: a fault label exists only when flip, no-op-null, identity-stability, and bypass-lineage arms all hold under replay — and the certificate's own validity is the paper's first measured result. The released instrument — certified corpus, control-lattice protocol, method-zoo harness, re-certification scripts — is designed to outlive the methods evaluated on it, the way Defects4J outlived a decade of fault localizers. The two absorbed axes make it operational: label survival across backbones tells consumers *where* the corpus's authority ends, and replay-budget curves tell them what causal evidence *costs*. When the method churn washes through this crowded area, the durable artifact is the ground truth.
