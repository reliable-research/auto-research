## N1/N2 Motivation (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.motivation`)
The problem addressed in this work is zero-shot detection of machine-generated text: determining whether a passage was produced by a given language model without training a separate classifier or collecting labeled data. Prior work has shown that simple zero-shot baselines, such as thresholding average log probability under the source model, are already effective (@solaiman2019release), and that token-level statistics like rank and entropy provide additional signal (@gehrmann2019gltr). However, these approaches rely entirely on first-order properties of the model’s predictive distribution.

Several limitations of these methods motivate a new direction. Detection accuracy is strongly affected by decoding strategies, with sampling choices such as temperature, top-k, and nucleus sampling systematically altering detectability (@ippolito2020easiest). Moreover, theoretical analysis shows that detectors based purely on distributional differences are fundamentally bounded by the total variation distance between human and model text distributions (@sadasivan2023reliably). Empirically, paraphrasing can largely eliminate surface-level cues used by existing detectors (@krishna2023paraphrasing), indicating that lexical or token-frequency artifacts are not robust signals. These observations suggest that existing zero-shot detectors exploit incidental artifacts of generation rather than intrinsic properties of model samples.

This gap creates an opportunity to design a detector that probes a deeper property of language models: how their probability functions behave locally around a text. Rather than asking whether a passage has high likelihood under a model, the key question becomes whether the passage lies at a local maximum of the model’s probability landscape. Identifying such a signal would provide a zero-shot detector that is less sensitive to decoding strategies and not captured by standard distributional metrics.



## N6 Methodology (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.method`)
We propose DetectGPT, a zero-shot detection method that identifies machine-generated text by estimating the local curvature of a language model’s log-probability function around a candidate passage. The core idea is to compare the log probability of the original text to the expected log probability of small, semantic-preserving perturbations; a consistent drop under perturbation indicates negative curvature characteristic of model-generated samples. This formulation directly addresses the limitations of first-order detectors by leveraging higher-order information about the probability landscape.

1. **Perturbation-Based Neighborhood Sampling**: We generate multiple semantically similar variants of a candidate passage using an off-the-shelf mask-filling model such as T5 (@raffel2020t5). This component is needed to probe the local neighborhood of the text on the data manifold rather than applying arbitrary token noise. It builds on the insight that meaning-preserving perturbations are essential for avoiding trivial likelihood drops unrelated to curvature.

2. **Perturbation Discrepancy Scoring**: For a source model, we compute the difference between the log probability of the original passage and the average log probability of its perturbed variants. This discrepancy captures how sensitive the model’s probability is to small semantic changes and extends prior zero-shot baselines that rely solely on raw log probability (@solaiman2019release, @gehrmann2019gltr). The score can be normalized by the variance of perturbation log probabilities to improve robustness.

3. **Curvature Interpretation via Finite Differences**: We interpret the perturbation discrepancy as an approximation to the negative trace of the Hessian of the log-probability function, using finite-difference and Hutchinson-style estimators. This component is needed to ground the method in a geometric property of the model rather than heuristic statistics, addressing the theoretical concerns raised by distributional limits (@sadasivan2023reliably).

4. **Zero-Shot Decision Rule**: Detection is performed by thresholding the curvature-based score without any supervised training. This preserves the zero-shot setting emphasized in earlier work and aligns with findings that perturbation-based scoring with proxy models can generalize well across generators (@mireshghallah2023smaller).

Together, these components form a coherent system: semantic perturbations define a meaningful local neighborhood, discrepancy scoring measures systematic probability drops, and the curvature interpretation explains why this signal distinguishes model-generated text from human writing.
