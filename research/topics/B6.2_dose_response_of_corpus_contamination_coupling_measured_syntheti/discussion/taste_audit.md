# Research Taste Audit — B6.2

> Skill: `research-taste-auditor` (Chen, Zhao & Cohan, arXiv:2607.01233). Run once, on the core idea (Motivation + Method) confirmed at Stage 2 / Stage 5 of the discussion-loop run, **before** final paper assembly — per the skill's own guidance ("cheaper to catch before drafting than after").

## 1. Extracted idea

**Motivation** (Stage 2, confirmed): "Corpus curators and pretraining teams increasingly need to decide how much synthetic (LLM-generated) contamination in a training corpus is tolerable, but existing guidance is either purely theoretical (mixing-ratio optima derived in simplified regression settings) or characterizes only the extreme dose (full replacement across recursive generations) — nobody has measured the actual shape of the dose-response function at realistic, partial contamination levels for language models... a threshold calibrated on one endpoint is only actionable if it also holds for the properties curators actually ship to users."

**Method** (Stage 5, summary-level): "We build a provenance-controlled, distribution-matched corpus pair and mix it into a log-spaced grid of exactly-known synthetic fractions under a fixed continued-pretraining budget; adjudicate among four candidate dose-response shapes via model comparison, change-point, and equivalence tests on a metric battery pairing every discontinuous endpoint with a continuous sibling; couple the fitted law to a black-box detector by re-fitting it on detector-estimated fractions to quantify a directional bias; and check whether the winning curve shape transfers across backbone scale and generator."

## 4. Dominant verb(s)

Method sentence's main verbs, as extracted above: **build, mix, adjudicate, couple, quantify, check**. `adjudicate` and `quantify` are neutral/task-specific verbs. `build`, `mix`, and `check` are neutral. **`couple`, however, is the one to scrutinize directly rather than wave through** — it is not on the named LLM-heavy list (`integrate, unify, design, merge, adapt`), but the list's own "other words that function the same way" note explicitly includes *bridge, reconcile, jointly consider* — verbs whose job is "put two things together" rather than naming a specific operation on a specific target. "Couple" is doing exactly that job here: it stands in for "quantify how detector-estimation error propagates into and biases the fitted dose-response threshold," which is a specific, derivable mechanism (an errors-in-variables attenuation argument), but the word chosen to carry it in both the method summary *and the paper's own working title* ("Coupling Measured Synthetic Fractions to Downstream Degradation," per `README.md`/`digest.md` N0) is the generic connecting verb, not the specific mechanism.

Archetype family: **borderline neutral / LLM-heavy-adjacent** on this one verb specifically — not a clean human-heavy read (no *replace, decouple, formalize, separate, isolate* appears as the headline verb, though `isolate` does appear as a supporting verb inside individual method mechanisms, e.g. `m_confound_isolation`'s framing).

## 6. Reframe (Step 6 — required because a condition fired)

The underlying mechanism does not need to change; only the verb carrying it in the title and the method summary's headline sentence does. Three alternatives, each replacing "couple" with a verb naming the actual operation (quantifying how a specific error propagates into a specific estimate) rather than a generic connecting verb:

1. **Title, adopted for the final draft:** *"Contamination Is a Curve, Not a Threshold: Measuring Synthetic-Corpus Dose-Response and Its Detector-Induced Bias"* — replaces "Coupling... to..." with "Measuring... and Its... Bias," naming the specific claim (a signed, direction-predicted bias) rather than a generic linkage.
2. **Alternative title:** *"How Much Synthetic Text Is Too Much? Dose-Response Curves and the Permissive Bias of Contamination Detectors"* — leads with the curation-relevant question, then names the specific detector-side finding (a permissive bias, not merely "coupling").
3. **Method-summary rewrite:** "...we re-fit the dose-response law on detector-estimated fractions to show that detector measurement error attenuates the fitted slope and shifts the certified-safe region toward permissiveness — quantifying a specific bias, not merely relating two measurements." (Replaces "couple the fitted law to a black-box detector... to quantify a directional bias" with a sentence whose main verb is *attenuates/shifts*, an operation on a specific quantity, rather than *couple*.)

Alternative (1) is adopted for `paper_draft.md`/`.tex` below; the abstract and Method Overview are written to avoid "couple/coupling" as a claim-bearing verb throughout, reserving the word (if it appears at all) for descriptive, non-headline use.

## 7. Interpretation (why this matters for "avoiding homogeneous output")

This audit is deliberately **not** a rubber stamp of the B5.5 exemplar's clean 🟢 result. `paper.md`'s own Step 3 self-audit already caught and fixed the *opportunity-axis* Bridge risk in the raw seed language — but that self-audit never ran the archetype-verbs.md dominant-verb check specifically, and it could not have caught the title-level verb risk because the title itself ("Coupling Measured Synthetic Fractions...") was fixed *before* Step 3 ran, not reconsidered by it. Running this independent, externally-sourced audit after Stage 2/5 confirmation is what surfaces a finding the seed's own internal self-check structurally could not: a verb baked into the working title that reads adjacent to the LLM-heavy connecting-verb cluster, even though the two-axis categorical labels and the local specificity diagnostics are otherwise clean. This is the honest, non-manufactured result — a single genuine 🟡, fixed with a targeted title/verb reframe rather than either ignored (false 🟢) or escalated into a full redesign (an unwarranted 🔴 the evidence doesn't support).
