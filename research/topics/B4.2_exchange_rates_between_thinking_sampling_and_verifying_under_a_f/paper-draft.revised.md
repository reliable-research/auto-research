# Exchange Rates of Test-Time Compute: Thinking vs. Sampling vs. Verifying under a Fixed Dollar Budget

*Revised draft (v2, 2026-08-14). Supersedes the autodiscussion draft "Dollar-Matched Substitution Rates for Joint Reasoning, Sampling, and Costed Verification"; re-scoped to the pre-registered design in `discussion.md`. Pre-results registered report: all empirical sections state the frozen design; numbers land per the milestone plan (§6.5).*

## Abstract

Deployed language-model systems buy reliability with test-time compute through three levers: lengthening chain-of-thought reasoning ("thinking"), drawing more candidate samples ("sampling"), and paying a judge to select among candidates ("verifying"). Vendors now publish accuracy-versus-cost curves for individual levers — reasoning-effort tiers and multi-agent sample scaling — and recent research prices thinking against verification or sampling against rerouting in two-lever budgets. Yet no study estimates the three-way substitution structure, and almost all prior work treats the verifier as free, although verifier tokens draw on the same budget they are meant to optimize. We measure the exchange rates among thinking, sampling, and verifier-based selection under a single fixed dollar budget in which the verifier's own tokens are charged. Configurations on an (L, N, V) grid — thinking-token caps, sample counts, and priced verifier tiers plus a zero-cost oracle-selection reference — are compared inside matched realized-cost bins (±10%) across four task families ordered by verifiability: code, competition math, graduate MCQ, and open-ended QA. From fitted accuracy surfaces with explicit N×V and L×V interaction terms we estimate marginal accuracy per dollar, pairwise substitution rates, and the efficient accuracy-per-dollar frontier with its allocation mix. Because a dollar is a price-sheet artifact, we adopt a two-tier claim hierarchy: Tier-1 headline claims (lever orderings and interaction signs) must survive fixed-quantity repricing of the frozen token ledger over a ±3× price-ratio region; Tier-2 numeric substitution rates are published as dated, price-contingent estimates. The design pre-registers hypotheses that exchange rates are budget-dependent with an interior optimum for thinking, that frontier composition follows task verifiability, and that interactions are non-additive (N×V complementary, L×V substitutive). The result is the first measured answer to the deployment question no single-lever cost curve can address: which lever the next marginal dollar should fund.

## 1 Introduction

Test-time compute has become the main short-horizon instrument for improving language-model reliability, and its cost structure is now public-facing: vendor evaluations increasingly report accuracy against inference cost for *individual* levers. Current vendor documentation charts score-versus-cost curves across reasoning-effort tiers, and separately across parallel multi-agent configurations that scale the number of sampled solution attempts [OpenAI-GPT5.6-2026]. The research literature mirrors this single-lever structure: budget forcing controls thinking length [Muennighoff et al., 2501.19393; Aggarwal & Welleck, 2503.04697], repeated sampling scales coverage [Brown et al., 2407.21787; Wang et al., 2203.11171], and verifier-guided selection converts coverage into accuracy [Lightman et al., 2305.20050; Zhang et al., 2408.15240].

Single-lever cost curves, however, cannot answer an allocation question. Whether the marginal dollar should buy a longer reasoning trace, another candidate, or a stronger judge depends on quantities no single-lever chart measures: the *interactions* between levers. A verifier is worthless at N=1; longer candidates are more expensive to judge; extra samples without selection buy coverage, not accuracy. Recent two-lever studies confirm that these couplings bind — thinking trades against verification [Think-Again-or-Longer, 2606.19808], generation trades against generative verification [Singhi et al., 2504.01005], sampling trades against rerouting under a marginal-value-per-cost rule [Resample-or-Reroute, 2607.08665] — but each fixes the third lever, and nearly all denominate budgets in tokens or FLOPs while leaving the verifier's own consumption unpriced. Treating verification as free is particularly misleading in deployment terms: verifier tokens are billed from the same budget, and an unpriced judge can erase on the invoice the accuracy it earns on the benchmark.

**This paper measures the three-way exchange rates directly.** Under a fixed dollar budget with a frozen, dated price sheet, we grid-sweep thinking-token caps L, sample counts N, and priced verifier tiers V — charging verifier input and output tokens inside the same budget — and compare configurations only within matched realized-cost bins. Four task families ordered by verifiability (code with executable tests, competition math, graduate multiple choice, open-ended QA) let us test whether the efficient frontier's composition shifts as correctness becomes harder to check. Three hypotheses are pre-registered: **H1** exchange rates are budget-dependent, with an interior optimum for thinking length; **H2** frontier composition is family-specific and ordered by verifiability (code most verify-heavy, open QA least); **H3** interactions are non-additive, with N×V positive (complements) and L×V negative (substitutes).

A dollar-denominated result invites an immediate objection: a dollar is one price sheet's opinion. We make this objection a design constraint. Every run is recorded as a token-level ledger that can be repriced without re-running inference, and claims are split into two tiers. **Tier-1** claims — the ordering of levers by marginal accuracy per dollar and the sign pattern of interactions, per family and budget bin — must remain invariant when the input:output and verifier:generator price ratios are perturbed by ±3× around the frozen sheet, and must also hold under both a GPU-hour-amortized price and a hosted provider's list price for the same open weights. **Tier-2** claims — numeric substitution rates — are published as a dated appendix table explicitly marked as pricing-regime-specific. "Verifying" throughout means *post-hoc selection over N candidates by a priced judge*; sequential revise-with-feedback loops are out of scope and named as future work.

**Contributions.**
1. **A three-lever, verifier-priced, dollar-denominated measurement design**: matched realized-cost bins over an (L, N, V) grid, with the verifier's own tokens charged in-budget and a zero-cost oracle-selection arm separating coverage from selection quality.
2. **Exchange-rate estimands with a robustness hierarchy**: marginal accuracy per dollar, pairwise substitution rates, and N×V / L×V interaction signs, reported only where matched-cost support exists, with Tier-1 orderings/signs required to survive ±3× fixed-quantity repricing and dual price bases.
3. **A cross-family stability analysis** of frontier allocation mixes on the compositional simplex (Aitchison-distance permutation tests), testing whether verify-heaviness tracks task verifiability — including the honest asymmetry that open-ended QA has no free majority-vote proxy, which is itself a finding about where verification dollars are unavoidable.
4. **A pre-registered, budgeted, gated experimental plan** (pilot go/no-go with three falsifiable gates; ~1,100–1,550 A100-hours plus a capped $2K API replication) sized to be executable, with every descoping decision pre-declared.

## 2 Related Work

**Compute-optimal test-time scaling.** Snell et al. [2408.03314] allocate compute between proposer and verifier strategies per prompt difficulty at matched FLOPs; Wu et al. [2408.00724] trade model size against inference strategy; Liu et al. [2502.06703] extend the analysis across policy models, PRMs, and difficulty; Zhang et al. [2410.22480] learn mixtures of sampling configurations; adaptive allocation appears in [2602.01070]. *Boundary:* these works optimize strategy choice under FLOP/token budgets; the verifier's cost is not a priced lever, no dollar meter exists, and reasoning length is not jointly varied.

**Repeated sampling and aggregation.** Self-consistency [2203.11171] and its call-scaling analysis [Chen et al., 2403.02419] characterize majority aggregation; Brown et al. [2407.21787] show coverage scales log-linearly in N and that selection, not coverage, is the binding constraint at large N; Zhao et al. [2502.01839] scale sampling with self-verification. *Boundary:* the sampling axis is varied with reasoning length fixed and aggregation uncharged; coverage ≠ deliverable accuracy — the priced selector that converts one into the other is exactly what we meter.

**Verification and its cost.** Process supervision [2305.20050], generative verifiers [2408.15240], and verifier-based-scaling theory [Setlur et al., 2502.12118] improve selection quality but treat verifier compute as an accuracy control, not an expenditure. Closer to us, a 2025–2026 line begins pricing verification: Singhi et al. [2504.01005] split token-equivalent compute between solving and generative verification; selective verification trades thinking against checking under a budget [2606.19808]; asymmetric verification shows verifier calls can dominate the accuracy-cost frontier in deep search [2510.06135]; verification granularity is tuned for compute efficiency [2606.19354]. *Boundary:* each of these is a **two-lever** analysis (fix L vary N/V, or fix N vary L/V) in token units on one task family; none estimates the joint three-way surface, prices all levers in dollars, or tests price-ratio robustness.

**Length control and inference economics.** Budget forcing [s1, 2501.19393], length-controlled RL [L1, 2503.04697], token-budget estimation [2412.18547], and the overthinking literature [2412.21187; inverse scaling with thinking length, 2507.14417; cost-aware evaluation, 2604.10739] establish that the thinking lever has an interior optimum and that cost-normalized evaluation is now mainstream — for one lever. Cost-of-Pass [Erol et al., 2504.13359] ranks whole pipelines by dollars per solved problem, and [2511.23455] documents inference price-performance trends; budget-aware decision rules appear in [2607.08665; 2607.26828]. *Boundary:* pipeline-level economics does not decompose spending into substitutable levers; single-price-sheet analyses cannot say which conclusions are pricing artifacts.

**Why the cross-product is not incremental.** The union of the above yields three single-axis cost curves, several two-lever token-budget studies, and one pipeline-level dollar metric. Substitution rates are *joint* quantities: they live in the interaction terms (N×V, L×V) that no single-axis or fixed-third-lever study can estimate even in principle, and their dollar denomination changes with the verifier priced in-budget. The measured sign pattern of those interactions, its dependence on budget level, and its stability across task families and price sheets are new information, not an aggregation of existing curves. Vendor-published single-lever cost charts [OpenAI-GPT5.6-2026] make this gap operational: operators already see each lever's own curve and still cannot compute an exchange rate between levers.

## 3 Problem Definition

Let $\mathcal{T} = \{\text{code}, \text{math}, \text{MCQ}, \text{openQA}\}$ be task families in decreasing order of direct verifiability, each with instance distribution $\mathcal{D}_t$ and native outcome $Y_t$ (pass@1-after-selection for code; exact-match accuracy for math and MCQ; rubric score for open QA), analyzed separately in native units.

A configuration is $q = (L, N, V)$ with thinking-token cap $L \in \{512, 1024, 2048, 4096\}$ (budget forcing), sample count $N \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$ at temperature $T{=}0.8$, and selection procedure $V \in \{V_0, V_{\mathrm{cheap}}, V_{\mathrm{strong}}\}$: $V_0$ = zero-verifier-dollar aggregation (majority vote where an answer-equivalence relation exists; the single first sample for open QA — see §4.4), a cheap small-LLM judge, and a strong judge. Verifiers perform *selection only* over the N candidates (no revision, no regeneration). A zero-cost oracle selector $V_{\mathrm{orc}}$ is run at every grid point as an upper-bound reference and is excluded from all cost comparisons and estimands.

Each run produces a token ledger $r(q, x) \in \mathbb{R}^K_{\ge 0}$ (generator input/output tokens; verifier input tokens — the candidates it reads — and output tokens — its judgments). Under a dated price vector $\pi$, realized cost is $C_\pi(q, x) = \pi^\top r(q, x)$; the verifier is thus charged inside the same budget. For bin center $b$, the matched-cost set is $\mathcal{Q}_b^\pi = \{q : |\bar{C}_\pi(q) - b| / b \le 0.10\}$ on expected realized cost, and every reported contrast requires all its configurations in one bin.

The accuracy surface $\mu_t(L, N, V) = \mathbb{E}_{x}[Y_t(q, x)]$ is fitted per family by a monotone-spline/GAM model with explicit N×V and L×V interaction terms. Estimands:

- **Marginal gain per dollar** for an admissible one-step lever move $\delta_a$, $a \in \{L, N, V\}$:
 $G_{t,a}^\pi(q) = \frac{\mu_t(q \oplus \delta_a) - \mu_t(q)}{\bar{C}_\pi(q \oplus \delta_a) - \bar{C}_\pi(q)}$,
 where the denominator is the genuine cross-bin cost increment of the move (never the small residual mismatch inside a bin).
- **Exchange rate (MRS)** between levers $a, b$: $\mathrm{MRS}_{t, a \to b}^\pi(q) = G_{t,a}^\pi(q) / G_{t,b}^\pi(q)$, reported only at interior frontier points with ≥3 neighboring grid cells; edge cells receive qualitative dominance statements.
- **Interaction sign**: the matched-cost difference-in-differences $I_{t,ab}^\pi(q) = \mu_t(q \oplus \delta_a \oplus \delta_b) - \mu_t(q \oplus \delta_a) - \mu_t(q \oplus \delta_b) + \mu_t(q)$, tested by likelihood-ratio tests on the surface's interaction terms.
- **Frontier and mix**: the per-(family, bin) non-dominated (cost, accuracy) set and its allocation composition $(s_{\mathrm{think}}, s_{\mathrm{sample}}, s_{\mathrm{verify}}) \in \Delta^2$, the shares of realized spend by lever role — a scale-free point on the simplex, enabling metric-invariant cross-family comparison via Aitchison distance.

**Claim hierarchy.** Let $\mathcal{P}(\pi_0) = \{\pi : \tfrac{1}{3} \le \tfrac{\pi_j / \pi_k}{\pi_{0,j} / \pi_{0,k}} \le 3\}$ be the perturbation region around the frozen sheet $\pi_0$. **Tier-1 (headline):** lever orderings by $G$ and interaction signs, per (family, bin) — reported only if invariant for every $\pi \in \mathcal{P}(\pi_0)$ *and* under both the GPU-amortized and hosted-list-price bases. **Tier-2 (dated):** numeric MRS values under $\pi_0$, published in a dated appendix labeled pricing-regime-specific. Claims failing full-range invariance but holding on ≥80% of the log-price volume are reported separately as *retention* claims, never as headlines.

**Hypotheses.** H1: $G$-orderings change across budget bins, and $G_{t,L}$ crosses zero at an interior $L^*$ (overthinking). H2: $s_{\mathrm{verify}}$ on the efficient set is ordered code > math > MCQ > openQA. H3: $\bar{I}_{t,NV} > 0$ and $\bar{I}_{t,LV} < 0$.

## 4 Method

*Figure 1: Overview of the exchange-rate measurement pipeline. Budget bins and the three compute channels (thinking L, sampling N, verifying V) enter matched-cost estimation with the verifier's own cost inside the budget; ±3× repricing of the frozen ledger separates Tier-1 headline claims from dated Tier-2 numbers; pilot gates control scale-up.*

### 4.1 Dollar-cost accounting (M1)

Every call logs (input tokens, output tokens, role ∈ {generator, verifier}, model) from engine usage fields; dollars are computed by multiplying against a **price sheet frozen at experiment start** and published as a dated artifact. Open-weight pricing has two bases: primary = a GPU-hour-amortized rate (utilization measured during the pilot; formula published), secondary = a hosted provider's list price for the same weights (verified at freeze). Verifier calls are metered identically: candidates it reads are input tokens, judgments are output tokens. All results are additionally reported in normalized output-token-equivalent units, decoupling the analysis from any dollar figure. The ledger is frozen after execution; every repricing analysis is a fixed-quantity inner product, requiring no new inference. (Package-exclusive device-second metering, footprint ladders, and serialization replay from the v1 draft are demoted to an appendix accounting variant; token-metered dollars are the deployable unit practitioners are billed in.)

### 4.2 Grid, budgets, and matched-cost bins (M2, SP4, SP6)

The nominal grid is $4 \times 5 \times 3 = 60$ cells plus the oracle arm, crossed with 3–4 budget levels per family. Because realized cost is stochastic (models cannot be forced to emit exactly L tokens), matched budgets are an *empirical* property: a pilot token-statistics pass (arm A0) estimates each cell's expected cost, an expected-cost pre-screen keeps only matched-cost-bin members (~35 configurations per family in the pilot), and realized cost is recorded per query with regression adjustment inside bins. Bin membership uses cross-fitted cost estimates so the same observations never both define and exploit a bin. Grid economics is treated as a design problem: identifiability of the N×V and L×V terms is checked by simulated power analysis on pilot data *before* scale-up, and the doubling grids for L and N are retained (not densified) because interaction identification, not curve smoothness, is the binding constraint.

### 4.3 Surface fitting and exchange-rate estimation (M5, SP2)

Per (family, budget bin): the non-dominated (mean realized cost, accuracy) set; the fitted surface with monotone smoothing in $\log L$ and ordered-factor differences in N; MRS by local finite differences at interior frontier points; elbow location by max curvature with a bootstrap distribution (10,000 cluster resamples over questions). Any shape descriptor that does not survive spline-smoothing perturbation is demoted from the abstract. Cross-family stability: Aitchison-distance permutation tests (10,000 permutations, Holm-corrected α=0.05) on efficient-set allocation compositions at matched bins. Interaction signs: likelihood-ratio tests on the N×V and L×V terms (H3).

### 4.4 Verifier band and grading hygiene (SP5)

Verifier quality is a confound: a weak judge makes the verify lever look bad. We therefore report a *band*, not a point: the zero-dollar floor $V_0$, cheap and strong judge arms, and the oracle ceiling at every grid point — the oracle separates "no coverage to select from" from "selection failed." $V_0$ is operationalized per family: exact-answer equivalence for math/MCQ; passing-set equivalence on public tests for code (no judge cost); for open QA there is **no majority arm** — $V_0$ is the first sample, because clustering free-form text requires a judge and would leak verifier cost into the "free" arm. This asymmetry is reported as a finding: where verification is hard, selection has no free proxy. Open-QA grading uses a rubric judge disjoint from all in-budget verifiers, with a 200-item double-human audit (target κ ≥ 0.7); grader disagreement propagates into the open-QA error bars. Temperature is a registered scope condition (T=0.8), with a one-cell T ∈ {0.6, 1.0} probe at the pilot's best allocation to bound sensitivity.

### 4.5 Price-robustness analysis (SP1)

For each candidate Tier-1 claim, the frozen ledger is repriced over $\mathcal{P}(\pi_0)$, perturbing the input:output and verifier:generator price ratios on a log grid with refinement near any observed ordering or sign change; bins, comparability, and frontier membership are recomputed at each price point. A claim is headline-eligible only if the ordering/sign is unchanged over the full region and under both price bases; claims holding on ≥80% of log-price volume are reported as retention claims. Because budget caps co-scale with the overall price level, common scaling of all prices is excluded by construction; only relative-price risk is at issue, which is exactly what the perturbation covers.

## 5 Experimental Design

**Task suite (M3), contamination-controlled and hard-tailed:** code = HumanEval+ and a post-cutoff LiveCodeBench slice, graded by test execution; math = MATH-500 plus the AIME 2024/2025 hard tail; MCQ = GPQA-Diamond; open QA = FRAMES (SimpleQA subset as alternate). Pilot: math + code, ~300 questions each; full stage: all four families, ≥500 where the benchmark allows. Prompts are stratified by difficulty (pilot-estimated solve rate) so exchange rates are reported per difficulty stratum as well as pooled — H1's interior optimum is expected to move with difficulty.

**Models:** pilot = one open-weight reasoning model with controllable thinking in the 7–14B class (s1-style budget forcing; exact checkpoint fixed at freeze); full stage adds a ~70B open-weight model (reduced grid) and one commercial API model on a coarse (N, V) plane at one budget level — the API arm carries the list-price realism and is capped at $2K. Thinking-length control is exact only on open weights (SP3); the API arm therefore replicates only the (N, V) plane, and this limitation is stated.

**Arms:**

| Arm | Content | Role |
|---|---|---|
| A0 Calibration | token-statistics pass; price-sheet freeze; grader audit (200 open-QA items, 2 annotators) | cost pre-screen; grading validity |
| A1 Pilot grid | 7–14B model × {math, code} × ~35 matched-cost configs × 300 q | H1 first estimate; go/no-go |
| A2 Full grid | all four families; ≥500 q; 3–4 budgets; denser bins | H1–H3 headline |
| A3 Scale robustness | ~70B model, reduced grid | Tier-1 ordering robustness |
| A4 API replication | commercial API, coarse (N, V) plane, one budget, ≤$2K | dollar realism (first to cut) |

**Research questions (each mapped to a decision):** **RQ1** (H1): do $G$-orderings change across budget bins, and does $G_L$ cross zero at interior $L^*$? **RQ2** (H2): is efficient-set $s_{\mathrm{verify}}$ ordered by family verifiability? **RQ3** (H3): are N×V and L×V signs as registered, at LR-test α=0.05? **RQ4** (validity): does the ledger reconcile with engine usage exports within 1%, and does the open-QA grader pass κ ≥ 0.7? **RQ5** (robustness): which Tier-1 claims survive the full ±3× region and both price bases; which drop to retention status; how do conclusions shift under the free-verifier accounting convention of prior work (repricing comparator with verifier rates zeroed — quantifying exactly what "the verifier is free" distorts)?

**Compute and cost:** A1 ≈ 1–2×10⁸ tokens ⇒ ~100–150 A100-hours (vLLM, 1 node, 4–6 days); A2 ≈ 1–1.5×10⁹ tokens ⇒ ~700–1,000 A100-hours (~1.5 weeks, one 8-GPU node); A3 ≈ 300–400 A100-hours; A4 ≤ $2K API; annotation ~15–20 hours. Total ≈ 1,100–1,550 A100-hours + ≤$2K: one dedicated node for the month, reserved by Aug 15 (named blocker).

**Pilot go/no-go (Aug 27):** GO iff (i) matched-cost bins populate (≥25 configs/family within ±10%); (ii) the pilot frontier is non-degenerate (≥3 distinct allocation mixes on the efficient set) — a corner solution where one lever dominates everywhere is itself publishable ("the exchange-rate question has a corner solution") provided the oracle arm shows selection headroom existed; (iii) verifier arms beat $V_0$ somewhere at matched cost (else swap the verifier once and re-run the V slice; else demote verifying to a two-arm paper). NO-GO ⇒ ICML 2027 fallback with the pilot released as a priority-establishing preprint (ICLR preprint policy verified at CFP).

**Milestones (to ICLR 2027, abstract Sep 18 / paper Sep 25):** Aug 13–17 blockers (GPU reservation, API budget), price freeze, A0, adjacent-work sweep refresh, vendor-chart verification (outcome selects full vs degraded intro variant — the current draft uses the degraded variant, citing only what public charts show); Aug 18–27 A1 + simulated-power check, go/no-go; Aug 28–Sep 10 A2 (+A5 in-run), A4 early, A3 as capacity allows; Sep 10–16 analysis + figures + draft; Sep 18 abstract (worded to be truthful under either a rich frontier or a corner solution: "we characterize when exchange rates are non-constant, and when they are not"); Sep 25 paper (A3 may land here; abstract wording independent of A3). ## 6 Limitations and Scope

(1) "Verifying" = selection only; revise-with-feedback loops are future work. (2) Numeric exchange rates are dated: Tier-2 numbers are artifacts of the Aug-2026 pricing regime by construction, which is why the paper's headlines are restricted to repricing-invariant orderings and signs. (3) Thinking-length control is exact only on open weights; the API replication covers the (N, V) plane only. (4) Temperature is a scope condition (T=0.8) with a two-point probe, not an axis. (5) The open-QA family has no free aggregation arm; its exchange rates are conditional on paying for selection, which we argue is the deployment-relevant regime. (6) Long-form factuality with audited claim extraction — the v1 draft's fourth family — is deferred: its measurement error model is a paper of its own, and coupling it to the exchange-rate estimand would let scorer bias masquerade as a lever effect.

## 7 Conclusion

Single-lever cost curves — now published by vendors themselves — cannot tell an operator what the next marginal dollar buys, because substitution lives in the interactions between thinking, sampling, and verifying, and because the judge's own tokens are billed from the budget being optimized. This work pre-registers and executes the first three-way, verifier-priced, dollar-denominated measurement of test-time-compute exchange rates, with a claim hierarchy that survives the obvious objection to any dollar-denominated result: headline orderings and interaction signs must be invariant to ±3× price-ratio perturbations and to the choice between amortized-GPU and hosted list pricing. Whether the outcome is a budget-dependent frontier with family-specific composition (H1–H3) or a corner solution, the result replaces folklore allocation with a measured exchange rate — and establishes the three-way frame before single-lever charts harden into allocation advice they cannot support.
