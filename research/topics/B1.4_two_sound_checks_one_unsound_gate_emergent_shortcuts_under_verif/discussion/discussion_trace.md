## Seed brief

- **One-sentence description:** Test whether the Boolean (AND/OR) composition of two individually sound-certified verifiers over LLM-agent/code-generation tasks preserves soundness, or whether composition manufactures an emergent shortcut that passes neither verifier alone.
- **Apparent inputs:** a task family with a checkable ground truth (reference solution + differential-test corpus); two verifiers A and B, each independently certified sound on its own single-verifier task variant at a stated budget; a composition rule (AND/OR) and a matched adversarial search budget.
- **Apparent outputs:** matched task-variant suites with machine-checkable single-verifier certificates; existence/absence of a composed-gate shortcut; gap-rate estimates across a task/verifier-pair grid; a structural taxonomy of *why* composition failed where it did.
- **Closest research areas:** reward-hacking theory for verifiable/RLVR rewards (proxy-reward correlation breakdown); composed reward/verifier systems already deployed (flawed-positive detectors, reward chains, execution-free reward models, guardrail aggregation); single-verifier hardening and blind-spot discovery (adversarial test generation, judge-bias mining); fundamental limits of single verifiers (judge-correlation bounds, cryptographic filter impossibility).
- **In scope:** measuring, via constructed and budget-matched adversarial search, whether Boolean composition of two facet-certified verifiers over code/agent tasks preserves soundness, and characterizing the structural conditions under which it doesn't. **Out of scope:** non-Boolean aggregation (weighted/thresholded voting) as the primary claim (touched only as one control arm); ≥3-check pipelines as the primary claim (touched only as one control arm); non-code domains.

## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (proposer):
- `summary`: Given a task with a checkable ground truth, and two verifiers A and B each independently certified sound relative to the specific facet it checks, its own single-verifier task variant, and a stated finite certification procedure, determine whether the Boolean composition of A and B (conjunctive AND or disjunctive OR) remains sound — or whether composition admits an emergent shortcut: a candidate accepted by the composed gate that fails the held-out ground truth, which neither verifier admits when checked alone under an identical search budget.
- `input`: (1) a task family with a checkable ground truth — a reference solution plus a differential-test input corpus large enough to detect behavioral disagreement; (2) two candidate verifiers A and B (e.g., a unit-test suite and a static/property/judge check), each individually certified sound on its own single-verifier task variant at a stated certification budget; (3) a composition rule (AND or OR) and an adversarial search budget, granted identically to whichever gate — single or composed — is under attack.
- `output`: (1) a suite of matched task variants (single-A, single-B, AND-composed, OR-composed) sharing one ground truth, each single-verifier variant carrying a machine-checkable soundness certificate; (2) for the composed variants, either a certified absence of any accepted-but-failing candidate at the stated budget, or a discovered shortcut; (3) a rate estimate of how often such shortcuts arise across a task/verifier-pair grid, and a classification of each discovered shortcut by the structural relationship between A's and B's blind spots.
- `constraints`: each single-verifier task variant must be constructed so that per-verifier soundness is non-vacuous and independently checkable, not assumed; the search budget used to attack a composed gate must be identical to the budget used to certify (or attack) its single-verifier components, so any composed-only shortcut can be attributed to composition rather than to unequal search effort.
- `objective`: a composition rule is judged non-soundness-preserving on a task family if the composed gate accepts a candidate that fails the held-out ground truth, while both single-verifier variants remain certified sound at the identical search budget.
- `study_type`: `empirical` — stated explicitly, not silently inferred: digest N12 settles this directly ("the work measures and demonstrates a property of *existing* verification practice... rather than shipping a new system or benchmark as the primary claim"), and `paper.md` Step 2 independently arrives at the same archetype.

**critique:**
- summary: The draft is unusually precise for a first pass — it already resolves the "AND/OR of two extensionally sound acceptors is trivially sound" vacuity trap by relativizing soundness to facet/variant/budget — but `objective` quietly treats two epistemically different kinds of "certified sound" as one notion.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "objective", anti_pattern: "vague_success (evaluability gap) / fields_misaligned", comment: "The pilot's certification procedure is exhaustive enumeration over a bounded DSL — a complete decision procedure, so 'certified sound' there is a proof. The scale-up's certification is 'no shortcut found within a stated attack budget' — an absence-of-evidence claim that a longer search could always overturn (this is exactly digest N10 t1/t3 and paper.md A3's own stated risk). The objective as drafted doesn't distinguish these, so a scale-up 'emergent shortcut' finding reads as if it carries the same certainty as a pilot one. Left unfixed, this invites exactly the overclaim paper.md's own Assumptions section (A3) was written to guard against."}`
  - `{severity: minor, field: "constraints", anti_pattern: null, comment: "'independently checkable, not assumed' is doing useful work but would benefit from naming the two concrete mechanisms (exhaustive enumeration vs. budget-stamped adversarial search) rather than staying abstract — minor, can be handled by the refiner's formalization duty rather than a full re-propose."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner` (both flags are formalize-level fixes using content already in the seed — the pilot/scale-up distinction and the two certification mechanisms are already fully specified in `paper.md`'s Step 5 notation table and Assumptions A1–A6; nothing new needs to be invented).

### Refiner pass

- `objective` (refined, split into two explicit criteria): "(i) **Existence** (pilot, decided exactly): for a given composition rule, the pilot task family is exhibited as non-soundness-preserving if, over the *fully enumerated* bounded-DSL candidate space, the composed gate accepts a candidate failing the ground truth while both single-verifier variants are exhaustively verified shortcut-free over that same space — this is a proof, not a rate. (ii) **Prevalence** (scale-up, budget-relative): a composed gate is judged to exhibit higher empirical unsound-acceptance behavior than its strictest single-gate component if this holds under identical-budget adversarial search with a pre-stated statistical test — this is a bounded claim relative to the stated attack budget, not a certificate that no further-budgeted search would find a single-gate shortcut too. Reporting must keep (i) and (ii) visually and rhetorically separate at every stage downstream."
- `formal_summary` (added, per stage-1 refiner's formalize-for-free duty): "Task T=(spec, reference r, differential corpus D, |D|≥10⁴). GT(c)=1 iff c(x)=r(x) ∀x∈D. Verifier V: candidate→{accept,reject}. Gate G∈{A,B,A∧B,A∨B}. A *shortcut* against G on variant t is a candidate c with G(c)=accept ∧ GT(c)=0. Sound(V,t,Π)=1 iff certification procedure Π finds no shortcut against V on t, where Π = exhaustive (pilot, decidable) or Π = budgeted(B_cert) (scale-up, an absence-of-evidence claim stamped with its budget). The question: does there exist a task family and rule ∘∈{∧,∨} with Sound(A,t_A,Π)=Sound(B,t_B,Π)=1 but a shortcut against t_∘ — and under what conditions (coverage complementarity κ, defined in Stage 5) does this recur?"
- `constraints` (tightened per the minor flag): "...checkable via one of two named mechanisms: exhaustive enumeration of a bounded candidate space (pilot) or a budget-stamped adversarial search with zero unsound acceptances (scale-up)."
- `changes_made`: bifurcated `objective` into an exact/pilot criterion and a budget-relative/scale-up criterion; added `formal_summary`; named the two certification mechanisms in `constraints`.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 1** — `study_type: empirical`.

## Stage 2 — Motivation

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1:**
- `summary`: The assumption that stacking a second, independently-sound check onto a verification gate can only tighten it — never loosen it — is already load-bearing in deployed LLM-agent and code-verification pipelines, yet no cited system and no prior account of proxy-reward hacking actually tests whether the *composition step itself*, as distinct from each stacked check, preserves soundness.
- `why_it_matters`: If composing two individually-sound checks can silently manufacture a shortcut that neither check admits alone, then every pipeline that adds a second check as a safety upgrade — a style linter alongside unit tests, a flawed-reasoning detector alongside an outcome verifier, a second safety classifier alongside a first — may be reproducing exactly the failure mode it believes it is closing, and doing so more invisibly, because each individual verifier's own certificate remains technically true even while the composed gate fails. This is a materially different claim from "reward hacking exists," which reward-hacking theory already documents for a single proxy (and even for ensembles of reward models); it targets the specific, unaudited step of *combining* two already-hardened checks.
- `who_cares`: Designers of RLVR reward pipelines and guardrail stacks who add a second check as a safety measure without re-validating the combined gate; SWE-agent pipeline builders who pair execution-based tests with execution-free reward models; safety teams who compose per-category classifiers into one guardrail verdict.
- `evidence`: RewardChain and FAPO each motivate their added check by its own individual tightening effect on the outcome verifier, never by an analysis of the combined gate's soundness; R2-Guard's own motivation documents that independently aggregated per-category safety checks under-estimate risk "when unsafe intent is distributed across multiple weak signals" — direct, cited evidence that naive composition already leaks in a closely adjacent setting, just not yet characterized for Boolean AND/OR gates over code/agent verifiers specifically. CorrelatedProxies additionally notes that hacking "persists even when reward models are improved or combined via ensembles" — a hint from an adjacent literature that composition doesn't self-correct, not a direct test of Boolean gates.

**critique:**
- summary: Stake-specific, well-cited, and correctly distinguishes this claim from generic reward-hacking — but coverage of stage 1's third output (the structural taxonomy / complementarity signal) is thin.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "why_it_matters", anti_pattern: "partial_output_coverage", comment: "Stage 1 output (3) is not just a gap-rate number — it is a per-pair structural classification plus a measurable complementarity signal (coverage-overlap of the two verifiers' blind spots). why_it_matters motivates why an existence/prevalence finding matters, but never explains why a practitioner needs the *structural* breakdown specifically, as opposed to a bare 'composition sometimes fails' headline. Without that, a reader could reasonably ask: once I know gaps exist, what do I do with a taxonomy label? That gap is real and unaddressed here."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `why_it_matters` (extended): "...critically, an existence-or-prevalence finding alone tells a pipeline designer only that composition *can* fail, not which of their own check pairings are at elevated risk before they ship it. That is exactly what the structural taxonomy and the coverage-complementarity signal are for: a diagnostic computable from each verifier's mutant-kill set *before* running any adversarial search, rather than a post-hoc audit that already presupposes the damage has been found. A finding of 'gaps exist somewhere' is a warning; a finding of 'gaps concentrate where two verifiers' blind spots are complementary, in these structurally nameable ways' is closer to an actionable pre-deployment check."
- `changes_made`: added the ex-ante-diagnostic argument for the taxonomy/complementarity output specifically.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 2.**

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (4 sub-problems, from digest N5's C1–C4):

- **`sp_nonvacuous_construction`** — *description:* Construct matched task variants where each single-verifier's soundness is genuinely, checkably certified — not assumed — before any composition is attempted. *why_it_is_a_problem:* the Boolean composition of two *extensionally* sound acceptors on one fixed task is trivially sound (intersection/union of supersets of the truth set), so without a non-vacuous, facet-relative construction, any claimed "emergent shortcut" would be a definitional artifact rather than a real finding. *technical_challenge:* the construction must produce, for the same underlying task, a bounded candidate space rich enough to contain both genuine solutions and shortcut-shaped candidates, while keeping that space small enough that per-verifier soundness is exhaustively decidable in the pilot. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_rewardchain_fapo"]` — RewardChain and FAPO both validate their composed checks only by end-task performance gains, never by constructing a case where the composed gate's own soundness is directly examined; this sub-problem is exactly the missing construction.
- **`sp_existence_to_prevalence`** — *description:* Convert a hand-constructed existence counterexample into a rate estimate of how often composition-induced gaps arise across a realistic grid of tasks and verifier-pair types. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a single constructed counterexample only proves the phenomenon is possible; without a separate scaling design, the paper's warning cannot distinguish "a curated adversarial curiosity" from "a property that recurs across a realistic benchmark/verifier-pair grid." *technical_challenge:* the verifier-pair × composition-rule × shortcut-strategy space grows combinatorially, so this cannot be solved by constructing more counterexamples by hand — it requires a separately designed, budget-matched sampling protocol with a pre-specified statistical test. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no cited work attempts to estimate a *rate* of composition-induced failure at all; the nine related works either assume composition is safe or measure a single verifier's own failure rate, never the composed gate's.
- **`sp_attribution_of_emergence`** — *description:* Ensure any discovered composed-gate shortcut can be attributed to the act of composition itself, not to unequal adversarial search effort or an undetected latent bug in a single verifier's own certificate. *why_it_is_a_problem:* if the attacker is simply given a bigger effective budget against the composed gate than the certifier used to clear the single gates, an "emergent" gap is manufactured by unequal effort, not by composition — this would make the paper's central claim an artifact of its own measurement design. *technical_challenge:* since scale-up certification is itself a budget-bounded absence-of-evidence claim (Stage 1's criterion (ii)), distinguishing "composition caused this" from "the single-gate certificate at budget B_cert just happened to miss it" requires an explicit re-verification step, not a one-time certificate. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_correlatedproxies"]` — CorrelatedProxies observes that hacking persists under reward-model ensembling but never asks whether that persistence is attributable to the *combination operation* itself versus each component's own residual imperfection; this sub-problem makes that distinction operational for hard Boolean gates.
- **`sp_emergent_vs_sharedblindspot`** — *description:* Distinguish a shortcut that arises specifically because composition displaces the solution space (true emergence) from one that both verifiers would have missed independently even without composition (a shared blind spot, not a composition effect). *why_it_is_a_problem:* reporting either without the distinction conflates two different claims — "composition creates new risk" versus "these two verifiers were already both weak in the same place" — and only the former is the paper's actual thesis. *technical_challenge:* no single statistic separates the two explanations; it requires characterizing each verifier's failure surface independently (e.g., via mutant-kill coverage) before composing them, then checking whether a discovered shortcut sits inside or outside that pre-characterized overlap. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_utrl_biasscope"]` — UTRL hardens a single test suite and BiasScope mines a single judge's blind-spot inventory, but neither characterizes how two independently mapped failure surfaces interact once combined into a gate; this sub-problem is precisely that missing interaction analysis.

**critique:**
- summary: Coverage checks out — jointly solving all four would deliver stage 1's bifurcated objective (non-vacuous construction underwrites the existence criterion; the other three jointly underwrite the prevalence criterion and its correct interpretation) — but two sub-problems risk reading as the same confound stated twice.
- comments:
  - `{severity: minor, field: "sp_attribution_of_emergence / sp_emergent_vs_sharedblindspot", anti_pattern: "overlap", comment: "Both are about ruling out a false-positive 'emergent' label, and both cite a 'characterize failure surfaces independently' fix. They are genuinely distinct — one is about *measurement-effort symmetry* (did the attacker simply try harder against the composed gate?), the other is about *a structural confound that persists even under perfectly symmetric effort* (two verifiers that were already weak in the same place, with no composition-specific mechanism at all) — but the current phrasing doesn't make that distinction sharp enough to stop a reader from merging them into 'attribution, generally.'"}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `sp_attribution_of_emergence.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is a **budget-symmetry** problem — could a bigger effective search effort against the composed gate alone explain the finding? — not a question about whether the two verifiers' failure surfaces overlap in content."
- `sp_emergent_vs_sharedblindspot.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is a **structural-overlap** problem distinct from budget symmetry — it persists even when attacker effort is perfectly matched, because it asks whether the two verifiers were already blind to the same region *before* composition, independent of how hard anyone searched."
- `changes_made`: made the budget-symmetry-vs-structural-overlap distinction explicit in both `technical_challenge` fields.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 4** (4 sub-problems; 3 sourced from related-work limitations, 1 `__novel__` with a stated decisive-component justification).

## Stage 5 — Method Design

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (4 methods):

- **`m_bounded_dsl_generator`** → targets `sp_nonvacuous_construction`. *proposed_approach:* Programmatically construct matched tuples ⟨task, A-only variant, B-only variant, AND variant, OR variant⟩ sharing one reference solution and a ≥10⁴-input differential corpus, over a bounded program DSL (fixed AST-size/opcode budget) small enough to be exhaustively enumerable in the pilot. *key_insight:* the vacuity trap can't be argued away analytically, but it can be closed constructively — by building a candidate space small enough to *exhaustively* certify per-verifier soundness before composition, "no shortcut passes A or B alone" stops being an assumption and becomes a machine-checked fact the composed-gate claim can stand on. *hypotheses:* ["For each composition rule (AND and OR, scored independently), at least 3 of 5 exhaustively certified pilot task families contain ≥1 emergent shortcut: a candidate accepted by the composed gate while failing the differential ground truth over the fully enumerated candidate space, with both single-verifier variants certified shortcut-free over that same space."]
- **`m_facet_certifier`** → targets `sp_attribution_of_emergence`. *proposed_approach:* Every single-verifier certificate is stamped with its budget (exhaustive or B_cert); any shortcut labeled "emergent" against a composed gate triggers a mandatory re-attack of its single-gate variants at 10× that budget before the label is finalized. *key_insight:* since scale-up certification is an absence-of-evidence claim, the only way to make an "emergent" label mean something is to actively try to break it — re-spending disproportionate effort against the single gates specifically to see if the "emergent" shortcut was really just an under-budgeted single-gate miss. *hypotheses:* ["Re-attacking every emergent-labeled shortcut's single-gate variants at 10× the original certification budget will reclassify a nonzero but minority (<50%) fraction of Stage-1 emergent-labeled shortcuts as shared-blind-spot events — demonstrating the 10× re-attack control is load-bearing (it does catch some artifacts of under-budgeted certification) without being so large that it undermines the emergent-gap findings it is meant to validate."]
- **`m_budget_matched_attacker`** → targets `sp_existence_to_prevalence`. *proposed_approach:* Apply an identical attacker (fixed model, fixed prompt scaffold, fixed attempt budget N) to every single and composed gate across ~200 benchmark tasks and ~6 verifier-pair types, recording per-gate discovery curves; separately, run the same attacker against a UTRL-style adversarially hardened single verifier as an alternative to adding a second check. *key_insight:* a single mixed measurement design — one attacker, one budget, applied uniformly — yields two distinct, separately falsifiable estimands from one instrument: whether composed gates show elevated unsound-acceptance relative to their strictest single component (the prevalence question), and whether hardening one verifier is a better or worse use of the same effort than adding a second one (the "more checks vs. better checks" question) — one search infrastructure, two readouts, not two mechanisms. *hypotheses:* ["Under matched attack budgets, FAR(composed) exceeds FAR(strictest single gate) with McNemar exact test p<0.05 (Holm-corrected across the 12 primary comparisons) and a discordant-pair asymmetry ≥7 percentage points, in at least 4 of the 12 (6 verifier-pair types × 2 composition rules) primary comparisons, including at least one AND-gate comparison.", "For at least one verifier-pair type, FAR of a UTRL-style adversarially hardened single gate is lower than FAR of a naive AND gate of the corresponding unhardened pair by ≥5 percentage points (McNemar exact p<0.05, Holm-corrected within the secondary family), at equal total attack budget — i.e., hardening one check and adding a second check are not interchangeable defenses."]
- **`m_gap_classifier`** → targets `sp_emergent_vs_sharedblindspot`. *proposed_approach:* Label every discovered shortcut with an ablation triple (does it pass A's single gate? B's single gate? the composed gate?) to separate emergent shortcuts from shared-blind-spot events, classify emergent shortcuts into a structural taxonomy (OR-weak-branch, AND-constraint-displacement, facet-split, threshold-interaction), and regress per-cell emergent-gap incidence on a coverage-complementarity score κ = 1 − Jaccard(kill-set_A, kill-set_B) over a shared mutant pool. *key_insight:* the ablation triple and the taxonomy come from one shared instrument — the same mutant-kill-set data that produces κ also produces the taxonomy labels, because a verifier's mutant-kill set is exactly what defines its "blind spot region," so structural class and complementarity score are two views of the same underlying object, not two separate analyses bolted together. *hypotheses:* ["The logistic-regression coefficient of κ on per-cell emergent-gap incidence, controlling for task difficulty and gate type, is positive with Wald p<0.05 (Holm-corrected within the secondary family) and an odds ratio ≥1.5 per 1-SD increase in κ.", "At least 60% of OR-gate emergent shortcuts fall in the weak-branch taxonomy class and at least 50% of AND-gate emergent shortcuts fall in the constraint-displacement class (exact binomial CI excluding the uniform-over-four-classes rate of 25%, p<0.05)."]

**critique:**
- summary: Every method names a real mechanism (none is a goal restatement of its sub-problem) and every hypothesis carries a stated numeric threshold — but two of the four methods each carry two hypotheses, and only one of those two carries an explicit justification for why that's one mechanism rather than scope creep.
- comments:
  - `{severity: minor, field: "m_budget_matched_attacker.hypotheses / m_gap_classifier.hypotheses", anti_pattern: null, comment: "Both methods bundle two hypotheses. m_gap_classifier's key_insight already explains why (shared instrument, two readouts); m_budget_matched_attacker's key_insight gestures at 'one search infrastructure, two readouts' but doesn't say why the *same statistical evidence* (matched-budget attack curves) is what makes both the prevalence claim and the hardening-vs-adding claim testable from one design rather than two separate studies bolted together."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass
