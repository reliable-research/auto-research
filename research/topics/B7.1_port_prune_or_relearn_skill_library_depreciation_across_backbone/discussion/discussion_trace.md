## Stage 2 — Motivation

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1:**
- `summary`: Every backbone upgrade silently converts an agent's accumulated skill library from a maintained asset into an unaudited liability candidate, and teams currently choose among port-everything, wipe-and-relearn, or ad hoc pruning with no measured basis for any of the three.
- `why_it_matters`: Skill libraries are the standard mechanism by which agents amortize exploration cost into reusable capability, and every skill in them was acquired under a specific backbone whose failure modes it encodes — a workaround for a weak model's parsing errors, a scaffold for reasoning steps the weak model could not take alone. When the backbone is upgraded, that encoding is silently invalidated, yet the library carries no record of which behaviors were compensations. If stale skills merely go unused, the loss is bounded (wasted acquisition cost plus retrieval overhead); if they actively mislead the stronger model — negative transfer — the library becomes worse than nothing, and the standard assumption that accumulated skills are a monotone asset is wrong in the direction that silently degrades deployed systems. Upgrades are frequent (every major model release), so the port/prune/relearn decision recurs, and both corner answers are costly: never-retire risks negative transfer, full-relearn forfeits the amortized acquisition investment.
- `who_cares`: Teams operating skill- or memory-augmented coding and embodied agents across model upgrades (the decision is forced on them at every release); designers of skill registries and marketplaces deciding retention/versioning policy; researchers extrapolating skill-library gains measured under one backbone (the Voyager-line evaluation convention) to future backbones.
- `evidence`: The staleness-diagnostics literature measures the *environment* axis only: Library Drift (arXiv:2605.19576) shows unbounded accumulation degrading a coding agent below its no-library baseline (+0.0pp vs +16.2pp for a human-curated library) under codebase drift; SkillGuard/DriftBench (arXiv:2605.10990) benchmark skill degradation as contract violation across 880 pairs — both hold the backbone fixed (`README.md` line 24: "无一测模型代际轴"). The economics framing exists without measurement: Token Economics (arXiv:2605.09104) names skills capital goods but reports no per-skill payback or depreciation curve (`README.md` line 25). Cross-model work reports snapshot transfer accuracy (AFTER, arXiv:2606.23127: 73.1%) with no cost accounting and no retention decision (`README.md` line 26). And the natural experiment is publicly available at every model release — which also means the window is closing (`README.md` line 37: diagnostics filling at ~5 papers/quarter).
- `why_now`: (folded into evidence above — every vendor's release cadence provides the treatment for free, and the occupancy sweep shows the diagnostics core already TAKEN on the adjacent axis.)

**critique:**
- summary: The stakes are specific and the evidence is real (every claim carries a file/ID), but coverage of Stage 1's confirmed outputs is incomplete — two of the four outputs are never motivated.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "why_it_matters", anti_pattern: "partial_output_coverage", comment: "Stage 1's confirmed outputs include (3) a *mechanism decomposition* of value loss and (4) a *decision-adequacy* verdict, and its summary makes per-skill resolution central. ### Refiner pass

- `why_it_matters` (extended, two additions): "...critically, the upgrade decision is not one decision but K of them: a library-level A/B toggle at upgrade time can only choose between the two corner policies it compares, and — if per-skill value change is heterogeneous — both corners can be far from the frontier simultaneously: never-retire keeps the harmful minority, full-relearn re-buys the still-valuable majority. Whether that heterogeneity exists is precisely a per-skill, per-upgrade measurement question, and it determines whether any selective policy can beat the corners at all. Moreover, *which action is right depends on why a skill lost value*: an obsolete-but-harmless skill (the new backbone succeeds without it) is a cost-accounting problem that tolerates lazy pruning; an actively interfering skill (invoked, and inducing failures the bare model avoids) demands urgent retirement even if rarely used; a cost-drag skill (same outcomes, more tokens) is a compression target. These three mechanisms produce identical library-level deltas, so only a mechanism decomposition turns the measured number into a defensible action."
- `changes_made`: added the per-skill-resolution argument (a) and the mechanism-decomposition argument (b); no strength added to any evidence claim.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 2.**

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (5 sub-problems):

- **`sp_liability_attribution`** — *description:* Attribute any performance/cost gap between the upgraded backbone running with the old library and the same backbone running bare to the library's *content*, rather than to context load or to changed invocation behavior. *why_it_is_a_problem:* the headline asset-vs-liability verdict is only meaningful if the contrast isolates content: a library is also thousands of prompt tokens (pure context load) and a changed action space (the stronger model may invoke skills more or less often), and either alone can produce the observed gap. *technical_challenge:* content, token load, and invocation propensity co-vary in any naive with/without comparison; isolating them requires arms that hold two of the three fixed while varying the third — including a library that is token-matched but content-free, and invocation regimes that remove the model's selection freedom. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no prior work toggles a frozen library across backbone generations at all (README line 24), so none faces this attribution problem.
- **`sp_per_skill_accounting`** — *description:* Estimate each skill's marginal utility (success benefit and cost delta) under compositional, retrieval-gated use, where skills act in combinations and full factorial ablation over 2^K subsets is infeasible. *why_it_is_a_problem:* the port/prune/relearn decision is per-skill; a library-level number cannot rank retirement candidates (Stage 2's heterogeneity argument), and Token Economics' framing stops exactly at this missing estimand (`lim_no_payback_curve`). *technical_challenge:* a skill's observed contribution is entangled with which other skills are present and with which tasks the retriever activates it on; the estimator must define a per-skill contrast that is computable within a token budget (masking one skill at a time on its own activation set) and defend the conditions under which that contrast identifies a per-skill quantity. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_no_payback_curve"]`.
- **`sp_mechanism_separation`** — *description:* Separate three mechanisms of value loss — obsolescence (the new backbone succeeds without the skill), interference (the skill actively induces failures the bare model avoids), and cost drag (same outcomes, more tokens) — which produce identical library-level deltas. *why_it_is_a_problem:* the right upgrade action differs by mechanism (Stage 2), and contract-violation detection is blind to two of the three (`lim_contract_blindspot`: an obsolete or interfering skill can be perfectly contract-conformant). *technical_challenge:* the mechanisms are only distinguishable at the level of per-task paired traces (what did the bare run do on exactly the tasks where the skill fired?), so the separation must be built from the same paired replays as the accounting, with mechanically-checkable signatures rather than judgment calls. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_contract_blindspot"]`.
- **`sp_estimate_stability`** — *description:* Establish that per-skill estimates from finite, stochastic replays are stable properties of the (skill, backbone) pair rather than sampling artifacts. *why_it_is_a_problem:* per-skill activation sets are small (a skill may fire on 20–50 tasks), agent runs are stochastic, and an unstable estimate would make every downstream ranking and verdict noise; this is a *variance* problem — enough independent evidence per skill against run-to-run noise. *technical_challenge:* stability must be demonstrated, not assumed — via agreement of estimates computed on disjoint task splits — and the power budget per skill (activations × seeds) has to be set so that instability is detectable rather than invisible. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]`.
- **`sp_decision_sufficiency`** — *description:* Design a depreciation-thresholded retirement policy that beats the corner policies. *why_it_is_a_problem:* the measurement is only worth its cost if it improves the decision. *technical_challenge:* choosing the threshold. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_no_payback_curve", "lim_snapshot_transfer"]`.

**critique:**
- summary: Four of five sub-problems are well-separated with real challenges; the fifth is a method in disguise, and its framing quietly re-imports the beats-baseline objective Stage 1 already demoted.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "sp_decision_sufficiency", anti_pattern: "method_leak", comment: "'Design a depreciation-thresholded retirement policy that beats the corner policies' is a proposed solution, not a problem — and 'beats' resurrects the outcome-tier framing removed in Stage 1 (cross-stage consistency). The underlying problem is: determine whether the per-skill estimates, at their achievable noise level, carry enough information to support the port/prune/relearn decision — i.e. whether *any* rule reading only the estimates can approach the oracle frontier. That is a property of the measurement (decision-adequacy), method-free as stated, and it is what Stage 1 output (4) actually promises."}`
  - `{severity: minor, field: "sp_estimate_stability / sp_decision_sufficiency", anti_pattern: "overlap (risk)", comment: "Both concern trusting the estimates. Make the division explicit: stability = is the estimate a reproducible property (measurement reliability, a variance question); sufficiency = even given a reliable estimate, is its information content adequate for the decision (an identification/coverage question — e.g. harm concentrated in rarely-activated skills below the accounting floor would make reliable estimates decision-insufficient)."}`

### Refiner pass

## Stage 5 — Method Design

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (5 methods):

- **`m_frozen_replay`** → targets `sp_liability_attribution`. *proposed_approach:* Acquire a library once per (stream, M_0) with a fixed Voyager/AWM-style acquisition loop, freeze it with per-skill acquisition ledgers (provenance backbone, acquisition tokens, validation record), then replay the *identical* task stream under each backbone in paired arms — bare vs full-library — with harness, retriever, prompts, and turn budgets byte-identical across arms except library content; run both free-use (model chooses invocation) and forced-use (retrieved skill must be attempted) regimes to separate content effects from invocation-propensity shifts. *key_insight:* backbone generation becomes a clean treatment variable only if everything the model touches except the backbone is frozen; the pairing (same task, same seed, both arms) cancels task difficulty and lets the *same-backbone* bare arm absorb the capability confound that would sink any cross-backbone comparison. *hypotheses:* ["H1: In at least one tested (stream × upgrade-pair) cell, the frozen old library reduces the upgraded backbone's paired task-success rate by ≥2 percentage points, or raises its token cost per solved task by ≥10%, relative to the same backbone running bare (Holm-corrected p<0.05 across cells) — the asset-to-liability flip exists.", "H2: Median per-skill value retention declines with generation distance: for the same library, the two-generation upgrade shows lower median retention than the one-generation upgrade, in the majority of tested streams."]
- **`m_placebo_library`** → targets `sp_liability_attribution`. *proposed_approach:* A token-matched placebo arm: replace the library with syntactically valid, retrievable, task-irrelevant skills (drawn from a disjoint domain), matched in count, token length distribution, and retrieval-key statistics; identical harness. *key_insight:* the placebo carries the context load and retrieval mechanics without the content. *hypotheses:* ["H3: The placebo library reproduces less than 50% of the real library's measured harm (success and cost deltas vs bare) in every cell where H1's flip is observed — the harm is content-driven, not context-load-driven."]
- **`m_utility_ledger`** → targets `sp_per_skill_accounting` (+ feeds `sp_estimate_stability`). *proposed_approach:* For each skill with ≥ an activation floor of retrievals, estimate marginal utility by leave-one-out masking: replay a sampled subset of the skill's activation tasks with the library minus that one skill (retriever re-run without it), paired against full-library runs on the same tasks and seeds; combine with the acquisition ledger to compute amortized net value (acquisition cost − cumulative reuse benefit) per skill per backbone. *key_insight:* masking one skill on its own activation set turns an infeasible 2^K factorial into K bounded paired contrasts, and the acquisition ledger converts benefit into the economic quantity (payback) the port/prune/relearn decision needs. *hypotheses:* ["H4: Per-skill value change under upgrade is heterogeneous: in the majority of (stream × upgrade-pair) cells, at least 20% of floor-eligible skills retain ≥50% of their acquisition-time marginal utility while at least another 20% fall to zero or negative retained utility.", "H5: Per-skill retention estimates computed on disjoint halves of each skill's activation set agree at Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6 (rank agreement over floor-eligible skills), in the majority of cells."]
- **`m_mechanism_decomposition`** → targets `sp_mechanism_separation`. *proposed_approach:* Classify every floor-eligible depreciated skill by mechanical trace signatures computed from the same paired replays: *obsolescence* — bare-arm success on the skill's activation set ≥ with-skill success, skill delta ≈ 0; *interference* — with-skill failures on tasks the bare arm solves, with the skill invoked in the failing trace; *cost drag* — success delta ≈ 0 with positive token delta; aggregate shares of library-level harm attributable to each class. *key_insight:* the three mechanisms are indistinguishable in aggregate but leave distinct, mechanically-checkable signatures in per-task paired traces — no judge model, no human labels. *hypotheses:* ["H6: Interference accounts for at least 25% of the library-level net harm in at least one cell where H1's flip is observed — depreciation is not pure cost accounting; actively misleading content exists."]
- **`m_retention_sweep`** → targets `sp_decision_sufficiency`. *proposed_approach:* Rank floor-eligible skills by estimated retained value; sweep the retention threshold to trace the (total cost, success rate) frontier, charging full re-acquisition cost for refreshed skills and metering all measurement cost; compare against the corner policies and against an oracle that ranks by held-out ground-truth value. *key_insight:* oracle-normalization converts 'is the policy good' (a method question) into 'how much of the achievable frontier does the *estimate* recover' (a measurement-adequacy question). *hypotheses:* ["H7: The depreciation-thresholded rule beats never-retire and full-relearn on the cost–success frontier."]

**critique:**
- summary: Mechanisms are concrete and every method traces to its sub-problem, but one hypothesis is exactly the outcome-tier claim two earlier stages already removed — third recurrence of the same drift — and one carries no floor at all.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "m_retention_sweep.hypotheses[H7]", anti_pattern: "unfalsifiable_hypothesis (no floor) + cross-stage inconsistency", comment: "'Beats never-retire and full-relearn on the frontier' has no effect floor, no operating point, and is the beats-baseline claim Stage 1's confirmed objective explicitly forbids ('never as a beats-baseline contest'). The method's own key_insight (oracle-normalization) already contains the right hypothesis — state it: a pre-registered fraction of the oracle frontier's cost reduction recovered at a pre-registered operating point. Corner policies then appear as reported reference values, not as the claim."}`
  - `{severity: minor, field: "m_placebo_library.key_insight", anti_pattern: "weak_key_insight", comment: "'Carries the context load without the content' restates the approach. The insight is the identification logic: any harm the placebo reproduces is attributable to load/mechanics and is *subtracted* from the content-attributed harm — the placebo is the measurement's null instrument, and H1's causal reading is licensed only jointly with H3."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `m_retention_sweep.hypotheses[H7]` (rewritten): "H7: Thresholding on the estimated per-skill retained value recovers at least 80% of the oracle frontier's cost reduction relative to the never-retire corner, at the pre-registered matched-success operating point, in the majority of (stream × upgrade-pair) cells." `[SR-fix]` (applied later, from `stage_review.md` Experiments REVISE): the operating point is pinned *in the hypothesis text itself* as "the cost comparison taken where each policy's success rate is within ±1pp of the never-retire corner's success rate", eliminating the swept-frontier cherry-pick; frontier curves are reported descriptively only.
- `m_placebo_library.key_insight` (rewritten): "The placebo is the measurement's null instrument: it isolates the harm attributable to context load and retrieval mechanics alone, so content-attributed harm is what remains after subtracting it — H1's causal reading is licensed jointly with H3, not by H1's contrast alone."
- `m_utility_ledger` `[SR-fix]` (applied later, from `stage_review.md` Method REVISE): the approach now states its licensing assumption explicitly — leave-one-out masking identifies a per-skill quantity only if cross-skill interactions are second-order; a pairwise-interaction probe (pair-masking on the top-frequency skill pairs; pre-set bound on the interaction share of total attributed value, 15%) is added to the approach, with a pre-committed downgrade: if the bound is exceeded, utilities are reported at retrieval-cluster granularity instead of single-skill granularity and H4/H5 are re-evaluated at that granularity.
- `changes_made`: floored and oracle-normalized H7; sharpened the placebo insight into its identification role.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 5** — 5 methods, **7 hypotheses** (H1–H2 on `m_frozen_replay`, H3 on `m_placebo_library`, H4–H5 on `m_utility_ledger`, H6 on `m_mechanism_decomposition`, H7 on `m_retention_sweep`).
