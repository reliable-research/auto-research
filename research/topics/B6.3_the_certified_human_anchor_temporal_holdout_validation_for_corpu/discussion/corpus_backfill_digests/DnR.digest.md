## N1/N2 Motivation (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.motivation`)
Current zero-shot AI-text detectors reveal a consistent tradeoff between informativeness and practicality. Probability-based perturbation methods such as DetectGPT and Fast-DetectGPT show that model response under controlled perturbation is highly discriminative, but they depend on likelihood access or repeated model evaluations, which limits deployment with closed commercial models and raises query cost @mitchell2023detectgpt; @bao2024fastdetectgpt. Black-box generative alternatives partly avoid this constraint: DNA-GPT compares regenerated continuations with the original suffix, and RAIDAR measures invariance under rewriting. However, both still require multiple calls and operate over relatively unconstrained generation spaces, introducing computational overhead and instability, especially when text is short or the transformation model differs from the source model @yang2024dnagpt; @mao2024raidar.

Recent black-box methods further sharpen this gap. Binoculars demonstrates that useful detection signals can be obtained without logits, but does so through a contrast between two models rather than a direct transformation-based recoverability signal @hans2024binoculars. DALD shows that hidden probability information can be approximated in black-box settings, yet this requires surrogate alignment machinery that is indirect and sensitive to distribution mismatch @zeng2024dald. Text Fluoroscopy indicates that observable intrinsic properties of text can generalize better than domain-specific semantic cues, suggesting that a detector should rely on robust structural signals rather than inaccessible probabilities @yu2024fluoroscopy. Taken together, these works expose a concrete open problem: can we design a black-box detector that preserves the distributional sensitivity of perturbation-based methods while requiring only a single LLM call?

This gap matters because real-world detection systems must operate at scale over heterogeneous source models, often with only API access and limited budget per document. The opportunity is to replace direct posterior estimation with an observable proxy: if machine-generated text lies closer to an LLM’s learned distribution, then after a carefully chosen semantic-preserving disruption it should be easier for another LLM to reconstruct. A detector built around this recoverability signal could retain the core insight behind perturbation and rewriting methods while removing their dependence on repeated calls and explicit probability access @mitchell2023detectgpt; @mao2024raidar.

## N6 Methodology (verbatim from corpus record `proposal.method`)
We propose a single-call Disrupt-and-Recover framework for black-box AI-text detection. The core idea is to apply a structured local disruption to the input, ask a black-box LLM to recover the original text once, and use the similarity between the recovered and original text as a proxy for posterior concentration. This formulation addresses the identified gap by converting an inaccessible distributional property into observable semantic and structural recovery signals under a tightly constrained recovery task.

1. **Within-Chunk Shuffling Disruption**: We first segment the text by punctuation and randomly permute tokens only within each chunk. This creates a semantic-preserving but order-disrupting input whose recovery space is far more constrained than paraphrasing or free continuation, making one recovery sample informative. This extends the transformation-consistency logic of @mao2024raidar while adopting the perturbation perspective of @mitchell2023detectgpt in a model-free, black-box-compatible form.

2. **Single Black-Box Recovery Call**: We submit the shuffled text to a recovery LLM with an instruction to restore the correct word order without adding or removing words. This step is needed to expose how strongly the model can reconstruct the original under constrained disruption, but unlike RAIDAR, DNA-GPT, or Fast-DetectGPT, it uses only one generation call rather than multiple rewrites, regenerations, or perturbation evaluations @mao2024raidar; @yang2024dnagpt; @bao2024fastdetectgpt. The single-call design is the operational mechanism by which efficiency and black-box practicality are achieved.

3. **Semantic Recoverability Measurement**: We compute semantic similarity between the original and recovered text using BERTScore F1. This component captures whether the recovered output remains close in meaning even when exact wording or local phrasing shifts occur, which is necessary because recoverability should reflect more than token-level matching. It is consistent with the broader lesson from @yu2024fluoroscopy that robust intrinsic signals beyond explicit probabilities can support generalizable detection.

4. **Structural Recoverability Measurement**: We compute structural similarity using rank-based word-order correlations such as Kendall's tau and Spearman's rho after token alignment. This is needed because the disruption is specifically an ordering perturbation, so a faithful detector must measure restoration of sequence structure rather than semantics alone. In this sense, the method operationalizes the perturbation-response principle from @mitchell2023detectgpt and the reconstruction comparison idea from @yang2024dnagpt at the level of local ordering fidelity.

5. **Lightweight Detection Head**: We feed the semantic and structural recoverability metrics into a simple binary classifier that outputs the final label. This component aggregates complementary signals into a stable decision rule while remaining far simpler than surrogate-distribution modeling approaches such as @zeng2024dald. It turns the recovered sample into a practical estimate of whether the text induced a concentrated or dispersed recovery posterior.

These components form a coherent system because the disruption defines a constrained recovery problem, the single recovery call exposes the model’s effective posterior behavior, and the semantic-structural metrics translate that behavior into observable evidence. Together they provide a black-box, efficient approximation to the concentration signal that prior work could access only through logits, multiple generations, or more indirect proxies.
