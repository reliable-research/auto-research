## Datasets

- **AgentDojo** (Debenedetti et al., 2024) — real tool-calling tasks (email, banking, travel, Slack) with programmatic checkers and structured, injectable tool returns. Pilot-stage domain: cheapest to instrument the corruption injector and replay oracle against. Used as the core corpus for RQ1–RQ5, RQ7 and as one of two frameworks for RQ6.
- **GAIA** (Mialon et al., 2023) — long-horizon, multi-step assistant tasks where a single early observation is load-bearing many steps later. Justification is hypothesis-linked, not provenance-only (this was a Stage 6 critic finding, fixed in the refiner pass — see `discussion_trace.md` Stage 6): used specifically to stress-test **RQ6**'s locus factor (early/mid/late injection tercile) and its type-dependence claim under longer causal chains than AgentDojo's shorter tasks provide.
- **Who&When human-annotated failure logs** (OpenReview:GazlTYxZss) — source of real, human-annotated genuine-plan-failure controls with independently verified clean observations, avoiding the circularity of self-labeling "genuine" plan failures from synthetic data alone. Feeds the false-positive control set used in RQ3's non-inferiority check.

## Attribution methods under test (run unmodified, blinded to injection metadata)

Five methods, per `paper.md`'s Methodology / E1 baseline list: Who&When's LLM-judge variants (all-at-once, step-by-step, binary-search protocols — counted as up to 3 judge variants for the trust-license probe), an AgenTracer-style trained tracer (borrowed as an out-of-distribution stress test, not a like-for-like baseline — see Stage 3's refiner note in `discussion_trace.md`), and a SupervisorAgent-style runtime monitor replayed post hoc.

## Baselines / comparators (sanity-check floors, not outcome-tier competitors)

- **Constant "always-predict-plan" attributor** — floors the comparison: any method whose accuracy on the corrupted set does not beat naive majority-guessing is not performing attribution on corrupted evidence at all. Removes the *trivial-majority-class confound*.
- **Overt-tool-error arm** (identical tasks/loci, visible fault signal — exceptions, timeouts, empty returns) — establishes that methods *can* localize tool faults when the fault emits a signal; the necessary comparator for the Δ-trust gap (RQ5). Task- and step-locus-matched against the treatment set, removing the *task-difficulty confound*.
- **Matched agent-side-fault arm** (identical wrong value, injected into the agent's action instead of the tool's observation, same operators/subtlety/budget) — isolates whether any measured bias is specific to the tool-observation channel or reflects the general difficulty of noticing any subtly wrong value. Removes the *injector-difficulty confound*.

**Placebo validity gate (checked before any RQ below is scored, per `paper.md`'s B0-placebo).** Two null-by-construction checks run first: (i) a sham-injection arm (identity mutation o′_t = o_t through the identical injection→replay→serialization pipeline; null criterion: attribution distribution on sham-processed vs. untouched twins equivalent within ±3 percentage points per layer share); (ii) a label-shuffle placebo (1,000 permutations of the D_sem/D_overt arm labels within matched task-step pairs; null criterion: permuted Δ-trust-gap distribution centered on zero). A non-null result on either invalidates the run in full — no RQ may be scored from an invalid run.

**Premise-collapse branch (governs whether RQ5/RQ6 are scored as pass/fail or reported descriptively).** If m_A(D_overt) > 40% for a majority of the 5 methods, methods cannot reliably localize even overt, signal-emitting tool faults; the schema-validity distinction is not yet interpretable, RQ5/RQ6 are reported descriptively only, and the headline finding becomes a competence audit anchored on the constant-attributor floor (`paper.md` Fallback F0).

## RQ1 — Does the automated legality screen approximate human undetectability?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_form_invisibility`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_schema_preserving_injector` / Stage 6 `rq_screen_undetectability`):** "At least 90% of corruptions that pass all three automated gates will also be judged 'plausible in context' by an independent human rater blind to which observations were corrupted, confirming the automated screen approximates human undetectability rather than being a weaker proxy for it."
- **Setup:** Draw a stratified sample of 150 corrupted observations that pass all three automated legality gates (30 per operator family: numeric perturbation, entity substitution, boolean flip, list truncation/reorder, stale-value substitution), embedded in surrounding trajectory context, alongside a calibration set of 50 candidates the injector rejected at the plausibility gate. Present both sets to an independent human rater blind to which observations were corrupted or rejected. (`paper.md`'s Instrument-Validation section additionally runs a smaller 50-gate-passing + 50-gate-rejected calibration pass, with 2 raters, inside the walking skeleton before this scale-up sample — the skeleton pass freezes the screen prompt/model/cutpoints, per gate G5, before the 150+50 scale-up sample above is drawn.)
- **Sample-size rationale:** 150 gate-passing (30/operator family, enough per family for a stable rate estimate) + 50 rejected-candidate calibration, following the walking-skeleton's smaller 50+50 pilot that must first pass acceptance (rater–rater κ ≥ 0.7; ≥90% of gate-passing corruptions rated plausible-in-context; gate-rejected calibration rated plausible at a substantially lower rate, target < 60%; screen–rater disagreements concentrated in the borderline band [0.5, 0.8), not collapsed).
- **Statistical test:** Proportion comparison (gate-passing plausibility rate vs. rejected-candidate calibration rate); Cohen's κ between two independent raters as an instrument-reliability precondition (`paper.md` guidance.md-R2 validation step). **Cutpoint sensitivity (per `paper.md`'s instrument-validation plan; noted here 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` "missing RQs" item 4):** at scale, the M5 grid is re-stratified at screen confidence cutpoints 0.7 and 0.9 to show the "subtle" construct is not an artifact of the frozen 0.8 constant.
- **Expected outcome:** At least 90% of gate-passing corruptions are independently rated plausible-in-context, versus a substantially lower rate (target: below 60%) for the rejected-candidate calibration set.
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted if the gate-passing plausibility rate falls below 90%, or if it is not substantially higher than the rejected-candidate calibration rate — either would mean the automated screen is a weak proxy for human undetectability, and any downstream misattribution claim would need re-examination for whether it measures diagnosis under a genuinely invisible corruption.
- **Metrics:** Human-rated plausibility rate on gate-passing vs. rejected-candidate sets; screen–rater Cohen's κ.

## RQ2 — Does load-bearing-field targeting drive verified corruption yield?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_ground_truth_construction`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_counterfactual_replay_oracle` / Stage 6 `rq_verified_yield`):** "The yield of decisive corruptions (twin-flip rate) among schema-gate-passing candidates is at least 20% when corruptions target fields the agent's subsequent step actually references (load-bearing fields), versus a substantially lower yield for corruptions on non-referenced fields — confirming load-bearing-field targeting, not corruption volume alone, drives verified yield."
- **Setup:** Attempt corruption injection on load-bearing fields (fields referenced by the agent's own subsequent step in the uncorrupted run, identified by string/AST trace matching) versus non-load-bearing fields, matched for operator family and task domain; compute twin-replay-verified yield separately for each group over 100 pilot candidates per group.
- **Sample-size rationale:** 100 pilot candidates per group, directly tied to the walking-skeleton's assumption-A1 go/no-go gate G1 (yield ≥ 20% on 100 candidates; digest N6 pilot arithmetic) — below this yield, scale-up does not proceed at all until load-bearing-field targeting is revised and the pilot re-run.
- **Statistical test:** Two-proportion comparison of verified yield between the load-bearing and non-load-bearing groups.
- **Expected outcome:** Verified yield on load-bearing-field candidates is at least 20%, versus a substantially lower yield (target: below 10%) on non-load-bearing-field candidates.
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted if load-bearing-field yield falls below 20%, or if the two groups' yields are statistically indistinguishable — either would mean load-bearing-field targeting is not what drives verified yield.
- **Metrics:** Twin-replay verified yield rate per group.

## RQ3 — Does a trust-license prompt reduce misattribution?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_evidential_circularity`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_blinded_trust_probe_harness` hyp. 1 / Stage 6 `rq_trust_license_reduces`):** "For at least 2 of 3 LLM-judge variants, adding the trust-license sentence significantly reduces the misattribution rate on the corrupted set (paired test) without a significant increase in false-positive tool-blaming on genuine plan-failure controls."
- **As quantified in `paper.md`'s pre-registered spec (H3, E3):** "For ≥2 of 3 judge variants, the one-sentence trust license reduces m_A(D_sem) by ≥5 percentage points absolute (paired McNemar p < .05) AND the FPR increase on genuine-plan-failure controls is ≤3 percentage points absolute (non-inferiority margin, one-sided 95% CI upper bound ≤ 3 pp)."
- **Setup:** Within-method paired design: run each of the 3 LLM-judge variants twice on the same verified corrupted trajectories per framework — once with, once without, the one-sentence trust-license addition (a prompt sentence stating tool observations may be silently wrong and should be independently sanity-checked). Applies only to the 3 LLM-judge baselines (the tracer/monitor cannot take a prompt intervention — pre-declared scope, `paper.md` E3).
- **Sample-size rationale:** Reuses RQ5's n ≥ 300 verified corrupted trajectories per framework per arm (D_sem) — no separate sample is drawn; the same verified treatment set is scored twice (with/without license). **Power statement for the paired reduction (added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` F-4's H3 half — McNemar power depends on the discordant-pair rate, not n alone):** at the pre-registered 5 pp net reduction under an assumed 5% worsening rate (discordant rate 15% → ≈45 discordant pairs per judge at n=300, true favorable share 2/3), the exact McNemar test has only ≈0.57 power; ≥72 observed discordant pairs give ≈0.8 power (0.81; ≈0.89 at 90) at the same favorable share (exact binomial, two-sided α=.05, reproducible). **Pre-declared band:** H3's reduction clause is scored per judge variant only when ≥72 discordant pairs are observed (frameworks pooled, consistent with H3's per-variant statement); below 72, the reduction verdict is INCONCLUSIVE — δ_A reported with CI, and F3's "does not repair" wording does not fire as a null-based claim. The FPR non-inferiority clause is scored on its own one-sided 95% CI regardless.
- **Statistical test:** Paired McNemar for the reduction; Farrington–Manning non-inferiority test for the FPR increase on M6(b) plan-failure controls; Holm correction across the 3 judges.
- **Expected outcome:** For at least 2 of 3 judge variants, the license reduces the misattribution rate by at least 5 percentage points absolute (paired McNemar, α = 0.05) without a significant false-positive increase on genuine plan-failure controls (non-inferiority margin: 3 percentage points).
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted if the license yields no significant ≥5pp misattribution reduction for a majority of judge variants, or if any reduction is bought with a false-positive increase exceeding the 3-point margin. (A significant *increase* in misattribution under the license is governed by wrong-direction rule (ii) in the global fallbacks below, not by this refutation clause.)
- **Metrics:** δ_A (paired misattribution-rate reduction) per judge variant; FPR_A on plan-failure controls. **Descriptive mechanism readout (pre-registered, no hypothesis attached — added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` "missing RQs" item 3):** from each judge rationale, log whether the corrupted observation was flagged as suspicious yet blame was still placed elsewhere — separating "never noticed" (C1) from "noticed but overrode," a decomposition that sharpens the C2-vs-C3 mechanism story beyond the single trust-license bit, at no extra run cost.

## RQ4 — Is any trust-license reduction partial rather than complete?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_evidential_circularity`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_blinded_trust_probe_harness` hyp. 2 / Stage 6 `rq_trust_license_partial`):** "The reduction from the trust license is partial, not complete, leaving a majority of the original misattribution rate intact, because knowing observations may lie does not by itself reveal which observation lies."
- **Setup:** Same paired runs as RQ3; compare the post-license misattribution rate against the same method's misattribution rate on matched overt-error controls.
- **Sample-size rationale:** Same as RQ3 — no separate sample; read off the same paired runs' post-license rate versus the overt-error-control rate already computed for RQ5.
- **Statistical test:** Comparison of post-license misattribution rate against the matched overt-error-control rate (the "majority of the Δ-trust gap remains" threshold is a direct read of the same δ_A/Δ_A quantities computed for RQ3/RQ5; `paper.md`/`pipeline.md` do not define a separate significance test beyond this point comparison).
- **Expected outcome:** The license reduces but does not eliminate misattribution — post-license misattribution rate remains above the method's overt-error-control rate for a majority of judge variants, leaving a majority (target: above 50%) of the original Δ-trust gap intact.
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted toward "fully fixed" if post-license misattribution rate is statistically indistinguishable from the overt-error-control rate for a majority of judge variants; refuted toward "no effect at all" if this coincides with RQ3's own falsification (no significant reduction). A result landing strictly between these is the only outcome consistent with the partial-repair hypothesis as stated.
- **Metrics:** Proportion of the original Δ-trust gap remaining after the license, per judge variant.

## RQ5 — Is the misattribution bias specific to the tool-observation channel? (Δ-trust gap)

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_counterfactual_entanglement`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_matched_control_design` hyp. 1 / Stage 6 `rq_delta_trust_gap`):** "Misattribution rate on the semantic-corruption treatment set exceeds misattribution rate on the matched overt-error control by a large, method-general margin (the Δ-trust gap), while misattribution on the matched agent-side-fault control (same wrong value, different locus) stays low — showing the effect is specific to the tool-observation channel rather than to the general difficulty of spotting any subtly wrong value."
- **As quantified in `paper.md`'s pre-registered spec (H1, E1):** "For ≥3 of the 5 non-human baselines..., Δ_A ≥ +10 percentage points AND the paired test rejects at Holm-corrected p < .05. Additionally m_A(D_overt) ≤ 40% for those methods (they must be competent on overt errors, else the gap is uninterpretable)."
- **Setup:** Pilot: one single-agent tool-calling framework (AgentDojo), 2 backbone judge families, 100 verified trajectories per arm (semantic-corruption, overt-error, agent-side-fault). Scale-up: 3 frameworks (AgentDojo, GAIA, Who&When-logged, subject to the walking skeleton's G6 framework-narrowing gate), ≥300 verified trajectories per arm per framework. **Judge-capability axis (added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` "missing RQs" item 2):** the scale-up additionally crosses one judge capability tier — a weak and a frontier judge within one model family, on one framework — pre-registering the check that the Δ-trust gap does not vanish at the frontier tier (ruling out the "weak-judge artifact that the next model generation fixes" rival).
- **Sample-size rationale:** n ≥ 300 verified corrupted trajectories per framework per arm — digest N8: ~80% power for a 10 percentage-point gap at α=.05 given an assumed ~30% baseline misattribution rate.
- **Statistical test:** McNemar for task-matched treatment/control pairs, two-proportion z otherwise; Holm–Bonferroni correction across the 5 attribution methods; cluster-robust errors by task template. **Suffix-length stratification (added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` F-6):** an overt error typically truncates the trajectory at the fault step while a schema-valid corruption lets the agent run on, so fault-to-manifestation distance is a named confound of the headline contrast; Δ_A is therefore additionally reported stratified by post-fault suffix length (fault-to-manifestation lag), with the pre-committed interpretation that if Δ_A collapses within suffix-length strata, the headline gap is attributed to fault-to-manifestation distance rather than to the observation channel, and the channel-specificity claim rests solely on the agent-side-fault (B-alt) contrast.
- **Aggregation rule (pre-committed as a fraction, added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` failure-space walk):** the "≥3 of 5" majority criterion is registered as *a strict majority of the surviving baseline denominator* — if an A5 smoke-test failure drops a baseline (denominator 4: ≥3; denominator 3: ≥2), the rule stays defined without post-hoc choice.
- **Expected outcome:** The Δ-trust gap is at least 10 percentage points and significantly positive (Holm-corrected p < .05) for a strict majority (≥3 of 5) of methods — conditional on those methods clearing the overt-error competence bar (m_A(D_overt) ≤ 40%) — while misattribution on the agent-side-fault arm stays within a few points of the overt-error control.
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted if the Δ-trust gap is statistically indistinguishable from zero (or negative) for the majority of methods. Refuted in the channel-specificity sub-claim if the agent-side-fault arm's misattribution rate is comparable to or exceeds the tool-observation arm's. (A *significantly negative* Δ is governed by wrong-direction rule (i) in the global fallbacks below — reported at full prominence, not folded into the null wording.)
- **Metrics:** Δ-trust gap (Δ_A) per method, overall and per suffix-length stratum; agent-side-fault-arm misattribution rate relative to the overt-error control.

## RQ6 — Does misattribution skew toward the plan layer, and does the skew depend on corruption type?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_counterfactual_entanglement`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_matched_control_design` hyp. 2, added at Stage 5's critic round / Stage 6 `rq_direction_skew`):** "Among misattributed cases, blame skews toward the plan layer rather than the execution layer (exact binomial test against 0.5), and the skew strengthens for subtle, late-trajectory, numeric/stale-value corruptions relative to overt, early, boolean/structural ones (a corruption-type/locus/subtlety factor is a significant predictor of blame direction in a mixed-effects logistic model)."
- **Setup:** Among RQ5's misattributed cases, cross-tabulate blamed layer (plan vs. execution) by operator family, injection locus tercile (early/mid/late), and subtlety, on AgentDojo and GAIA.
- **Sample-size rationale (power-corrected — this is where `paper.md`'s `design_review.md` pass fixed a Stage-6-era gap):** the originally drafted ≥50-case minimum gives only ≈0.51 power for the pre-registered criterion (exact binomial, two-sided α=.05, true π_plan=0.65) — a coin flip against a real skew. At n=120 the same test has ≈0.89 power (critical count 72/120). Pre-declared bands: n ≥ 120 misattributed cases → H2 scored; 50 ≤ n < 120 → INCONCLUSIVE (π_plan reported with CI, no PASS/FAIL verdict); n < 50 → descriptive only, no test. Each M5 grid cell is pre-filled to a target of ≥15 verified trajectories.
- **Statistical test:** Exact binomial per method (Holm-corrected across methods) for the direction claim; mixed-effects logistic regression (fixed effects = the three grid factors, random intercepts = task and framework) for the type-dependence sub-claim (likelihood-ratio test, p < .05). Post-fault suffix length is additionally included as a fixed-effect covariate in the mixed model (added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` F-6) — a covariate control, not part of the verbatim H2/H2b criteria.
- **Expected outcome:** Pooled plan-share among misattributed cases is significantly above 0.5 (target: at least 0.65), and at least one grid factor is a significant predictor of blame direction, with the skew toward plan strengthening for subtle, late, numeric/stale-value corruptions.
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted on direction if plan-share does not differ significantly from 0.5, or skews toward execution instead. Refuted on type-dependence if no grid factor is a significant predictor in the mixed-effects model — either result would mean misattribution is directionally diffuse rather than channeled toward one layer, a distinct but still-reportable failure geometry.
- **Metrics:** Plan-share (π_plan) and its confidence interval; mixed-effects model coefficients for the three grid factors.

## RQ7 — Is the plan/tool/execution mapping rubric reliable?

- **Tier:** mechanism
- **Target sub-problem:** `sp_layer_ontology_mismatch`
- **Target hypothesis (verbatim, Stage 5 `m_prereg_mapping_rubric` / Stage 6 `rq_mapping_reliability`):** "The pre-registered mapping rubric achieves Cohen's κ ≥ 0.7 between two independent annotators across all five attribution methods' outputs, and excluding rubric-disputed trajectories from the main analysis does not change the direction (though it may change the magnitude) of the headline misattribution-rate comparison."
- **Setup:** Dual annotation of a held-out sample (target: 50 outputs per method across all 5 methods, 250 total) applying the pre-registered rubric R_A — written before any experimental result is examined — converting each method's native output (agent name, free-text rationale, discrete flag) into {plan, tool, execution}. Sensitivity re-analysis of the RQ5/RQ6 headline comparisons excluding rubric-disputed trajectories.
- **Sample-size rationale:** 50 outputs/method × 5 methods = 250, sized to support a stable per-method κ estimate; ties to assumption A3's pilot dual-annotation of 50 outputs per method before unblinding.
- **Statistical test:** Cohen's κ (per method and pooled) against a ≥0.7 acceptance threshold; sign/significance stability check on the RQ5/RQ6 headline comparisons under the disputed-trajectory exclusion.
- **Expected outcome:** Cohen's κ is at least 0.7, pooled and per method, and the sign and significance of the RQ5/RQ6 headline comparisons are unchanged when disputed trajectories are excluded (magnitude may shift).
- **Falsification condition:** Refuted if κ falls below 0.7 for one or more methods, or if excluding disputed trajectories flips the sign or significance of the RQ5/RQ6 headline comparisons — either would mean the mapping rubric itself, not the attribution methods' behavior, is a material driver of the paper's central findings.
- **Metrics:** Cohen's κ per method and pooled; headline-result stability under the disputed-trajectory exclusion.

## H4 — Descriptive arm: are human annotators fooled too? (no tier, no pass/fail bar — added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` F-8)

- **Status:** pre-registered descriptive arm, not a mechanism-tier RQ — it targets no Stage 5 hypothesis and carries no PASS/FAIL bar; it is registered here so that neither direction of the human result can be cherry-picked post hoc.
- **Setup:** the human-annotator reference already specified in `digest.md` M3 — a stratified 10% subsample of the verified semantic-corruption treatment set (stratified over the M5 grid), annotated by human raters blind to injection metadata, applying the same pre-registered plan/tool/execution ontology used for the automated methods.
- **Readout:** human misattribution rate on the D_sem subsample with a 95% CI, reported alongside (a) the best automated method's rate on the same subsample and (b) the matched overt-error-control rate.
- **Pre-committed wording, both directions:** (i) if humans are also fooled (human misattribution rate comparable to the automated methods'), the implication is reported as a scope statement: human-annotated attribution ground truths (Who&When's labels; MAST's taxonomy, per digest N4 item 7) are themselves exposed to the corruption channel this paper measures — stated as measured contamination exposure, not extrapolated beyond the sampled strata; (ii) if humans are not fooled (human rate close to the overt-error floor), human performance is reported as the repair upper bound against which the trust-license reduction (RQ3/RQ4) is compared.

## Global fallbacks and combination rules (governs how RQ verdicts compose)

- **F0 (premise-collapse):** fires if m_A(D_overt) > 40% for ≥3 of 5 methods (RQ5's competence precondition fails). F0 pre-empts F1/F-G1 wholesale; RQ5/RQ6 are then reported descriptively only, with pre-declared INCONCLUSIVE verdicts (both condition on an interpretable Δ).
- **F1 / F-G1 (robust-and-competent cell):** applies only if F0 did not fire and RQ5's Δ-trust gap is null — the archetype pivots to a benchmark/resource finding, per `paper.md`'s pre-committed pivoted abstract sentence. Per `design_review.md`'s failure-space walk (noted 2026-07-05): the pivoted benchmark paper additionally pre-declares the A3-archetype evidence add-ons it would owe — a contamination analysis, headroom against the constant-attributor floor, and a maintenance statement for the released suite.
- **Wrong-direction rules (added 2026-07-05 per `design_review.md` F-5(3)/(4)):**
  - **(i) Δ significantly negative** (methods *better* on semantic corruption than on overt errors, for a majority of methods): reported at full prominence as a wrong-direction finding in its own right — not folded into F1's null wording, which covers only "Δ within ±10 pp, n.s." — since it would contradict the trust-asymmetry mechanism in the opposite direction and demands its own explanation before any pivot.
  - **(ii) Trust license significantly *increases* misattribution on D_sem** (for one or more judge variants): reported at full prominence as a wrong-direction result — the license actively harms attribution — not under F3's null wording ("does not repair," n.s.); it blocks the removable-trust-default (C2) reading outright for the affected variants.
- **F2 / F2b (RQ6 outcome cells):** F2 covers a null direction result (misattribution directionally diffuse); F2b covers a null type-dependence result (no M5 factor significant). F2 and F2b are independent — any of the four H2×H2b outcome cells maps to a main/F2 wording for direction crossed with a main/F2b wording for type-dependence.
- **F3 (RQ3/RQ4 mechanism cell):** if the trust license does not repair attribution, the blind spot is reported as a counterfactual-reasoning deficit (C3) rather than a removable trust assumption; if instead reduction succeeds but FPR inflates beyond the 3pp margin, the finding is reported as "trust is a dial, not a switch."

Every RQ above resolves to a Step-5-table quantity or an H-id in `paper.md`; no RQ depends on an unformalized adjective (per `paper.md`'s Claim–Evidence Map).
