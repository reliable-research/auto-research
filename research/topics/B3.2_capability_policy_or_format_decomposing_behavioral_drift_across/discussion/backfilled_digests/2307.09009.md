## N1 Problem Formulation (extracted)
- Evaluates the March 2023 and June 2023 snapshots of both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 on seven task categories:
  math problems, sensitive/dangerous questions, opinion surveys, multi-hop knowledge-intensive questions,
  code generation, US Medical License exam questions, and visual reasoning.
- Measures per-task accuracy/behavior deltas between the two snapshots for each model.

## N2 Motivation (extracted, verbatim abstract)
"GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are the two most widely used large language model (LLM) services. However, when and
how these models are updated over time is opaque. ... Overall, our findings show that the behavior of
the "same" LLM service can change substantially in a relatively short amount of time, highlighting the
need for continuous monitoring of LLMs."

## N9 Results (extracted, verbatim abstract highlights)
- GPT-4 (March 2023) identified prime vs. composite numbers at 84% accuracy; GPT-4 (June 2023) dropped to
  51% -- partly explained by reduced chain-of-thought amenability.
- GPT-3.5 improved on the same prime/composite task from March to June (opposite direction from GPT-4).
- GPT-4 became less willing to answer sensitive/opinion-survey questions in June than March (policy-axis
  tightening, in this paper's terms).
- GPT-4 improved on multi-hop questions June vs. March; GPT-3.5 dropped on the same task.
- **Load-bearing finding for B3.2's motivation**: "Both GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 had more formatting mistakes in
  code generation in June than in March" -- the paper attributes part of the code-generation regression to
  formatting mistakes (consistent with B3.2 digest N2/N4's characterization of this as a documented
  format-drift-scored-as-capability-loss incident), alongside a broader claim that decreased
  instruction-following is "one common factor behind the many behavior drifts."
- The paper reports one blended per-task delta per model per period; it does not decompose any observed
  delta into capability/policy/format components, and audits only OpenAI's models (single vendor).
