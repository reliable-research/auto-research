## Stage 1 — Problem + Motivation

**STAGE 1 VERDICT: PASS**

**Why:** The problem clears the "so what" test before any method appears — the Introduction's premise paragraph states the stakes in first-principles terms: "a visible test suite is only ever a proxy for correctness, and proxies can be gamed once something optimizes against them," then grounds the pain concretely via a real, computable quantity ("mutation-kill score, the fraction of systematically injected, behavior-changing bugs a suite catches... The gap is stated as still-open with corpus-search backing (pipeline.md Stage 2: "a targeted corpus search across three independent keyword angles ... found no paper that treats this adequacy score as a controlled independent variable"), and feasibility is pre-registered via a minimum-viable pilot (RQ1/RQ2 on 10 instances, and paper.md's walking-skeleton G-S1–G-S4 gates) rather than an untested leap.

## Stage 2 — Related Work

**STAGE 2 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the draft:** The four thematic threads are genuinely argued, not a citation dump (each ends on a problem-tied limitation, e.g. the adversarial-testgen thread: "none of it holds a suite fixed at a controlled, degraded adequacy level in order to diagnose whether an already-capable agent exploits its gaps"). But the first thread flattens two structurally different prior works into one undifferentiated pair: "Work on inference-scaling limits under resampling shows that accepting the first sample to clear a fixed, weak unit-test selector converges not to perfect correctness but to a hard ceiling... and a companion analysis characterizes exactly how a verifier's full ROC curve... reshapes what best-of-N rejection sampling accepts. Both establish that an imperfect verifier systematically admits incorrect-but-passing outputs..." The digest's own grounding note (N3, entry 1) already singles out the resampling-limits work specifically as **"The closest corpus analog to this idea's visible-vs-hidden gap"** — precisely because it already contrasts a weak visible selector against stronger hidden tests, the same visible/hidden structure this paper's own $T_v$/$T_i^{hid}$ setup uses — a structural mirror the ROC-curve companion paper does not share (it is a pure noisy-verifier characterization with no visible/hidden distinction at all). That asymmetry never made it into the assembled Related Work prose, which treats the pair as interchangeable ("Both establish..."). Per the Stage 2 bar: "hiding the method-twin is the fastest way to a desk reject... reviewers find it anyway; confront it first."

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit nearest-neighbor sentence naming the resampling-limits work as the single closest prior study — crediting its shared visible/hidden structure precisely, and stating the delta (static generator, no adequacy sweep) — rather than letting it and its ROC-curve companion read as an undifferentiated "both."

## Stage 4 — "Experiments" (Analysis/Finding variant: falsification, not correlation)

**STAGE 4 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the draft:** The one question this stage asks in every archetype — is the claimed cause isolated from its strongest alternative — is well-served structurally: RQ8 is a genuine falsification design (a budget-matched, feedback-free selection-only arm that must be *beaten* by the iterative arm's slope before "agent-side gaming" can be claimed at all, not merely correlated with it). But the first assembled draft's eight RQ subsections all stopped at "**Expected outcome.** ..." without ever stating the converse — what observation would *refute* that expectation. E.g. RQ3's subsection reads "The overfitting gap is at least 10 percentage points larger at the lowest tested adequacy level than at full adequacy... " with no paired statement of what a null or contradicting result would look like or how it would be reported. Per the Analysis/Finding bar: "the strongest finding papers construct a case where, if the claim were false, the result would differ... demand at least one such test" stated as a check, not implied. The underlying `paper.md` material already had exactly this discipline and it simply didn't survive into the assembled `paper_draft.md`: Step 6's Fallback clauses are precise and pre-committed — e.g. E1's "If H1 fails via (i)/(ii) (flat curve): the paper's claim becomes: 'coding agents' held-out correctness is insensitive to visible-suite adequacy... visible-test overfitting is not an operative failure mode'" — but none of these eight Fallback clauses (E1–E4, B-alt, plus the A1/A2 pilot gates) appear anywhere in `paper_draft.md`'s Experiments section before this review.

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit **Falsification condition** line to all eight RQ subsections, stating the specific observation that would refute each expected outcome — pulling directly from `paper.md`'s pre-existing Step 6 Fallback clauses (H1–H4, B-alt) and Assumptions-table failure modes (A1/A2 for RQ1/RQ2), not inventing new statistical machinery.
