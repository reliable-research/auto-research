# Which Trajectories Deserve Verification? Selective Checking of Sampled Candidates under a Scarce Trusted-Verifier Budget

## Abstract

Test-time scaling samples many candidate trajectories per task instance, and reliable selection among them increasingly depends on trusted verification — executable tests, ground-truth graders, or strong judges — whose per-call cost permits checking only a small fraction of candidates. Existing work meters the wrong side of this pipeline: generation-budget allocation, verifier-imperfection theory, and per-item escalation all assume verification is either free, noisy-but-total, or budgeted per task rather than pooled. We formalize the complementary problem: given a frozen pool of N sampled trajectories for each of M instances, grouped into answer-equivalence classes, and a single global budget of B ≪ N·M trusted verifier calls shared across all instances, choose which classes to verify and how to combine verified outcomes (including eliminations from failed checks) with unverified cheap signals into one forced answer per instance. We contribute (i) a diagnosis of whether verifier-free signals — mode share, sequence log-likelihood, verbalized confidence — can aim the budget at all, measuring per-signal discriminative power and the mass of confidently wrong consensus that upper-bounds any signal-ranked policy; (ii) a tiered family of allocation policies, from static signal ranking at two granularities through ε-insurance mixtures and sequential greedy reallocation up to a value-based policy with cross-instance shadow pricing and a consensus-probe reserve, each deployed only when its preregistered data floors are met; (iii) an elimination-aware, dependency-capped aggregation rule that remains total under partial evidence; and (iv) hindsight-optimal static and adaptive ceilings that decompose the achievable headroom. Evaluation replays all policies over identical frozen pools (two math datasets × two open 7–8B models, N=16, ~64K generations, with a droppable code-execution slice), sweeping budget fractions 5–100% against uniform-allocation and full-verification anchors, with preregistered gates and a pre-committed negative-result branch. All pools, signals, labels, and the replay harness are released.

## 1. Introduction

Modern reasoning and code-generation pipelines improve accuracy by sampling multiple candidate solutions and selecting among them. Selection is most reliable when a trusted verifier — unit-test execution, a ground-truth grader, or a strong judge whose verdicts are accepted — checks candidates directly. But verification is becoming the metered resource: sandbox execution, process-reward scoring of all N candidates, and judge review are linear-in-N costs, and the judge is often a larger model than the generator. A deployer holding a sandbox quota faces a question the literature does not answer: *of the N·M trajectories already sampled across a batch of M instances, which B ≪ N·M deserve the verifier calls?*

Prior work budgets adjacent resources. Generation-side allocation decides how many samples to draw or how much compute to spend per input (Snell et al., 2024; Damani et al., 2024; Aggarwal et al., 2023). Verifier-imperfection theory analyzes noisy verifiers that score *every* candidate — trust, not scarcity, is the variable. Per-item escalation and cascade frameworks, including Anytime Verified Agents (TMLR 2026), allocate verification within a single task under per-task budgets, without cross-instance coupling. Cost-sensitive search such as Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify (Dughmi et al., 2026) minimizes per-prompt cost to the first verified-positive candidate under a monotonicity assumption linking cheap scores to correctness. Budgeted instance-level verification with confidence signals has been studied most directly by the heteroskedastic-signals analysis of Zhang et al. (arXiv:2606.15841, 2026), which is a cautionary result: heterogeneity in signal quality structurally limits the gains of uncertainty-ranked checking over simple baselines. None of these addresses the combination that defines our setting: a *globally coupled* budget over *competing trajectories for the same instances*, where one call resolves an answer-equivalence class, failed calls yield elimination evidence that aggregation must exploit, and a final answer is forced for every instance even when the budget expires without a pass.

The central empirical tension is stated up front rather than assumed away: the highest-value calls sit exactly where cheap signals say "confident" — consensus-wrong instances on which all N samples agree on an incorrect answer are invisible to uncertainty ranking and fatal to majority voting. Whether verifier-free signals can aim a scarce budget despite this adversarially structured proxy gap is not knowable a priori; it is the question our budget–accuracy curves settle, with a preregistered falsification condition under which "cheap signals cannot beat uniform allocation" is itself the reported finding.

**Contributions.**
1. **Problem formalization.** Selective verification over a frozen trajectory pool: answer-class-level calls, a globally coupled budget, elimination-aware forced aggregation, and evaluation by budget–accuracy curves normalized between uniform-allocation and full-verification anchors, with hindsight-optimal static and adaptive ceilings.
2. **Signal diagnosis before policy design.** Per-signal discriminative power (AUROC with confidence intervals) against verifier labels and, decisively, the measured mass of verified-incorrect high-consensus classes — the quantity that upper-bounds every signal-ranked static policy.
3. **A tiered, floor-gated policy family.** Static ranking at trajectory-joint and instance→answer-class granularities; difficulty-conditioned and threshold-escalation baselines adapted from the generation-budget literature; an ε-insurance mixture that reserves budget for confident consensus; sequential greedy reallocation; and, at the top tier, a value-based allocator with dependency-capped actionability, cross-instance shadow pricing, and a wrongness-oriented consensus-probe reserve — deployed only when preregistered labeled-data floors are met, with a declared fallback ladder otherwise.
4. **Elimination-aware total aggregation.** A dependency-capped minimax-support-regret rule that outputs an answer after *any* verification history, giving passes lexical priority, removing refuted classes with their cheap support, and remaining robust to dropping any single signal family.
5. **A released resource.** Frozen pools (answers, signals, labels, costs) and the allocation-replay harness, enabling policy research without regeneration.

## 2. Related Work

**Sampling and test-time scaling (different resource).** Self-consistency selects the most frequent sampled answer without verification (Wang et al., ICLR 2023) and supplies both our zero-budget anchor and our cheapest signal. Repeated-sampling studies characterize coverage scaling when verification is abundant or approximated (Brown et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025). Compute-optimal test-time scaling (Snell et al., 2024) and calibrated adaptive sampling (Aggarwal et al., 2023) allocate *generation*; Damani et al. (2024) allocate compute by predicted marginal reward. None meters verification of an already-sampled pool.

**Learned and generated verification (different trust model).** Outcome- and process-supervised verifiers rank candidates (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2023); CodeT generates test cases to score code candidates (Chen et al., 2022). These construct verification signals; we allocate calls to a verifier whose verdicts are already trusted. Verifier-imperfection theory (ROC-style accuracy–compute analyses) treats trust as the variable and call count as an outcome; our setting is the dual — perfect verdicts, hard call ceiling — and our verifier-noise sweep is the designed bridge between the regimes.

**Budgeted and selective verification (nearest neighbors).** Three 2026 works occupy adjacent cells and sharpen our delta. (a) The heteroskedastic-signals analysis of budgeted LLM verification (arXiv:2606.15841) allocates a fixed budget of checks across instances by confidence signals and proves that signal-quality heterogeneity limits achievable gains; it operates at instance granularity, one check per instance, without answer classes, eliminations, or sequential reallocation — its negative result is precisely why our diagnosis-first design and negative-result branch exist. (b) Anytime Verified Agents (TMLR 2026) combines uncertainty estimation with selective verification cascades, but under *per-task* budgets: no global coupling, so "second class of a hard instance vs. first check of the next instance" — our core trade — does not arise. (c) Adaptive Generate-Rank-Verify (Dughmi et al., arXiv:2605.17609) is per-prompt first-positive search with costly verification under a monotone score–correctness assumption; our diagnosis measures exactly the consensus-wrong mass that violates such monotonicity, and our aggregation must decide even when no positive is found. Best-of-Evidence (Zhang et al., arXiv:2607.20950, vision-language) allocates partial-evidence queries *within* one candidate pool via candidate–factor graphs; our budget couples decisions *across* instances. General trajectory-verification frameworks (LLM-as-a-Verifier, arXiv:2607.05391) supply verifiers; they do not ration them.

**Formal ancestors (different object).** Active testing and label-efficient evaluation allocate scarce ground-truth labels by informativeness (Kossen et al., 2021); fixed-budget best-arm identification allocates pulls to identify one best arm. Our delta is the deployment object: a call resolves an answer-equivalence class inside a structured pool; payoff is batch accuracy after aggregation; eliminations and cross-instance coupling have no analogue in i.i.d. active testing. FrugalGPT (Chen et al., 2023) routes among models by price; Trust-or-Escalate-style per-item escalation ignores budget coupling and is included as a baseline.

**Boundaries within the topic family.** The adversarial sibling (B1.5) lets the generator optimize against the checker; we hold the generator non-strategic, and our frontier upper-bounds any allocation once an adversary is added — the non-adversarial case must be solved first. Stopping rules meter *thinking* (the generation budget); we meter *checking* after generation, and the two compose in a pipeline without shared machinery. System-level serving economics ($/resolved-task across a serving stack) is likewise out of scope: our estimand is trajectory selection — which classes to verify and with what precision/recall under budget — not infrastructure throughput.

**Hidden-twin contingency (pre-committed).** If a direct prior on coupled-budget selective verification of trajectory pools emerges before submission, we reposition on the three components a first paper is unlikely to have jointly — elimination-aware aggregation as a measured axis, sequential reallocation against hindsight ceilings, and math↔code transfer — and cite it as the anchor rather than competing on priority. ## 3. Problem Definition

Let 𝕀 = {1,…,M} be a frozen set of instances. Instance i has N sampled trajectories partitioned into answer-equivalence classes 𝒞ᵢ = {c_{i1},…,c_{iK_i}}, K_i ≤ N (exact-match classes for math; frozen probe-input output-equivalence classes for code, with the probe suite fixed before any verification). Cheap evidence x_{ik} for class c_{ik} comprises its mode share m_{ik} = (1/N)·Σⱼ 1[τ_{ij} ∈ c_{ik}], the within-instance percentiles of sequence log-likelihood, and verbalized confidence of its member trajectories; all signals are computed and frozen before any verifier call. Let y_{ik} ∈ {0,1} be the unknown correctness of class c_{ik}.

A trusted verifier implements V(i,k) = y_{ik}: one call resolves an entire class — a positive identifies a correct class; a negative *eliminates* it, which is information, not waste. A sequential policy π observes the frozen evidence X and the history H_t of past calls and either selects an unqueried (i,k) or stops, subject to the single global constraint |S_π| ≤ B with B ≪ Σᵢ K_i. No instance holds an independent allowance: every call displaces a potential call on every other instance. After allocation, a total aggregation rule δ outputs one class per instance from the mixed evidence — verified passes, eliminations, and unverified cheap signals — even if no call passed or every class was eliminated.

The primary object is the budget–accuracy curve A(B) = (1/M)·Σᵢ y_{i,δ(i)}, reported with the derived summaries: budget-recovery fraction (A(B) − A(0)) / (A_full − A(0)) where A(0) is the self-consistency anchor and A_full = (1/M)·Σᵢ maxₖ y_{ik} the full-verification ceiling; area under the budget curve; and accuracy-per-call as a secondary ratio (the raw ratio is non-monotone and undefined at B = 0, so it never serves as the primary metric). Two hindsight ceilings bound policies: OPT-static, the best fixed query set of size B in hindsight, and OPT-adaptive, the best sequential spend in hindsight; their gap measures the value of adaptivity itself. Budgets sweep b = B/(N·M) ∈ {5, 10, 25, 50, 100}% per dataset–model pair, with b ∈ {5, 10}% as the primary scarce regime.

**Why this is hard.** The proxies' errors are structured against the policy: high mode share, likelihood, or confidence need not imply correctness, and the consensus-wrong instances that verification could rescue are exactly those the signals mark "certain — skip me." Call value is history-dependent (a pass resolves an instance; an elimination reshapes the surviving candidate set), so allocation cannot decompose per instance, and aggregation cannot reduce to picking the highest-scoring unverified class.

### 4.1 Signal Extraction and Dependency Families

Three class-level scores are formed per instance — mode share, mean within-instance log-likelihood percentile, mean verbalized-confidence percentile — each rank-normalized across the instance's classes, plus derived instance features (answer-class entropy, top-2 vote margin, class count). On a development split, absolute Spearman correlations over instance–class observations are clustered by complete linkage at threshold 0.8 into **dependency families**, frozen before evaluation. A family votes once — the mean of its members' rank-normalized votes — so duplicating a correlated signal cannot manufacture corroboration. Verbalized confidence is the only signal costing extra generation (one short elicitation per trajectory); it survives past the pilot only if its dry-run AUROC lower confidence bound exceeds 0.55.

**Diagnosis deliverable (Experiment 1, gating everything downstream):** per-signal AUROC against verifier-labeled class correctness with DeLong intervals, and the **consensus-wrong mass table** — the fraction of instances whose modal class is verified incorrect at mode share above each threshold. This mass upper-bounds the recovery of every signal-ranked static policy and decides whether the ε-insurance and consensus-probe mechanisms are load-bearing or idle.

### 4.2 A Tiered Family of Allocation Policies

Policies are ordered by the data they require; each tier deploys only when its preregistered requirements hold, and the deployed tier is declared, not silently chosen.

**Tier 0 — anchors.** Uniform-random allocation at matched B (the control every claim is measured against); no verification (self-consistency); full verification.

**Tier 1 — static signal ranking (label-free).** Most-uncertain-first at two granularities: *trajectory-joint* (rank all N·M candidates) and *instance→answer-class* (rank instances, then verify the largest unresolved class first — most information per call, since near-duplicate checks are dominated). Two adapted baselines expose what neighboring formulations lose: difficulty-conditioned instance ranking (generation-allocation style, instance-level only) and per-item confidence-threshold escalation (no budget coupling). The instance-vs-trajectory contrast is a headline comparison: do trajectory-level signals matter beyond instance difficulty?

**Tier 1.5 — ε-insurance mixture.** Spend (1−ε)·B most-uncertain-first and ε·B uniformly over high-consensus instances, ε ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.25}. This converts the consensus-wrong diagnosis into design: if the diagnosed mass is significant, pure uncertainty ranking is provably leaving recovery on the table, and the optimal ε shifts away from zero.

**Tier 2 — sequential greedy reallocation.** Spend one call on the current most-valuable target, observe, mark instances resolved on a pass, re-rank survivors (an eliminated class releases its support and can promote a sibling class or another instance). Scored against both OPT ceilings; the OPT-adaptive − OPT-static gap says how much sequentiality is worth before any learned machinery is added.

**Tier 3 — value-based allocation with shadow pricing (floor-gated).** Where development data suffice, calls are valued by their expected effect on final accuracy rather than raw signal order. (i) **Actionability:** for each unverified class, both verifier outcomes are simulated on the ledger and the aggregator is re-run in the full-family and each leave-one-family-out view; the flip fraction and mean support-gap shift across views yield a dependency-capped decision-sensitivity score — correlated families agreeing is not counted as independent evidence. (ii) **Value models:** cross-fitted class-correctness and elimination-probability models are trained *only* on verifier outcomes charged to the same budget (B = B_dev + B_eval, with B_dev ≤ 0.4·B so evaluation retains ≥ 0.6·B), with instance-level folds and conservative lower-confidence endpoints; a one-step robust gain scores each candidate call. (iii) **Shadow price:** at each replan, the opening call to an unserved instance must clear λ = the gain of the marginally affordable unserved instance, making cross-instance opportunity cost explicit; a priority guard permits repeat calls to a served instance only when no unserved instance offers greater price-cleared value density; allocation stops early when no call has positive robust gain. (iv) **Consensus-probe reserve:** ε_probe = 0.10 of the evaluation budget probes classes on which all families agree with low actionability and high single-family support, ranked by runner-up disagreement and a development-derived calibration-gap prior — targeting exactly the confidently wrong consensus invisible to value ranking.

**Fallback ladder (preregistered, resolves the sizing risk).** Tier 3 requires ≥ 80 labeled development states over ≥ 30 distinct instances. At the smallest budgets this floor may fail — at b = 5% of a 16K-slot pair the planning arithmetic leaves roughly 100 states over ~34 instances, i.e., no slack. The ladder is therefore declared in advance: floors met → Tier 3 is the deployed policy; floors unmet → Tier 1.5/2 (actionability-ranked, depth-1, consensus reserve intact) *is the headline policy at that budget*, reported as such — not as a degraded footnote — with the floor-trigger budget disclosed. The paper's claims attach to the deployed ladder, so every budget has a defined, evaluable headline.

### 4.3 Elimination-Aware Total Aggregation

Each class is unresolved, passed, or eliminated. If any class passed, passes form the eligible set; otherwise all non-eliminated classes are eligible — an eliminated class is removed *with its cheap support*, so failed calls permanently reshape the vote. Over the full-family view and each nonempty leave-one-family-out view, class support is the mean of included family votes; the selected class minimizes worst-view support regret (ties: full-view support, then canonical identifier). The rule is total: it decides after any history, including all-eliminated instances (last-eliminated class, the weakest refutation) and the one-family case (single capped vote). The naive aggregator (verified-correct if any, else raw majority) is ablated at matched B across all policies, isolating the value of eliminations (discard-failures ablation) and of family caps (independent-three-votes ablation).

### 4.4 Verifier Realism and Cost Accounting

The pilot verifier is oracle lookup — trusted and free to execute — isolating allocation quality from verifier quality; the circularity that the pilot verifier is also the evaluation oracle is architecturally contained (signals frozen pre-call; verifier outcomes touch only selection and aggregation) and then *broken* in the code slice, where the trusted verifier is held-out unit-test execution while evaluation uses the full test suite. Per-call costs (wall-clock, CPU) are measured, enabling accuracy-per-dollar for realistic verifiers; a verdict-flip noise sweep at rate ρ bridges toward the noisy-verifier regime studied by verifier-imperfection theory. Development spend, elicitation spend, and audit spend are always charged and reported against the same total budget.

## 5. Experimental Design

**Frozen pools.** Primary (frozen scope): GSM8K (fixed 500-instance subsample) and MATH-500 × Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, N = 16 — ~64K generations (~1–2 days, one A100/H100 with vLLM) plus gated confidence elicitations; oracle verification is label lookup. Droppable bonus slice: HumanEval+ (164) and MBPP+ (378) with held-out-test execution as the trusted verifier. Explicitly out of scope for this paper: N = 64 scaling, PRM-based noise sweeps beyond the synthetic ρ-sweep, and the full math↔code transfer matrix. Everything after generation is CPU replay on identical pools: sample once, allocate forever.

**Pilot gates (preregistered).**
- *G1 (week 1, 50-instance dry run):* keep only dataset–model pairs with base accuracy ∈ [30%, 70%] and mode-share IQR > 0.2; if all four pairs fail, walk the pre-named difficulty ladder {GSM8K, MATH-500 all, MATH-500 L3–5, MBPP+} and, if needed, a weaker model tier (3B-class) — a documented design choice, since the problem statement is model-agnostic. The hidden-twin literature sweep runs here and at T-minus-2-weeks.
- *G2 (pools complete):* best signal AUROC lower CI > 0.55 on kept pairs, and selective-vs-uniform separation at b = 25% powered ≥ 80%. If G2 fails with adequate power, the paper proceeds with the inverted claim — *cheap signals cannot aim a trusted-verifier budget better than uniform* — which contradicts the working assumption of every escalation pipeline and ships with identical artifacts.
- *G3:* analysis plan frozen (test families, primary configuration) before the full replay grid; 5 seeds × full policy grid on the primary configuration, 2 seeds elsewhere.

**Research questions.**
- **RQ1 (diagnosis).** What is each signal's AUROC against verifier labels, and how large is the consensus-wrong mass? (Decides everything downstream.)
- **RQ2 (recovery).** At b ∈ {5, 10}%, how much of the full-verification gap does the deployed ladder recover over uniform allocation at matched total spend? Positive: budget-recovery fraction ≥ 0.5 with uniform-margin lower bound ≥ 0.10 (paired bootstrap, per-pair direction consistency). The hypothesis attaches to the *deployed* tier at each budget — fallback included — eliminating the prior draft's fallback/hypothesis mismatch.
- **RQ3 (granularity & coupling).** Does instance→answer-class ranking beat trajectory-joint ranking, and both beat the uncoupled per-item threshold baseline?
- **RQ4 (trajectory vs. instance signals).** Does class-aware allocation beat difficulty-conditioned instance ranking at matched B?
- **RQ5 (adaptivity).** Sequential greedy vs. static at matched B; both against OPT-static and OPT-adaptive; report the OPT gap as the value of adaptivity.
- **RQ6 (insurance & consensus probing).** Does ε > 0 beat ε = 0 when the diagnosed consensus-wrong mass is nontrivial? Premise (always evaluable): probe ELIMINATE rate ≥ 0.10 by exact binomial on the audited reserve. Targeting margin (conditional): decorrelated targeting vs. equal-size random probing, paired within instance; where the multi-family/transferability preconditions fail, reported descriptively — the premise, not the margin, carries the blind-spot claim.
- **RQ7 (aggregation).** Elimination-aware vs. naive at matched calls; discard-failures and independent-votes ablations; no-pass-conditional comparison against self-consistency.
- **RQ8 (value machinery, conditional on floors).** Where Tier 3 deploys: its margin over the Tier 1.5/2 fallback, plus shadow-price (λ = 0) and lookahead (depth-1) ablations — attribution for the learned components, never a substitute for RQ2.

**Analysis.** Paired McNemar and 10K-resample paired bootstrap vs. uniform at each budget, Holm–Bonferroni within declared families; per-pair direction consistency required for confirmatory claims; underpowered conditional analyses reported as diagnostic, never confirmatory. All replay seeds fixed and logged.

**Artifact release (deliverable, not afterthought).** Frozen pools (answers, signals, verifier labels, costs), the replay harness, and all policy/aggregator configurations ship with the submission; every branch of the gate tree — positive, negative, or repositioned — releases the same resource.

## 6. Limitations

Single model scale (7–8B) pending the ladder outcome; oracle circularity fully broken only in the code slice; verbalized confidence may not survive its gate; the consensus-targeting margin may be evaluable only descriptively on a 4-pair pool; hindsight ceilings are replay-bound (they bound policies on these pools, not the population). The 2026 instance-level limits result (arXiv:2606.15841) predicts that signal heterogeneity may cap our gains; our design treats that outcome as a reportable branch rather than a threat.

## 7. Conclusion

Selective verification reframes test-time scaling around the resource that is scarce in deployment: trusted verdicts. We pose the coupled-budget, answer-class-level allocation problem; measure first whether cheap signals can aim the budget at all; field a floor-gated ladder of policies from uniform through shadow-priced value allocation; and force decisions through an elimination-aware total aggregator — all on frozen, released pools where every policy is a replay. Whatever the curves show — recovery, or a well-powered refusal — the result binds: either a small, well-aimed budget captures most of full verification, or the confidently wrong structure of cheap signals makes uniform checking unbeatable, and deployed escalation pipelines should know which world they are in.
