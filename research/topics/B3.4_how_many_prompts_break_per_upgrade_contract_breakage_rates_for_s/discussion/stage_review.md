## Stage 1 — Problem + Motivation

**STAGE 1 VERDICT: PASS**

**Why:** The problem clears the "so what" test before any method appears. The Introduction's second paragraph states the gap in first-principles terms — "A contract that validated cleanly against last month's model and a contract that fails against this month's model are not different objects — they are the same acceptance predicate applied to two different sources of output — but nothing in how these systems are built today reports what fraction of a real contract library crosses that line" — which attacks the root cause (an unmeasured downstream consequence of a known drift phenomenon) rather than justifying a method preview. The stakes are concrete, not a generic AI-importance appeal: "Teams currently find out reactively, from a production incident, rather than from a number they could check before adopting a new version." The Einstein ratio holds: the first two Introduction paragraphs are pure problem/stakes, with zero method leak — the word "validator" does not appear until paragraph 5.

## Stage 3 — "Method" (Analysis/Finding variant: the analytical apparatus)

**STAGE 3 VERDICT: PASS**

**Why:** Each construct is defined precisely *before* it is used — the contract triple (T, D, A), the pass/fail thresholds, the breakage rate B(v→v′), and the noise floor B_null are all given in Problem Definition ahead of any claim that invokes them (no "named after the fact" violation). The inferential chain is licensed at its one load-bearing hinge, stated explicitly rather than asserted by fiat: "applying the identical pass/fail procedure to two different partitions of the same output pool — once split by version, once split by resampling within a version — so that 'is this real breakage' becomes a checkable, apples-to-apples comparison instead of an assumption." That hinge is exactly what makes the noise-floor comparison non-circular, and the draft states it as the *governing move* of the whole design (Method Overview's opening sentence), not as one module among six. No orphan components — every one of the six modules (validator, harness, noise-floor estimator, fragility model, cheap-signal check, mitigation bound) traces to a named sub-problem in `pipeline.md`, with no leftover machinery; the cheap-signal check in particular is not decorative — it is the module that rules out the specific alternative explanation ("generic distribution shift") that the noise-floor comparison alone cannot address.

**Note for Stage 4:** the discharge test here isn't ablation (that's the method-paper bar) — it's whether the finding is falsification-tested, not merely correlational. Checked next.

## Stage 4 — "Experiments" (Analysis/Finding variant: falsification, not correlation)

**STAGE 4 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the draft:** The one question this stage asks in every archetype — is the claimed cause isolated from its strongest alternative — is well-served structurally: RQ5 is a genuine falsification design (a head-to-head AUC comparison against a cheap distribution-shift detector, not a correlation, to rule out the "this is just generic drift" alternative), and RQ3's noise-floor test is likewise a real falsification design (if B(v→v′) doesn't clear B_null, the central claim is refuted, not merely under-supported). But the first assembled draft's seven RQ subsections stopped at "**Expected outcome.** ... rather than being spread uniformly [or similar]" without ever stating the converse — what specific observation would *refute* each expected outcome. Per the Analysis/Finding bar: "the strongest finding papers construct a case where, if the claim were false, the result would differ... demand at least one such test," stated as an explicit, named check rather than left implicit in the expected-outcome wording. An "Expected outcome" alone reads as a hope; a stated falsification condition is what makes it a pre-registered, breakable claim — and the underlying `pipeline.md`/`paper.md` material already had this discipline (paper.md's own per-RQ "Fallback" clauses and N9's per-RQ "Falsification condition" entries) that had not yet survived into the first assembled `paper_draft.md`'s Experiments section.

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit **Falsification condition** line to all seven RQ subsections, stating the specific observation that would refute each expected outcome — not a restatement of the outcome's negation in vague terms, but the concrete statistical condition (mirroring the rigor already present in `paper.md`'s Step 6 "Fallback" clauses and digest N9's falsification conditions). For example, RQ3 now reads: "**Falsification condition.** Refuted if, across all tested transitions and contract types, B(v→v′) is statistically indistinguishable from B_null after correction — i.e., if apparent cross-version breakage is no larger than same-version resampling variance, the central claim that upgrades measurably break contracts beyond noise is false..."
