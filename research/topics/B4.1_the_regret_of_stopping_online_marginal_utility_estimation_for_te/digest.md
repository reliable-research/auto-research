## N5 Challenges

`conflation_check`: **true** (C1 = information-theoretic unobservability of the oracle, C2 = proxy-signal miscalibration, C3 = non-stationarity/non-monotonicity of the utility curve, C4 = evaluator's exchange-rate degree of freedom — four distinct obstacles).

- **challenges**:
  - `C1` `[HYPOTHESIS, tightly derived from the core question]`: "The oracle stopping point is defined over the *future* of the trajectory — whether continuing would ever raise (verifier-checked) expected accuracy enough to pay for its compute — so it is unobservable at decision time by construction; any online rule acts under genuine uncertainty about a counterfactual continuation, and this gap cannot be assumed away, only measured."
  - `C2` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "Every online signal available at a checkpoint (answer confidence, predictive entropy, self-consistency vote share) is a proxy for correctness that is known to decouple from it: models are systematically confidently-wrong on some instance classes, and parallel samples can spuriously agree on the same wrong answer, so a stopping rule built on these signals inherits their miscalibration in exactly the regime where stopping matters."
  - `C3` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "The marginal utility of one more reasoning segment is non-stationary within a trajectory and non-monotone across lengths — accuracy vs. length follows an inverted-U on many tasks, meaning continuation can have *negative* marginal utility (overthinking flips correct answers to incorrect) — so no single fixed threshold on any signal can be optimal across tasks, difficulties, or positions in the trace."
  - `C4` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "Regret is only defined relative to an accuracy–compute exchange rate λ (how many tokens one accuracy point is worth), which the problem does not supply: every regret number, and potentially the *ranking* of stopping rules, can shift with λ, so the measurement instrument must sweep λ rather than commit to one value."

## N6 Methodology

- **overview** `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "The method is a measurement instrument, executed in two stages. **Stage 1 (pilot — the seed's minimal experiment):** for one open reasoning model on ~3 mechanically-verifiable benchmarks, log complete reasoning trajectories, segment them at reasoning-transition checkpoints, and at every checkpoint force a trial answer and record verifier-checked correctness plus three online signals (confidence, entropy, self-consistency vote share). From each fully-logged trajectory, reconstruct the per-instance oracle stopping checkpoint for a grid of accuracy–compute exchange rates λ, then replay the logged trajectories through a bank of online stopping rules (confidence-, entropy-, self-consistency-threshold, plus published rules DEER-style and TrimR-style) and compute each rule's regret curve. **Stage 2 (scale-up):** extend to 3 model families and 5–6 benchmarks, add a learned online marginal-utility estimator trained on the pilot's logged trajectories with cross-task validation, and characterize regret as a function of task difficulty, model family, and λ. Because rules are replayed offline over the same logged trajectories, all rule comparisons are exactly paired per instance."
- **key_components**:
  - `M1` — *Step-wise trajectory logger* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: **what is built**: an instrumented decoding harness. **Instrument**: segment each CoT at reasoning-transition markers (paragraph breaks and reflection tokens such as "Wait"/"Alternatively", following the transition-point insight of `[DEER, OpenReview:NpU7ZXafRi]`); at each of the K checkpoints per trace (target K ≈ 10–30), branch a counterfactual probe — an answer-inducing prompt ("...Final answer:") — and draw N = 8 probe samples at temperature 0.6 while the main trace continues unmodified. **Recorded per checkpoint**: verifier label of each probe answer (empirical accuracy â_k), mean answer-span log-probability (confidence), mean token entropy over the answer span, modal-answer vote share among the 8 probes (self-consistency), and cumulative token cost c_k. **Granularity**: per checkpoint × per instance × per model.
  - `M2` — *Oracle stopping reconstructor and regret estimator* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: **what is computed**: for each instance and each λ in a 7-point grid (λ spanning roughly 0.01–3 accuracy-points per 1K tokens, log-spaced), the oracle checkpoint t* = argmax_k [â_k − λ·c_k] over the logged trajectory; the regret of rule r on that instance is R_λ(r) = (â_{t*} − λ·c_{t*}) − (â_{t_r} − λ·c_{t_r}), with t_r the rule's stopping checkpoint. Aggregated into mean-regret curves over λ and over difficulty deciles (difficulty proxied by per-instance baseline pass rate). Also decomposes regret into stop-too-early vs. stop-too-late mass (sign of t_r − t*).
  - `M3` — *Candidate stopping-rule bank (heuristics)* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: **what is built**: reference implementations, replayable over logged trajectories, of (a) fixed-budget stopping at each checkpoint index; (b) confidence-threshold exit (DEER-style: stop when probe answer confidence > τ); (c) entropy-threshold exit (stop when answer-span entropy < τ); (d) self-consistency-threshold exit (stop when vote share ≥ τ, the agreement-threshold family that `[CaTS, OpenReview:jrSc4RJXy1]` criticizes); (e) answer-stability exit (TrimR-style: stop when two consecutive checkpoints' modal answers agree). Thresholds τ are calibrated per rule on a held-out development split by minimizing dev regret at each λ.
  - `M4` — *Verifiable task suite and verifiers* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: **what is assembled**: GSM8K and MATH-500 (exact-match numeric/expression graders), AIME 2024+2025 (integer-answer grader), GPQA-Diamond (multiple-choice grader), and LiveCodeBench (unit-test execution) — all answers mechanically checkable per the `[GIVEN]` constraint; per-instance difficulty labels from baseline pass rates.
  - `M5` — *Logged-trajectory corpus (release artifact)* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: the full checkpoint-level logs (signals + verifier labels + costs) packaged so that any future stopping rule can be scored in regret terms by offline replay, without re-running any model.
  - `M6` — *Learned online marginal-utility estimator* `[HYPOTHESIS]`: **what is built**: a small predictor (logistic regression and a ≤2-layer MLP, ~10^3–10^4 parameters) mapping the running signal history at checkpoint k (current confidence/entropy/vote share, their deltas over the last 3 checkpoints, and c_k) to the predicted utility gain of continuing, E[â_{>k}] − â_k − λ·Δc; the induced rule stops when predicted gain ≤ 0. Trained on Stage-1 logged trajectories; evaluated with leave-one-benchmark-out and leave-one-model-out splits to test transfer.
- **challenge_arguments**:
  1. `challenge_id`: `C1`, `mechanism_components`: `["M1", "M2"]`, `argument_text` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "The oracle is unobservable online, so the instrument makes it observable *offline*: exhaustive checkpoint-level probing (M1) records the full correctness trajectory a decision-maker would have needed, and M2 turns it into a well-defined post-hoc baseline — the method measures the cost of not knowing the future rather than pretending to know it."
  2. `challenge_id`: `C2`, `mechanism_components`: `["M1", "M3", "M2"]`, `argument_text` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "Logging confidence, entropy, and self-consistency side by side on the same checkpoints, then replaying single-signal rules through the regret estimator, directly localizes where each proxy decouples from verifier truth (e.g. confidently-wrong checkpoints appear as stop-too-early regret mass), turning proxy miscalibration from an anecdote into a measured quantity."
  3. `challenge_id`: `C3`, `mechanism_components`: `["M6", "M2"]`, `argument_text` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "Because the marginal utility is non-stationary and can be negative, M6 predicts a *signed* continuation gain from the running signal history instead of applying one global threshold, and M2's stop-too-late decomposition quantifies how much regret comes specifically from overthinking segments with negative marginal utility."
  4. `challenge_id`: `C4`, `mechanism_components`: `["M2"]`, `argument_text` `[HYPOTHESIS]`: "The exchange rate is a free parameter of the evaluator, so M2 sweeps a log-spaced λ grid and reports regret curves and rule rankings *as functions of λ*, making sensitivity to the exchange rate a reported result rather than a hidden choice."
- **unaddressed_challenges**: `["C2"]` — `[HYPOTHESIS]`: the instrument measures where confidence/entropy/agreement decouple from correctness but does not repair their miscalibration; C2 is diagnosed, not solved. (C1 is likewise only made measurable, not overcome — but that is the paper's explicit goal, so it is not flagged.)
- **feasibility** `[HYPOTHESIS]` (order-of-magnitude, planning-grade):
  - *Pilot (Stage 1)*: 1 model (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) × ~1,500 instances (500 each GSM8K/MATH-500/GPQA-Diamond) × ~20 checkpoints × 8 probes ≈ 2.4×10^5 short probe generations plus 1,500 full traces. Probes are short (≤64 answer tokens) and share the trace prefix, so with prefix-KV reuse this is roughly 100–300 A100-GPU-hours (single-node week) or low-hundreds-of-dollars API-equivalent.
