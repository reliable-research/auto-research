## Seed brief

- **One-sentence description:** Measure where, as a function of task difficulty, the marginal accuracy return of additional test-time "thinking" tokens turns negative rather than merely flattening, and test whether that tipping point can be predicted from cheap pre-hoc signals before the full reasoning budget is spent.
- **Apparent inputs:** a reasoning LLM with an externally controllable thinking-length knob (truncation / forced continuation / natural stopping); a task suite with a computable difficulty proxy; per-rollout generation logs with token-level logprobs.
- **Apparent outputs:** per-difficulty-stratum accuracy-vs-length curves with localized (or right-censored) tipping points; a cheap-signal predictor of the tipping point validated under held-out strata/domains; its accuracy-cost tradeoff relative to fixed-budget, oracle, and confidence-exit baselines.
- **Closest research areas:** test-time-compute / overthinking measurement (`[Chen et al., 2024, "Do Not Think That Much"]`); theoretical inverted-U accounts of CoT length (`[WhenMoreIsLess, OpenReview:6QDFsYxtI1]`); confidence-based dynamic early exit (`[DEER, OpenReview:NpU7ZXafRi]`); pre-hoc difficulty/self-consistency probes (`[DiffAdapt, OpenReview:644FH1vVIl]`, `[AdaptiveThinking, OpenReview:2i6Rp0gCq6]`); budget forcing (`[Muennighoff et al., 2025, s1]`).
- **In scope:** measuring the sign-flip boundary in unmodified, off-the-shelf reasoning models under controlled length manipulations, and validating a lightweight pre-hoc predictor of that boundary. **Out of scope:** training or fine-tuning a new reasoning policy; cross-model-family transfer of the predictor (declared out of scope for Stage 1 in the seed's own N6); non-auto-gradable/open-ended domains.

## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (proposer):
- `summary`: Given a reasoning LLM whose test-time "thinking" length is externally controllable, and given tasks stratified by an operational difficulty proxy, determine the length at which the marginal accuracy return of additional thinking turns negative, and determine whether that point can be predicted — before the full reasoning budget is spent — from cheap signals computed early in generation.
- `input`: (1) a task suite with a computable difficulty proxy (low-budget solve rate), cross-checked against dataset-native difficulty tags and a second model's solve rate; (2) a reasoning LLM with an external length-control mechanism supporting at least truncation, forced continuation, and natural (unconstrained) stopping; (3) per-rollout generation logs, including token-level logprobs, sufficient to compute entropy trajectories and to elicit a small number of early truncated-answer probes.
- `output`: (1) per-difficulty-stratum accuracy-vs-thinking-length curves with confidence intervals; (2) the localized tipping point per stratum, or an explicit right-censoring statement where none is found within the studied length range; (3) a cheap-signal predictor of the tipping point, validated under held-out strata and held-out domains, together with its accuracy-cost tradeoff relative to fixed-budget, oracle-stop, and confidence-based early-exit baselines.

## Stage 2 — Motivation

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1:**
- `summary`: Test-time "thinking" budget is one of the few levers that can be turned up after training to try to buy more accuracy from a reasoning model, but the entire literature that builds stopping rules, difficulty probes, and length-shortening objectives on top of this lever presupposes — without ever measuring — that there is a difficulty-conditioned point past which more thinking stops helping and starts hurting.
- `why_it_matters`: If budget-allocation systems keep scaling thinking length under the assumption that returns merely flatten, but the true failure mode is that returns can turn *negative* (a correct answer state gets overwritten by continued reasoning), then naive "always think longer, or think a fixed amount" policies do not just waste compute — they actively destroy correctness on exactly the easy/medium items where a policy would be tempted to under-allocate the least scrutiny. This is specifically not knowable today: stopping-rule methods (`[DEER, OpenReview:NpU7ZXafRi]`, `[CaTS, OpenReview:jrSc4RJXy1]`) optimize *when to stop* without publishing *where the boundary is*; pre-hoc difficulty probes (`[DiffAdapt, OpenReview:644FH1vVIl]`, `[AdaptiveThinking, OpenReview:2i6Rp0gCq6]`) sort queries into coarse regimes without validating against an oracle tipping-point curve; and the one theoretical account that does predict an inverted-U shape (`[WhenMoreIsLess, OpenReview:6QDFsYxtI1]`) validates it only on synthetic tasks with a constructed difficulty variable, not on natural benchmarks under an operational proxy.
- `who_cares`: Engineers building test-time-compute serving policies who must set a thinking-token budget per query today, without a measured boundary to anchor the decision; researchers building stopping rules or difficulty probes whose own methods implicitly assume this boundary exists and is learnable; and the "reasoning economics" line of work this topic's parent category names directly (`B4. 预算分配 / 推理经济学`), which needs a measured landscape to allocate against rather than an assumed one.
- `evidence`: `[Chen et al., 2024 ("Do Not Think That Much for 2+3=?")]`'s own motivation documents that reasoning models "expend redundant solution rounds on easy problems with negligible or negative benefit" — direct evidence the phenomenon exists in aggregate; `[WhenMoreIsLess, OpenReview:6QDFsYxtI1]`'s own theory independently predicts "an inverted U-shaped curve relating chain length to accuracy" — independent theoretical support for the shape, though not yet measured on natural data. No corpus paper among the four search angles run for `digest.md` N3 combines (a) natural-benchmark difficulty stratification, (b) a statistically localized tipping point, and (c) a validated pre-hoc predictor of that point — itself evidence the question is open.

**critique:**
- summary: Grounded motivation with real evidence, but coverage of Stage 1's third output is thin — the draft argues hard for *why the boundary map matters* and barely argues for why the *predictor* (as opposed to the coarse bins that already exist) is the piece worth building.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "why_it_matters", anti_pattern: "partial_output_coverage", comment: "Stage 1 output (3) is a validated cheap-signal predictor plus its accuracy-cost tradeoff relative to baselines — not just the existence of a boundary. The draft explains why DEER/CaTS/DiffAdapt/AdaptiveThinking fall short, but never explains why a *continuous, cheaply-predicted* tipping point is worth having once DiffAdapt/AdaptiveThinking already sort queries into three coarse regimes. A reader could reasonably ask: 'why not just use the existing 3-class probe — what does resolving the boundary more finely actually buy?' That question is real (digest N4's own limitation on coarse probes says exactly that their oracle-recovery is 'unknown', not that it's bad) and currently unaddressed."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `why_it_matters` (extended): "...critically, the value of resolving the boundary continuously rather than into three coarse bins is not obvious on its face — it has to be earned. A coarse Easy/Normal/Hard decision, applied uniformly within each bin, is cheaper to build and already exists (`[DiffAdapt, OpenReview:644FH1vVIl]`, `[AdaptiveThinking, OpenReview:2i6Rp0gCq6]`); if it already recovers most of the achievable accuracy-cost tradeoff, a continuous predictor is not worth its own added complexity. Nobody has measured *how much* oracle tradeoff a coarse bin decision leaves on the table relative to knowing the tipping point exactly — which is precisely the quantity this paper's predictor evaluation is built to report, win or lose. If the coarse probe turns out to already be nearly as good, that itself is a decision-relevant finding for budget-allocation systems (buy the cheap probe, skip the trajectory-level signal), not a failure of this paper."
- `changes_made`: added the explicit coarse-bin-vs-continuous-prediction argument, tying the predictor's motivation to a comparison whose answer is allowed to go either way.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 2.**

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

`digest.md` N5 already separates four problem-inherent obstacles from the N4 related-work limitations (its own `conflation_check: true`), so this stage's decomposition follows that four-way split directly rather than inventing a different one.

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (4 sub-problems):

- **`sp_difficulty_proxy_validity`** — *description:* Operationalize "difficulty," which is not directly observable for a fresh task instance, as a computable proxy without that proxy secretly encoding one model's idiosyncrasies rather than task-intrinsic difficulty. *why_it_is_a_problem:* every downstream curve, tipping point, and predictor claim is stratified by this proxy — if the strata are an artifact of which model measured them, the whole boundary map inherits that artifact. *technical_challenge:* the natural proxy (a model's own low-budget solve rate) is defined circularly with respect to that same model's later, full-budget behavior; there is no proxy-free ground truth for difficulty to check it against, only convergent evidence from independent proxies. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_wheniswless_synthetic_only"]` (WhenMoreIsLess validates its inverted-U theory only where the difficulty variable is constructed by the experimenter, not measured on natural data — exactly the gap this sub-problem closes).
- **`sp_changepoint_power`** — *description:* Detect, per stratum, the specific length at which the *slope* of the accuracy-vs-length curve turns significantly negative, rather than merely observing that accuracy is noisy. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a trend claim that isn't statistically localized is unfalsifiable — "it looks like it goes down eventually" is not a reportable finding. *technical_challenge:* accuracy at a (stratum, length) cell is a Bernoulli mean over stochastic rollouts, so a sign change in its *forward difference* is a second-order statistic requiring far more samples per cell than estimating accuracy itself would. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no cited work needs to solve this because none of them localizes a continuous sign-flip point with a stated significance threshold in the first place.
- **`sp_signal_transfer_validity`** — *description:* Determine whether cheap, pre-hoc signals (entropy trajectories, early-answer consistency) carry information about the tipping point beyond what a coarser difficulty-bin decision already provides, and whether that information transfers to held-out strata and domains. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a predictor that merely re-encodes difficulty-bin membership or trace-position priors would look informative in-sample without adding anything a cheaper method doesn't already give. *technical_challenge:* entropy and consistency are indirect proxies for eventual correctness whose relationship to the tipping point may not transfer across strata or domains, and entropy itself mechanically co-varies with position in the trace — confounding "signal" with "how much text has been generated so far." *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_coarse_probes_no_oracle_validation"]` (DiffAdapt/AdaptiveThinking predict coarse regimes but never validate how much of the oracle tradeoff a coarse decision recovers — this sub-problem is what makes that validation possible).
- **`sp_intervention_emergence_confound`** — *description:* Distinguish a genuine difficulty-conditioned property of the reasoning process from an artifact of *how* thinking length was manipulated (truncation, forced continuation) to produce the curve in the first place. *why_it_is_a_problem:* an externally truncated-and-forced-answer trace is not obviously the same object as a naturally short trace, so a negative-slope finding under one manipulation alone is at risk of being a format artifact rather than a property of extended reasoning. *technical_challenge:* no single manipulation realizes the true counterfactual — "the same, unmodified model choosing to think longer on the same item" — so full causal identification is not achievable with any experimental design available here; the best available move is triangulating agreement across manipulations that differ in exactly how they impose length, which mitigates but does not eliminate the confound. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_interventions_presuppose_boundary"]` (JET/ThinkingSpeed/FirstImpression change the model or its decoding to exploit an assumed boundary, entangling intervention and phenomenon — this sub-problem is what a measurement-only study must resolve instead).

**critique:**
- summary: Coverage checks out — solving all four jointly would deliver Stage 1's three outputs — but `sp_intervention_emergence_confound` is presented as if it will be fully resolved, when `digest.md`'s own N6 explicitly lists this exact confound as an **unaddressed challenge**, not a solved one.

## Stage 5 — Method Design

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (4 methods):

- **`m_difficulty_triangulation`** → targets `sp_difficulty_proxy_validity`. *proposed_approach:* Operationalize difficulty as the empirical solve rate over 8 rollouts truncated at 512 thinking tokens, quintile-bin items on that score, and require agreement (rank correlation) against two independent stratifications — dataset-native difficulty tags and a second model's own low-budget solve rate — before treating a stratum as difficulty-relevant rather than model-idiosyncratic. *key_insight:* true difficulty is unobservable, but a proxy's model-relativity risk can be *measured* rather than assumed away, by requiring convergent validity across independent proxies instead of trusting any single one. *hypotheses:* ["Quintile assignments from the primary model's low-budget solve rate will rank-correlate with dataset-native difficulty tags at Spearman ρ ≥ 0.4, and with a second model's low-budget solve rate at Spearman ρ ≥ 0.4; strata failing both checks will be reported as unreliable rather than used for downstream claims."]
- **`m_paired_changepoint_test`** → targets `sp_changepoint_power`. *proposed_approach:* Generate rollouts at a fixed length grid under three arms (truncation, forced continuation, natural stopping); per stratum, test for a significant negative forward difference via item-paired bootstrap resampling (10,000 resamples, Holm-corrected across strata × arms), reporting the tipping point as the smallest grid length with a significant negative step, or explicit right-censoring if none is found. *key_insight:* pairing each bootstrap resample at the *item* level (not the aggregate-curve level) is what gives a second-order slope-sign statistic enough power to survive individual-rollout noise, rather than smoothing over exactly the signal being sought. *hypotheses:* ["In at least 2 of 5 difficulty quintiles, the accuracy-vs-length curve will show a statistically significant negative-slope region (peak-to-max contrast ≥3pp, Holm-corrected one-sided bootstrap p<0.05) that replicates in sign across at least 2 of the 3 length-control arms.", "The localized tipping point will shift to longer lengths as stratum difficulty increases (Jonckheere-Terpstra trend test, p<0.05), with the hardest strata plausibly right-censored rather than reversing order."]
- **`m_decoupled_cheap_signals`** → targets `sp_signal_transfer_validity`. *proposed_approach:* Build a predictor of the tipping point from entropy-trajectory and early-consistency features computed from the first 256 thinking tokens plus 4 truncated-answer probes, trained and evaluated under cross-stratum and cross-domain holdouts, benchmarked against two decoupling controls — a reimplemented 3-class prefill-probe (the coarse-bin baseline) and a position-controlled length-only null feature set. *key_insight:* a predictor that merely re-encodes difficulty-bin membership or trace-position priors would look informative without adding anything beyond a cheaper coarse probe; only beating both controls under held-out strata/domains demonstrates the trajectory-level signal itself carries information. *hypotheses:* ["At least one cheap feature family (entropy-trajectory or early-consistency) achieves AUROC ≥0.65 (95% CI excluding 0.5) for classifying whether marginal gain beyond the 256-token probe point is negative, on held-out strata — and this AUROC is not matched by the length-only null feature set.", "Stopping at the predicted tipping point recovers at least 50% of the oracle-minus-best-fixed-budget accuracy gap at matched mean token cost, beating the best single fixed budget and exceeding the fraction the coarser 3-class prefill-probe baseline recovers."]
- **`m_triangulated_intervention_design`** → targets `sp_intervention_emergence_confound`. *proposed_approach:* Run three length-control arms (truncation + forced-answer, forced continuation via appended "Wait," and unconstrained natural stopping) on identical items, and report a negative-slope region as a confirmed finding only where at least two of the three arms agree in sign — treating single-arm-only detections as a candidate manipulation artifact, reported separately rather than folded into the headline claim. *key_insight:* no single manipulation realizes the true "same model, choosing to think longer" counterfactual, but agreement across manipulations that differ in exactly how they impose length is evidence a finding reflects the reasoning process rather than one manipulation's artifact — a stated mitigation, not a claimed identification. *hypotheses:* ["The negative-slope region detected under the truncation arm will replicate in sign (though not necessarily magnitude) under the forced-continuation arm in a majority of quintiles where a flip is detected at all; single-arm-only detections will be reported separately as unconfirmed rather than folded into the headline claim."]

**critique:**
- summary: Every method has a real mechanism (no goal-restatement) and every hypothesis is falsifiable with a stated threshold, but `m_decoupled_cheap_signals` has two issues: its second hypothesis is framed in language that reads as an outcome-tier "beats baseline" claim, and its target sub-problem's own technical_challenge names cross-*model-family* transfer, which the hypothesis set never actually tests.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "m_decoupled_cheap_signals.hypotheses[1]", anti_pattern: "not yet a stage-6 violation, but a framing risk that will become one", comment: "'beating the best single fixed budget and exceeding the fraction the coarser 3-class prefill-probe baseline recovers' is exactly the shape of an outcome-tier, beats-baseline claim. That's not disallowed at the hypothesis level (Stage 5 doesn't gate on tier), but stage 1's confirmed `study_type: empirical` forbids an outcome-tier RQ from being built on top of it at Stage 6 — this needs a flag now so Stage 6 doesn't inherit it uncritically as 'the headline comparison.' Consistent with digest N12's own hedge: the predictor is 'a small analysis instrument in service of the empirical claim... not a system or tool release with an independent evaluation story' — its evaluation needs to read as an instrument-informativeness check, not a competition."}`
  - `{severity: major, field: "m_decoupled_cheap_signals / sp_signal_transfer_validity.technical_challenge", anti_pattern: "mismatch", comment: "The sub-problem's technical_challenge (Stage 4) explicitly names transfer 'across task domains, or across model families' as part of what makes signal transfer hard, but neither hypothesis here tests cross-model-family transfer — both are scoped to held-out strata/domains within evaluated models.
