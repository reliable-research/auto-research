## Seed brief

- **One-sentence description:** Measure, category by category, which types of LLM-agent negotiation manipulation are driven to near-zero success by protocol-layer mechanism design alone, which persist under every tested protocol, and whether a candidate model-layer defense recovers ground on the ones that persist.
- **Apparent inputs:** parameterized bilateral bargaining scenarios with machine-checkable hidden ground truth (sampled private valuations, a fact table, a commitment ledger); a four-rung protocol-constraint ladder (free text → structured offers → +escrow → +fact audit) implemented as environment wrappers around fixed LLM negotiators; an attacker agent (prompted or scripted) instructed to attempt one of five manipulation categories; a model-layer defense arm (defended prompt, activation-probe monitor).
- **Apparent outputs:** a manipulation-success-rate matrix per (category × protocol) cell; a per-category protocol-fixable/protocol-invariant/undecided verdict; a directional adjudication of whether model-layer negotiation defense is an irreplaceable research direction; a defense-arm recovery result on protocol-invariant categories.
- **Closest research areas:** incentive-compatible mechanism design over formal action spaces (AMenuNet, DSIC auction design); LLM strategic-deception detection via activation probes (StrategicDishonesty, AmongUs); LLM social/negotiation evaluation scaffolds (SOTOPIA); classical protocol-redesign theory (Rosenschein & Zlotkin, 1994).
- **In scope:** measuring existing prompted/scripted LLM negotiators' behavior under a manipulated protocol-constraint ladder, plus testing two existing-technique model-layer defenses. **Out of scope:** training new negotiator models; proposing a novel protocol beyond the tested ladder; live/non-sandboxable deployments; verification of claims outside a machine-checkable fact table (explicitly named as an open residue, not solved here).

## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (proposer):
- `summary`: Given a fixed pair of LLM negotiation agents playing a bilateral bargaining scenario instrumented with hidden, machine-checkable ground truth, and given a ladder of protocol-layer constraints (free text, structured offer schemas, mechanical escrow, fact-audited claims) implemented as environment wrappers around the fixed negotiators, determine — separately for each of five manipulation categories (misrepresented private valuation, fabricated external fact/urgency, emotional/rapport coercion, false commitment/reneging, collusive signaling) — whether an attacker's manipulation success is driven to near-zero by some tested protocol rung, persists above a nontrivial rate under the strictest tested rung, or is neither cleanly, and whether a candidate model-layer defense reduces residual manipulation success on the categories that persist.
- `formal_summary`: Category set K = {a: misrepresented valuation, b: fabricated fact/urgency, c: emotional coercion, d: false commitment/reneging, e: collusive signaling}. Protocol ladder P = {P0 free-text, P1 structured offers, P2 = P1+escrow, P3 = P2+fact-audit}. Trial t in cell (k,p) yields transcript τ, attacker surplus u_A, defender surplus u_D, and hidden scenario parameters θ (sampled private valuations, a fact table with known truth values, a commitment ledger). A truthful-play surplus distribution S<sup>tru</sup><sub>k</sub> is precomputed from scripted truthful agents. Manipulation-success-rate MSR<sub>k,p</sub> is scored against θ, never against τ's structural form. Determine, for each k, whether MSR<sub>k,p</sub> reaches a pre-registered fixability threshold under some p, remains above an invariance threshold at the strictest p, or is undecided; and for each invariant k, whether a model-layer defense d reduces MSR<sub>k,P3</sub> while preserving the agreement rate.
- `input`: (1) five manipulation-category scenario families, each with a hidden ground-truth file (sampled valuations, fact table, commitment ledger, precomputed truthful-play surplus distribution); (2) the four-rung protocol ladder, held fixed as an environment wrapper independent of which model plays the negotiators; (3) an attacker agent in two modes (prompted, free-strategy; scripted, templated moves at fixed turns) instructed to attempt one category's manipulation; (4) two candidate model-layer defenses (a defended prompt; an activation-probe monitor).
- `output`: (1) a manipulation-success-rate matrix, one cell per (category × protocol rung), with confidence intervals; (2) a per-category verdict — protocol-fixable / protocol-invariant / undecided; (3) an overall directional adjudication of whether model-layer negotiation defense constitutes an irreplaceable research direction or folds into mechanism-design engineering; (4) for protocol-invariant categories, a defense-arm recovery result testing whether a candidate model-layer instrument reduces residual manipulation success without collapsing legitimate agreement.

- `objective`: what counts as a correct verdict is not yet specified beyond "matches the true pattern of manipulation success" — flagged below as incomplete.
- `study_type`: `empirical` — stated explicitly: digest N12 already classifies this as `empirical` ("the measured verdict, not the testbed, is the paper's central move"), and the seed's own core question ("哪几类操纵…能消除…哪些操纵…只能靠模型侧防御") is a measurement/adjudication question, not a proposed fix.

**critique:**
- summary: Strong, evidence-traceable draft — but the objective is underspecified in a way that quietly biases the study toward finding a split, and it doesn't yet say what happens when the evidence is ambiguous.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "objective", anti_pattern: "vague_success (evaluability gap)", comment: "As drafted, objective doesn't say what threshold makes a verdict 'protocol-fixable' vs. 'protocol-invariant,' nor what happens to a category whose confidence interval straddles both thresholds. paper.md's own Step 6 already commits to exact numbers (MSR ≤5% significantly below P0 → fixable; MSR ≥10% at P3 with CI excluding 5% → invariant; CIs spanning [0.05,0.10] → undecided, 'never forced to a side') — none of that made it into this stage's objective field, so as written the objective is not yet evaluable."}`

## Stage 2 — Motivation

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1:**
- `summary`: Protocol-layer mechanism design — structured bid schemas, verifiable commitment schemes — is the default engineering-first answer to negotiation manipulation between autonomous LLM agents, but whether it actually closes off manipulation, or only the fraction of it that happens to live inside its observable, enforceable action space, has never been measured category by category.
- `why_it_matters`: As LLM agents are delegated real bilateral negotiations — price, resource splits, contract terms — the field is implicitly betting engineering investment on the assumption that a well-designed protocol can neutralize deception irrespective of which model sits behind an agent. If some manipulation categories are content-borne (a false valuation typed into a perfectly well-formed structured field is still false) rather than structure-borne, that bet is only half-covered: protocol-only investment leaves exactly the manipulation channels that need model-layer defense unaddressed, while a compliance dashboard showing "100% schema-valid offers" makes it look solved. Classical incentive-compatibility guarantees (dominant-strategy-truthful bidding, achieved by construction in modern auction-design networks) hold only over the formal action space the mechanism can see — they say nothing about a negotiator that can also persuade, fabricate context, or renege through the natural-language channel the mechanism was never built to observe.
- `who_cares`: teams building agent-to-agent commerce or negotiation platforms who must decide where to put defense-engineering budget (protocol standardization vs. model hardening); AI-safety researchers deciding whether classical mechanism-design guarantees transfer to open-ended natural-language negotiation; the nascent model-layer-negotiation-defense research community, whose claim to be a distinct research direction (rather than a stopgap that protocol engineering will eventually make redundant) is directly contingent on at least one manipulation category surviving every tested protocol.
- `evidence`: `[AMenuNet, OpenReview:cNb5hkTfGC]` delivers dominant-strategy incentive compatibility by construction, but strictly over a formal bid action space; `[StrategicDishonesty, OpenReview:IbDr8xgUMW]` shows models can produce deceptive output that fools every tested *output-level* judge while remaining detectable only from internal activations — direct evidence that content a protocol cannot see can defeat a check built on top of it. No corpus paper crosses protocol constraints with manipulation categories in LLM-vs-LLM negotiation (N3's own search-honesty note, checked across four search angles: mechanism design/incentive compatibility, agent security/defense layering, deception/honesty, persuasion/social interaction) — itself evidence the question is open.

**critique:**
- summary: Stake-specific, evidence-backed motivation with real citations — but it stops one step short of covering everything Stage 1 promised as output.
- comments:
  - `{severity: major, field: "why_it_matters", anti_pattern: "partial_output_coverage", comment: "Stage 1's output (4) is a defense-arm recovery result on protocol-invariant categories — testing whether an actual model-layer instrument closes the gap, not just proving the gap exists. why_it_matters motivates why the fixable/invariant split matters but never explains why *testing a candidate defense* matters, as opposed to stopping at 'this category resists every protocol we tried.' A reader could reasonably ask: why not publish the invariance finding alone and call the defense question future work? That's a real, unaddressed gap as drafted."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `why_it_matters` (extended): "...critically, establishing that a category is protocol-invariant only proves a negative — that no protocol on the tested ladder reaches it — and by itself gives no evidence that model-layer research is a productive direction rather than a dead end reached by elimination. Testing whether an actual model-layer instrument (an activation-probe monitor, a defended prompt) recovers ground on exactly the categories protocols cannot reach is what turns 'this sub-field should exist' into 'this sub-field has already produced a working instrument on the residue' — which is a categorically stronger and more decision-relevant claim than invariance alone, and is exactly why the defense-arm test is part of this study rather than deferred as future work."
- `changes_made`: added the negative-result-vs-constructive-result argument, closing the output-coverage gap on Stage 1's output (4).
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 2.**

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (6 sub-problems, from digest N5's C1–C5 plus a sixth surfaced by Stage 2's refined motivation):

- **`sp_category_heterogeneity`** — *description:* Decompose "manipulation" into structurally distinct categories and compute a verdict for each one, rather than one aggregate success rate. *why_it_is_a_problem:* aggregating across categories would let a protocol's success on easy (enforcement-shaped) categories mask its failure on hard (content-shaped) ones — hiding exactly the boundary this study exists to locate. *technical_challenge:* five structurally different attack channels (reported values, asserted facts, affect, coordination, timing) must each carry independent hidden ground truth and be scored under a shared statistical decision rule without inflating false-positive verdicts across the resulting 5-category × multi-rung contrast family. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_amenunet_formal_action_space", "lim_ucbs_scalar_observable"]` — both cited works' guarantees are narrow precisely because they were built for a homogeneous, single-channel action space; this sub-problem is what a heterogeneous, multi-channel setting demands instead.
- **`sp_content_vs_structure`** — *description:* Determine whether a protocol constraint on message *structure* actually constrains message *content*, or only its packaging. *why_it_is_a_problem:* if content and structure are logically independent axes, "the protocol closed this channel" claims risk conflating a format change with a real truthfulness guarantee — a false claim sitting inside a well-formed field is still false. *technical_challenge:* no existing structural constraint yields a mechanical procedure for telling content-truthfulness apart from structure-conformance; both must be scored against independent hidden ground truth (the fact table, the sampled valuation), never inferred from message well-formedness. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_ucbs_scalar_observable"]`.
- **`sp_verification_bootstrap`** — *description:* Establish that the mechanism enforcing a commitment or auditing a claim is not itself gameable by the same class of strategic actor it constrains. *why_it_is_a_problem:* if the checker were itself an LLM-mediated process, "verifiable" would be circular — the check could in principle be manipulated the same way the underlying negotiation is. *technical_challenge:* drawing an explicit boundary between machine-checkable claims (which admit deterministic, environment-side enforcement) and claims that are inherently unverifiable (intentions, subjective valuations), rather than assuming all "verifiable commitment" schemes are equally non-gameable. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_rosenschein_formal_domains"]` — classical protocol-redesign results sidestep this problem entirely by working in domains with no open-ended claims to begin with, which is exactly why it resurfaces here as a problem worth stating on its own.
- **`sp_attribution_confound`** — *description:* Attribute an observed drop in manipulation success under a stricter protocol to the protocol closing the channel, rather than to the attacker failing to execute the attack in the new format. *why_it_is_a_problem:* a protocol change alters the defense surface and the attacker's task difficulty simultaneously (and can trigger safety refusal); a naive protocol-vs-P0 comparison can't tell "protocol defended" from "attacker got worse at attacking," producing a false protocol-fixable verdict. *technical_challenge:* requires measuring attack execution separately from attack success across both a free-strategy and a capability-floored attacker, with enough trials per cell for a mixed-effects model to isolate a protocol main effect from attacker-competence and scenario variance. *source_limitation_ids:* `["__novel__"]` — no cited work isolates this confound; it is a requirement this study's own design surfaces.
- **`sp_construct_validity`** — *description:* Establish that the rule-based, surplus-quantile success criterion actually tracks human judgment of "manipulation occurred," rather than mislabeling aggressive-but-honest bargaining or missing non-surplus harms. *why_it_is_a_problem:* every downstream verdict is only as trustworthy as this scoring criterion; if it diverges from human judgment, category verdicts would be an artifact of the scoring rule, not a finding about protocols. *technical_challenge:* open-ended negotiation transcripts carry no objective label, so validity must be established empirically (a human-annotated audit against the rule-based score) rather than assumed by construction, with a pre-committed go/no-go threshold before any scale-up conclusion is trusted. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_sotopia_no_ground_truth", "lim_honestypolicy_not_operational"]`.
- **`sp_defense_recovery`** — *description:* Determine whether a candidate model-layer defense can recover ground on exactly the categories no tested protocol reaches, without doing so only by suppressing legitimate agreement. *why_it_is_a_problem:* establishing protocol-invariance is a negative result; without testing an actual defense, the claim that model-layer research is a productive rather than merely residual direction rests on absence, not construction. *technical_challenge:* any manipulation-success reduction achieved by making the defender simply refuse to trade is a "defense-by-not-trading" that trivially reduces success without producing a usable safeguard, so recovery must be verified jointly with an agreement-rate-preservation constraint, and the probe's own reliability must be established before it is trusted as a defense signal. *source_limitation_ids:* `["lim_dishonesty_no_protocol_test"]` — this work shows probes detect deception that fools output-level judges but never tests whether combining that signal with protocol-level restriction changes outcomes, nor whether doing so preserves legitimate trade.

**critique:**
- summary: Coverage checks out (solving all six would jointly deliver Stage 1's four outputs), but two sub-problems sit close enough together that the difference needs to be made explicit rather than left implicit.
- comments:
  - `{severity: minor, field: "sp_content_vs_structure / sp_verification_bootstrap", anti_pattern: "overlap", comment: "Both are, at a glance, about 'the limits of what a protocol can guarantee' — which can read as the same underlying limitation stated twice. They are genuinely distinct — one is an axis-independence problem (structure and truthfulness are logically separate, so a format check never certifies content, regardless of who or what does the checking) and the other is a recursive-trust problem (even for claims a protocol *can* check, is the checker itself trustworthy?) — but the descriptions don't make that distinction explicit enough as drafted."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `sp_content_vs_structure.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is an **axis-independence** problem — it would remain true even if the checker enforcing the structure were perfectly trustworthy, because structure-conformance and content-truthfulness are independent properties of a message by definition, not a consequence of who checks it."
- `sp_verification_bootstrap.technical_challenge` sharpened to end with: "...this is a distinct **recursive-trust** problem — it only arises for claims a protocol *could in principle* check, and asks whether the checker doing that checking is itself gameable; it says nothing about claims (like private valuations) that no checker, trustworthy or not, could verify in the first place."
- `changes_made`: made the axis-independence-vs-recursive-trust distinction explicit in both `technical_challenge` fields.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 4** (6 sub-problems; 4 sourced from related-work limitations — two of them double-sourced — 1 double-sourced, 1 `__novel__` with a stated decisive-component justification).

## Stage 5 — Method Design

### Round 1 (critic phase)

**draft_v1** (6 methods):

- **`m_category_scenario_taxonomy`** → targets `sp_category_heterogeneity`. *proposed_approach:* Build five parameterized scenario families, each shipping a machine-readable ground-truth file (sampled private valuations, a fact table with known truth values, a commitment ledger, a precomputed truthful-play surplus distribution from scripted truthful agents); apply a pre-registered per-category verdict rule with Holm–Bonferroni correction across the resulting category × rung contrast family. *key_insight:* a category-level verdict rule with multiplicity control is what prevents a single aggregate number — or an uncorrected battery of comparisons — from either masking a real invariant category behind fixed ones, or manufacturing a false invariant verdict out of noise across many simultaneous tests. *hypotheses:* ["The per-category verdict rule yields a non-degenerate split — at least one category is classified protocol-fixable and at least one is classified protocol-invariant — rather than every category landing on the same side."]
- **`m_ground_truth_content_scorer`** → targets `sp_content_vs_structure`. *proposed_approach:* Score every trial's manipulation success against hidden scenario parameters directly (deviation from the sampled private valuation, the fact table's truth value, the ledger's record), never against the message's conformance to a protocol schema, so a false claim inside a well-formed field is still scored as manipulation. *key_insight:* because the scorer never inspects message form, an improvement measured under a stricter protocol can only reflect an actual reduction in ground-truth-verified manipulation, never a cosmetic change in how the same false content is packaged. *hypotheses:* ["Reneging-type manipulation collapses toward the fixability threshold under mechanical escrow (rung P2+), while misreported-valuation and emotional-coercion manipulation retain success significantly above the invariance threshold under the strictest tested protocol (P3), because escrow enforcement and content truthfulness are logically independent axes."]
- **`m_environment_side_enforcement`** → targets `sp_verification_bootstrap`. *proposed_approach:* Implement escrow auto-execution and fact-table lookup as deterministic, non-LLM environment code, restricting "verifiable" to exactly the machine-checkable claim subset and explicitly excluding claims outside the fact table (intentions, subjective valuations) from any verifiability claim. *key_insight:* a checker that is itself an LLM inherits the same manipulability as the negotiators it checks; grounding enforcement in deterministic table/ledger lookups sidesteps that recursive-trust problem for exactly the claims it can reach, while making the residual (unenforceable) claim space an explicit, reported boundary rather than a hidden assumption. *hypotheses:* ["Reneging success collapses under P2+ specifically because enforcement is deterministic and environment-side rather than because attackers lose persuasive capability — verified by the scripted-attacker control retaining a high attack-execution rate under P2+ even as success collapses."]
- **`m_dual_attacker_confound_control`** → targets `sp_attribution_confound`. *proposed_approach:* Run every (category × protocol) cell under both a free-strategy prompted attacker and a templated scripted attacker with manipulation moves inserted at fixed turns; score attack-execution separately from attack-success; fit a mixed-effects model (success ~ protocol × category + random intercepts for scenario and model pair) to isolate the protocol main effect from attacker-competence and scenario variance. *key_insight:* the scripted attacker provides a capability floor immune to competence or refusal confounds, so a protocol effect that survives in the scripted arm cannot be attributed to the attacker simply failing to execute the attack in the new format. *hypotheses:* ["A protocol rung's effect on manipulation success is directionally consistent between the prompted and scripted attacker arms, conditional on attack-execution rate > 0, for at least four of the five manipulation categories."]
- **`m_ground_truth_validity_audit`** → targets `sp_construct_validity`. *proposed_approach:* Before trusting any scale-up verdict, audit the rule-based scorer against human judgment on a held-out sample (200 transcripts, 2 annotators), computing Cohen's kappa; proceed to the full scale-up matrix only if kappa ≥ 0.7, otherwise revise the scoring rule before any category verdict is treated as evidence. *key_insight:* a walking-skeleton validity gate turns "we assume our scorer measures manipulation" into a checked, falsifiable precondition rather than a silent assumption that every downstream claim would otherwise rest on unexamined. *hypotheses:* ["The rule-based scorer reaches Cohen's kappa ≥ 0.7 against human-annotated judgments on the 200-transcript pilot audit, and the disagreement cases concentrate in the highest-valuation-asymmetry scenarios — where aggressive-but-honest bargaining most resembles exploitation — rather than being spread uniformly."]
- **`m_joint_defense_recovery_test`** → targets `sp_defense_recovery`. *proposed_approach:* On every category classified protocol-invariant, run two model-layer interventions at the strictest protocol (P3) — a defended prompt (explicit manipulation-awareness and counterpart-claim skepticism) and an activation-probe monitor trained on contrastive honesty data — and declare recovery only if manipulation success drops by at least 0.10 absolute (p<0.05, Holm-corrected across invariant categories × 2 interventions) while the agreement rate stays within 0.10 of the undefended P3 baseline. *key_insight:* gating "recovery" on a joint success-reduction-and-agreement-preservation condition is what rules out the trivial defense of refusing to trade — the same discipline that keeps every other verdict in this study from being satisfied by a degenerate signal. *hypotheses:* ["On each protocol-invariant category, the activation-probe monitor reduces residual manipulation success by at least 0.10 absolute (p<0.05) at the strictest protocol while keeping agreement rate within 0.10 of the undefended baseline, and does so more reliably than the defended-prompt intervention."]

**critique:**
- summary: Every method carries a real mechanism (no goal-restatement) and every hypothesis is falsifiable with a stated threshold — but the six hypotheses are not all the same *kind* of claim, and the method set doesn't yet say why that's intentional rather than an inconsistency.
- comments:
  - `{severity: minor, field: "m_ground_truth_validity_audit.hypotheses", anti_pattern: null, comment: "Five of the six methods' hypotheses are direct empirical predictions about the negotiation phenomenon itself (which categories collapse, whether a defense recovers ground). This one is a pure instrument-validity claim (a kappa threshold) that every other hypothesis is conditionally trustworthy on, not a competing domain claim. As drafted, nothing explains why a validity-gate claim belongs in the same hypothesis list as the domain claims it's meant to license, rather than being folded into the study's background assumptions."}`
- decision: `move_to_refiner`.

### Refiner pass

- `m_ground_truth_validity_audit.key_insight` extended: "...this hypothesis is deliberately **gate-shaped rather than domain-shaped**: every other hypothesis in this stage is conditionally trustworthy only if this one holds first — a construct-validity precondition, not a competing empirical claim about negotiation behavior — which is exactly why it is stated as a falsifiable hypothesis in its own right (with its own pre-committed go/no-go threshold) rather than buried as a background caveat in the study's assumptions section, where a failed audit could quietly get explained away instead of triggering a revision."
- `changes_made`: justified the gate-shaped-vs-domain-shaped hypothesis distinction explicitly, so the six-hypothesis set reads as one coherent design rather than an inconsistency.
- `declined_to_fix`: none.

**Confirmed Stage 5** — 6 methods, **6 hypotheses total** (one per method) — one instrument-validity gate (`m_ground_truth_validity_audit`) and five domain-facing claims, a deliberate mix explained above for an `empirical` study whose downstream verdicts depend on trusting its own measurement instrument.
