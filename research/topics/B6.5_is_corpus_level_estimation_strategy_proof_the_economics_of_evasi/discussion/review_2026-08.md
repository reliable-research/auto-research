## 3. Feasibility + staleness findings (se-paper-reviewer lens)

| ID | Severity | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **[major]** | **Unanchored cost construct / portfolio retirement conflict.** `portfolio_2026-08.md` retires B6.5 to Tier 4 as *"[unfeasible] — evasion economics played against a simulated adversary with assumed budgets; no accessible truth source."* The ground-truth side (α) *is* accessible via the semi-synthetic construction, but the critique lands on the cost side: both cost models are declared bookkeeping conventions with no external anchor (dollar cost, API price, detection-of-evasion risk), so the measured β is a property of the declared cost *family*, not of real attacker economics. The draft's construct threat concedes this only partially, while the title/abstract/conclusion still read as if real "economics" were measured. Fix applied: explicit simulated-cost-economy concession in Threats (construct) and Conclusion; scope sentence added to the Experiments setup. The retirement itself is a portfolio-level decision this review does not overturn; the revision makes the spec honest about exactly the gap the retirement names. |
| F2 | **[moderate]** | **No compute-budget arithmetic for the $B_{\min}$ search.** RQ3's grid alone is 4 corpus sizes × 2 cost models × 3–5 seeds ≈ 40 cells; each $B_{\min}$ requires a search over budgets (≈8–10 evaluations under bisection), and each evaluation re-paraphrases up to O(N) documents — at N = 10⁵ that is order 10⁶–10⁷ LLM paraphrase calls per cell if done naively, unaccounted anywhere in the draft. Fix applied: amortization design added to Experimental Setup (per-document paraphrase ladder pre-generated once per generator family and strength level, reused across all budget-search steps, both cost models, and both adversary types; budget search then only re-selects cached variants, making its marginal cost selection, not generation) plus the monotonicity assumption that licenses bisection. |
| F3 | **[moderate]** | **Reference staleness / citation hygiene.** (a) 7 of 12 `refs.bib` entries are "Anonymous, n.d., OpenReview id" for works that are published and citable — most damagingly the paper's own primary precedent MonitorAIScale, which is Liang et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2403.07183, and DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2301.11305). (b) The single-document-fragility premise — the paper's founding assumption — is carried by DetectGPT plus one calibration paper, with none of the 2023–26 paraphrase-attack literature (Krishna et al. arXiv:2303.13408; Sadasivan et al. arXiv:2303.11156; Adversarial Paraphrasing arXiv:2506.07001; PADBen arXiv:2511.00416; MASH arXiv:2601.08564) cited, even though that literature is both the premise's evidence and the source of attacker instruments. Fix applied: see "Revisions applied" and "Citation debt". |
| N1 | **[moderate]** | **Missing neighbor field: quantification learning.** The draft's three estimator designs instantiate standard QL families (naive average ≈ probabilistic classify-and-count; debias-and-aggregate ≈ adjusted count; mixture-MLE ≈ distribution matching), studied for two decades under prior-probability shift (Forman 2008; González et al. 2017; arXiv:2310.04565; arXiv:2107.08209). A reviewer from that community will say "this is adversarial quantification without the name." The delta survives — QL studies natural shift, not strategic budget-bounded manipulation — but the concession must be explicit. Fix applied: new Related Work paragraph (concede-and-claim). |
