# Research Taste Audit — T5.3

> Skill: `research-taste-auditor` (Chen, Zhao & Cohan, arXiv:2607.01233). Run once, on the core idea (Motivation + Method) confirmed at Stage 2 / Stage 5 of the discussion-loop run, **before** final paper assembly — per the skill's own guidance ("cheaper to catch before drafting than after").

## 1. Extracted idea

**Motivation** (Stage 2, confirmed): "As coding agents produce patches faster than teams can manually review them against explicit design constraints, automated constraint verification is being treated as viable delegated gatekeeping — but whether such a verifier's precision and recall actually match human review, and for which kinds of constraints, has not been measured... treating both [structural and semantic constraints] the same way behind one blended accuracy number hides exactly the information a team needs to decide where human review remains mandatory... treating human review as an infallible reference would be its own mistake: without measuring how much reviewers agree with each other in the first place, a team cannot tell whether an apparent verifier–human disagreement reflects a real verifier failure or simply the reviewers' own unresolved disagreement about what the card requires."

**Method** (Stage 5, summary-level): "We stratify verifier scoring by constraint category rather than reporting one aggregate number; measure the human–human agreement ceiling explicitly via a multi-reviewer adjudicated panel rather than assuming a perfect reference; route intent-laden constraints to a calibrated, abstention-capable LLM judge and structurally-checkable constraints to deterministic rule/AST or architecture-conformance checks, and use a budget-matched single-shot judge to test whether the observed category split is intrinsic to the constraints or an artifact of the routing mechanism; report a per-category precision-recall operating curve instead of a single threshold; and pre-screen the (card, patch) corpus for non-obviousness so results reflect genuine difficulty rather than corpus artifacts."

## 2. Taxonomy labels

**Opportunity Pattern:** primary **Evidence Gap** (secondary: **Failure/Risk Gap**)
- rationale: the motivation's load-bearing claim is "has not been measured" — a diagnosability absence about an existing governance practice (automated constraint gatekeeping), not a "two literatures never connected" framing. The secondary Failure/Risk Gap reading is licensed by the false-positive/false-negative adoption-risk language ("silently misses violations," "drowns reviewers in spurious flags," "quietly eroding the very design guarantees") — the worry that a governance mechanism already being relied on may be unreliable in an asymmetric, adoption-relevant way.
- Explicitly **not** Bridge Opportunity: there is no "X and Y have developed in isolation" clause anywhere in the motivation. `paper.md`'s own Step 3 self-audit independently checked this same question ("Is the opportunity a Bridge? PASS") before this audit ran, and this audit's independent read agrees: the opportunity is an unmeasured quantity gating a real operational decision (where to route human review effort), not a claim that two research areas need linking.

**Method Paradigm:** primary **Empirical Mapping** (secondary: **Formal Derivation**)
- rationale: "we stratify... measure... route... test... report... pre-screen" is measurement-instrument construction and characterization, not "integrate/unify two methods" as the paper's headline move. The secondary Formal Derivation reading comes from the automatable / human-required / reference-unstable / inconclusive **decision rule** itself (Stage 1's refined `objective` and `formal_summary`) being a conceptual, taxonomy-defining contribution in its own right, not merely a measurement pipeline.
- **This is the one place this audit does not simply rubber-stamp `paper.md`'s own Step-3 self-audit.** `paper.md` flagged its own method as a self-identified risk: *"Is the method mere Synthesis? RISK → CONTAINED — M3 is a hybrid (rule/AST + conformance + LLM-judge). Fix at the skeleton level: M3 is declared the measurement instrument, never the contribution."* That risk is real and worth taking at face value rather than accepting the paper's own "contained" verdict uncritically — see the `surface_stitching` score below, where this audit independently arrives at a non-zero (rather than a clean 0) score for exactly this reason, before applying the verdict logic to see whether it still clears the bar.

## 3. Diagnostics

- `surface_stitching` = **1** — not a clean 0. The verifier sub-mechanism inside `m_intent_routing_with_abstention` genuinely is three pre-existing technique types (rule/AST matching, Reflexion-style architecture conformance, LLM-as-judge) assigned to categories by a routing rule — this is closer to "combination" than a from-scratch single mechanism, and it would be dishonest to score this a 0 just because `paper.md`'s Step 3 already declared the risk "contained." What keeps it at 1 rather than 2–3: the routing rule itself is not just gluing — it is explicitly built to be *falsified* (the budget-matched B-alt invariance test in `rq_instrument_invariance` exists precisely to check whether the routing/decomposition mechanism, as opposed to the constraints' intrinsic operationalizability, is doing the work), which is a real mechanism-level addition beyond "we combine X and Y." A textbook 3 would report the hybrid's aggregate score and stop there; this design instead treats the hybrid's own construction as something to be checked for confounding, not simply presented as a synthesis win.
- `bottleneck_specificity` = **3** — the target is exact and nameable at every step: *which* five categories, *which* precision/recall/F1 floors, *which* κ-gap CI threshold (0.10), *which* violation-count floor (30/category), *which* non-obviousness pre-screen criterion. Matches the source paper's human baseline (2.56) and its strongest tested models (2.3–2.6).
- `boilerplate` = **1** — mostly load-bearing; the one soft clause is "coding agents produce patches faster than teams can manually review them," which is a fairly generic AI-velocity framing, though it is immediately cashed out into the specific, non-generic claim that follows ("automated constraint verification is being treated as viable delegated gatekeeping... has not been measured").

## 4. Dominant verb(s)

Method sentence's main verbs: **stratify, measure, route, isolate (via the invariance test), report, pre-screen**. `isolate` is explicitly in the human-heavy archetype cluster (with *decouple, separate, disentangle*); `stratify`, `measure`, `report`, and `pre-screen` are neutral, task-specific verbs, not connecting verbs. The word "hybrid" and "combines" appear only in the *internal description* of one sub-mechanism (`m_intent_routing_with_abstention`'s verifier construction), never as the main verb of the paper-level method sentence or the abstract's contribution claim. No instance of *integrate/unify/merge/adapt/combine/leverage* appears in a claim position at the paper level.
Archetype family: **human-heavy / neutral**, with one internal sub-mechanism description (not the headline claim) carrying the one soft "combine" note flagged in the `surface_stitching` score above.

## 6. Interpretation (why this matters for "avoiding homogeneous output")

Two things are worth separating. First, `paper.md`'s own Step 3 self-audit already ran an informal version of this same check before this audit started, and largely agrees with this audit's opportunity-axis read (not Bridge). Second, and more useful: this audit does **not** simply defer to `paper.md`'s "RISK → CONTAINED" verdict on the method axis. It independently re-derives why the containment holds — the hybrid verifier is explicitly instrumented to test its own confound (`rq_instrument_invariance`), not merely asserted to be an instrument rather than a contribution — and it registers that containment as a non-zero `surface_stitching` score rather than a 0, which is the more defensible number given what the method sentence actually says.

The practical takeaway for calibrating expectations elsewhere in this repository: a topic whose own upstream self-audit (`paper.md`'s Step 3, where present) reports a "contained risk" rather than a clean pass is exactly the kind of case where an independent audit earns its keep — it is cheap here to confirm the containment holds under a second, externally-sourced taxonomy rather than accept the topic's own self-assessment at face value, and in this case it does hold, but the audit trail for *why* is worth keeping rather than collapsing into a bare 🟢.
