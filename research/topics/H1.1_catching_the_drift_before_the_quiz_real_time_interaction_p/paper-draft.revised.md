# Catching the Drift Before the Quiz: Prefix-Only Detection and Placebo-Controlled Just-in-Time Intervention Against Skill-Eroding AI Interaction Patterns

*Revised draft v2 — 2026-08-14. Supersedes the autodiscussion package draft; unifies the formal problem definition with the experiment design, restores the four-arm internal replication, the decision-analytic gate, and the five-lane related-work structure mandated by discussion.md.*

## Abstract

AI coding assistance can accelerate task completion while weakening the comprehension developers need to supervise generated code. A randomized controlled trial of 52 developers learning an unfamiliar Python library (Shen & Tamkin, arXiv:2601.20245) showed AI-assisted participants scoring 17 points lower on a post-task concept and debugging quiz (50% vs. 67%, d = 0.738), and identified six interaction patterns — three delegation-style patterns scoring below 40% and three comprehension-seeking patterns scoring 65–86%. Those patterns, however, were labeled in hindsight from complete sessions; nothing in the literature tests whether the drift toward an erosive pattern is detectable in the behavior stream early enough to act on. We propose a gated, two-stage causal extension of that RCT. Stage 1 (offline) learns a prognostic risk score $r_k$ — the probability that a session, if uninterrupted, terminates in an erosive pattern — from query-stream prefixes only (no model internals, no biometrics), validated with developer-disjoint cross-fitting against the human prefix-labeling ceiling and prevalence baselines. A frozen model and cutoff are then evaluated once on a sealed gate set; a live study is authorized only if a conservative confidence bound on alert precision clears a prevalence-anchored, decision-analytically derived threshold. Stage 2 (live, gate-conditional) is a preregistered four-arm trial — hand-code, unnotified AI assistance, placebo-triggered notification, and an actively triggered one-shot additive comprehension nudge — that internally replicates the published hand-vs-AI gap, isolates nudge content from notification reactivity, and preserves the original quiz rubric with blinded grading. The design serializes the compound bet: a gate failure still yields the first characterization of how early erosive patterns are visible, while a gate pass yields the first closed JITAI-style loop whose sensed state is an AI-interaction pattern and whose distal outcome is skill formation.

## 1. Introduction

AI coding assistants are now routinely used to complete tasks involving unfamiliar libraries, creating a tension between immediate task progress and the formation of the knowledge needed to evaluate, debug, and maintain the resulting code. The causal fact is no longer in dispute for at least one setting: in Shen & Tamkin's randomized trial, 52 developers with Python experience but no exposure to the Trio async library completed tasks with or without AI assistance, and the AI-assisted arm scored 17 points lower on a subsequent conceptual, code-reading, and debugging quiz, without significant average time savings. Crucially, the harm was not uniform: post-hoc qualitative coding of session recordings identified six interaction patterns, and the outcome tracked the pattern, not AI use per se — full delegation, progressive reliance, and iterative AI debugging scored below 40%, while generate-then-ask, hybrid code+explanation queries, and purely conceptual questioning scored 65–86%.

A pattern that predicts the outcome and is visible in behavior is an invitation to detect and intervene. Yet every existing measurement arrives after the fact: the base RCT labels complete recordings; observational work detects an "AI-prompt signature" only retrospectively and at cohort level; survey and EEG studies measure downstream states, not the interaction stream. If erosive drift is only nameable after the quiz, assistants can do nothing about it at the moment it matters. The open question — stated by the base paper's framing but attempted by no one — is whether the early behavioral signature of an erosive session is recognizable from a partial session prefix, and whether acting on that recognition preserves learning.

Two failure modes make this harder than it looks. First, the pattern taxonomy is a hindsight summary of whole sessions: a learner may be genuinely pattern-indeterminate at query three. The detector must therefore estimate a *prognostic risk* — the probability that the session, if uninterrupted, terminates in an erosive pattern — not a diagnostic label, and its ceiling is an empirical question we measure directly by asking human annotators to label prefixes blind to session endings. Second, an intervention triggered by detection can "work" for the wrong reasons: interruption alone changes behavior (notification reactivity), and an intervention that degrades the assistant merely re-derives the hand-coding arm. Our design controls both, with a matched placebo notification and preregistered task-completion guardrails.

We therefore treat detection and intervention as a single, evidence-gated procedure with three properties. (i) **Prefix-only sensing**: the risk model reads only what a logging proxy sees — query text, timestamps, response text, paste/error events — never model internals or biometrics, so the instrument is deployable by any party, including auditors who do not own the model. (ii) **A sealed statistical gate**: the model, cutoff, and decision table are frozen and hashed before a sealed set of sessions is scored once; the live study opens only if a conservative lower confidence bound on alert precision clears a threshold derived from the observed erosive base rate and an explicit annoyance budget — not an arbitrary constant. (iii) **A four-arm live trial that carries its own validity check**: hand-code and unnotified-AI arms internally replicate the published 50%/67% contrast before the intervention contrast is interpreted, and a placebo-triggered arm separates nudge content from reactivity.

This program is deliberately serialized. If the gate fails, the offline stage still delivers the first quantification of how early erosive patterns are humanly and statistically visible — a publishable characterization with direct design implications. If the gate passes, the live trial delivers the first closed just-in-time loop where the tailoring variable is an AI-interaction pattern and the distal outcome is measured skill formation, on an instrument directly comparable to the published baseline.

## 2. Contributions

1. **A prognostic, prefix-only risk model for skill-eroding AI interaction**, with leakage-controlled developer-disjoint validation, evaluated against both prevalence baselines and a measured *human prefix-labeling ceiling* — itself a new scientific quantity (how early are these patterns visible at all?).
2. **A decision-analytic, sealed-set authorization gate** that converts small-sample alert precision into an auditable go/no-go rule (prevalence-anchored confidence bound plus an explicit annoyance budget), pre-registered as the ethical precondition for any live intervention.
3. **A gate-conditional, four-arm randomized evaluation** that simultaneously (a) internally replicates the published hand-vs-AI skill-formation gap, (b) estimates the effect of a one-shot additive comprehension nudge, and (c) isolates nudge content from notification reactivity via a timing-, length-, and presentation-matched placebo.
4. **Scoped novelty**: we import, and credit, the JITAI vocabulary (tailoring variable, proximal/distal outcomes) and the intelligent-tutoring closed-loop tradition; the contribution is the transplant of that loop to the setting where the assistant itself is the erosion vector, sensed through zero-hardware query-log features.

## 3. Problem Definition

Let a session be an ordered query stream $Q_i = (q_{i1}, \ldots, q_{iT_i})$ produced by developer $i$ working with an AI assistant on an unfamiliar-library task, and let $L_i \in \{0,1\}$ be the hindsight label (erosive-terminal vs. protective-terminal) assigned by the base paper's published coding protocol to the complete session. For prefix length $k$, write $Q_i^{(k)} = (q_{i1}, \ldots, q_{ik})$.

**Stage 1 (offline, detection).** Learn $f: Q^{(k)} \to [0,1]$ producing a calibrated prognostic risk $r_{ik} = f(Q_i^{(k)}) \approx P(L_i = 1 \mid Q_i^{(k)})$, using only prefix-observable features (no model internals, no future queries, no full-session statistics). With a cutoff $c$, the alert is $\hat{L}_{ik} = \mathbb{1}[r_{ik} \geq c]$. Evaluation reports precision and recall at prefix lengths $k \in \{1,3,5,10\}$ queries and at elapsed-time checkpoints $\{10, 20, 30\}$ minutes, against (a) the prevalence baseline and (b) the human prefix ceiling: the agreement of annotators who label the same prefixes blind to session endings.

**Gate.** Let $\hat{p}$ be the erosive-label prevalence on development data and let precision be computed on a sealed, developer-disjoint gate set scored once by the frozen $(f, c)$ at the half-session checkpoint. The live study is authorized iff
$$G = \mathbb{1}\left[\underline{\pi}_{90} \geq \hat{p} + 0.10 \;\wedge\; a \geq 5 \;\wedge\; \text{FPB} \leq 0.3\right] = 1,$$
where $\underline{\pi}_{90}$ is the one-sided 90% Clopper–Pearson lower bound on gate-set alert precision, $a$ the number of alerted sessions, and FPB the false-positive burden — expected alerts per protective-terminal session (the annoyance budget). The design *target* precision remains 0.70, retained only if the base-rate decision analysis endorses it; the gate itself is anchored to measured prevalence, not to a constant.

**Stage 2 (live, intervention; only if $G=1$).** Participants are randomized to four concurrent arms $A_j \in \{H, U, P, N\}$: hand-code ($H$), unnotified AI assistance ($U$), placebo-trigger ($P$), and active-trigger ($N$). The frozen classifier runs silently in all AI arms; in $P$ and $N$, the first cutoff crossing after a floor of 3 queries triggers, at most once per session, either a matched neutral notification ($P$) or a one-shot *additive* comprehension nudge ($N$) — the assistant's full answer plus a targeted comprehension question; nothing is withheld. With $Y_j$ the blinded, original-rubric quiz score and $\mu_a = \mathbb{E}[Y_j \mid A_j = a]$, the preregistered estimands are:

- **Replication (validity precondition):** $\Delta_{\text{rep}} = \mu_U - \mu_H$, expected negative, directionally replicating the published 50%/67% gap;
- **Primary:** $\Delta_{\text{content}} = \mu_N - \mu_P$ (nudge content beyond reactivity), intention-to-treat and within the pre-trigger flagged subgroup;
- **Secondary:** $\Delta_{\text{react}} = \mu_P - \mu_U$ (notification reactivity) and $\Delta_{\text{policy}} = \mu_N - \mu_U$ (total policy effect);
- **Process endpoints:** detection lag (query index and wall-clock of first alert) and preregistered guardrails — task completion and time-on-task in $N$ must remain within 10% of $U$.

**Interpretation tree (preregistered).** If $\Delta_{\text{rep}}$ fails to replicate directionally, all intervention contrasts are reported as estimation-only and the replication failure is itself a headline finding; the intervention is claimed effective only if the replication holds, guardrails are clean, and $\Delta_{\text{content}} > 0$ with its preregistered bound.

## 4. Challenges

**C1 — Label transfer from hindsight to prefix (the hard part).** Ground truth is a whole-session hindsight label; training a prefix model against it presumes the terminal label was "already true" early, which is false for learners who drift or self-correct. We attack this three ways: reframing the target as terminal-pattern *risk* (so indeterminacy is probability mass, not label noise); measuring the human prefix ceiling on a 30-session subset (the honest denominator for any classifier, and a reportable result); and a prefix-time validity check that flagged-at-$k$ sessions differ in *subsequent* behavior.

**C2 — Thin features.** The taxonomy came from rich qualitative reading; a logging proxy sees far less. Two-tier featurization (deterministic log features; LLM-tagged query intents with a frozen prompt and a 15% human audit at $\kappa \geq 0.75$) plus a deterministic-only ablation answers how thin the signal can be rather than assuming it.

**C3 — Small-$n$ inference.** With tens of sessions, point estimates of precision are noise. All development inference is cluster-bootstrap over developers with the full pipeline re-run; the gate uses an exact binomial bound on a sealed set scored once; the six-way pattern classification is demoted to a descriptive appendix (the scientific unit is the erosive/protective binary, which is also what the intervention needs).

**C4 — Interventions that "work" for the wrong reason.** Interruption alone changes behavior; withholding answers re-derives the hand-coding arm. Hence: additive-only nudges, one per session, matched placebo, and completion/time guardrails — an effect that survives all three is attributable to content acting on an ongoing session.

**C5 — Non-stationarity of the loop.** A fired nudge changes the subsequent feature stream. The one-nudge-per-session cap bounds this in the pilot; micro-randomized timing designs are deferred to scale-up.

## 5. Method

*Figure 1: System overview. Interaction telemetry (queries, timestamps, responses, paste/error events) is converted into prefix features and a calibrated per-query risk score $r_k$. The model, cutoff, and decision table are frozen and hashed, then evaluated once on a sealed gate set; only a pass opens the preregistered four-arm live study (hand-code / unnotified AI / placebo-trigger / active nudge), validated by the original-rubric skill probe with blinded grading. IRB review and freeze-and-hash preregistration run as explicit gates in parallel with the offline stage.*

### 5.1 Data: dual-source plan, no single point of failure

**Source A (pursued, not depended on):** the base RCT's 52 session transcripts under a data-use agreement with the authors; request sent in week 1, answer deadline preregistered (2026-09-15).

**Source B (fallback, auto-starts on deadline):** fresh collection of 40–50 AI-assisted sessions under a lightweight transcript-collection IRB protocol (separate from, and faster than, the live-RCT protocol), on the reconstructed task; these sessions double as the internal replication's first data. Every deviation of reconstructed materials from the original task/quiz is documented.

All available sessions are pooled and split **by developer** into a development set (target ≥ 40 sessions) and a sealed gate set (≥ 20 sessions), via an outcome-blind blocked hash balancing task family, experience, and source. Two annotators code all sessions with the published six-pattern protocol ($\kappa \geq 0.7$ required on the erosive/protective binary; disputes adjudicated). This corrects the prior draft's 20-development/32-gate allocation, which starved the model and made the gate's precision requirement statistically unreachable.

### 5.2 Prefix-only risk model

Cumulative features at each prefix: counts and running ratios of complete-solution requests, explanation/testing requests, hybrid requests, and conceptual questions (tier-2 intent tags); code-paste-then-error-paste cycles (the iterative-debugging tell); follow-up-after-code presence; user-authored diagnostic detail; query count, length, inter-query gaps, elapsed time (tier-1 deterministic features). A session-weighted elastic-net logistic landmark model with Platt calibration estimates $r_k$ after each query; sessions contribute equal total weight regardless of prefix count. Hyperparameters and calibration are selected by nested leave-one-developer-out cross-fitting; no deep models at this $n$. The cutoff $c$ is selected on fold-train data only, targeting the gate's operating point subject to the annoyance budget.

### 5.3 Freeze, seal, and gate

After development analyses (RQ1–RQ2) are computed, one final $(f, c)$ is fitted on all development sessions and frozen: serialized, hashed into a manifest together with $\tau$, the feature specification, exclusion rules, and the fully enumerated $(a, t)$ pass table. The sealed gate sessions are then scored once at the half-session checkpoint. The gate criterion is the decision-analytic rule of §3: prevalence-anchored 90% lower bound, minimum alert count, annoyance budget. Zero alerts is a preregistered no-go. One re-gate is permitted after a preregistered narrowing (delegation-pattern-only detection); a second failure retires the real-time premise as scoped and redirects the topic to retrospective session-audit tooling.

### 5.4 Live four-arm trial (gate-conditional)

Randomization is stratified by screening-quiz score into $H/U/P/N$; AI-arm allocation is concealed from participants (debrief-based partial disclosure, IRB-approved). The classifier runs silently in $U$, $P$, $N$; risk at the trigger moment is frozen before any message is delivered, so the flagged subgroup is defined pre-treatment in all three AI arms. The active nudge is additive-only and template-bank-first: the assistant answers normally, then appends a comprehension question keyed to the just-delivered code, instantiated by an LLM from a vetted template bank with a deterministic fallback; 100% of pilot firings are audited for relevance and timing (intervention-integrity metrics). The placebo is matched in trigger policy, timing, length, and presentation, with content-free text. Quizzes are graded by two arm-blinded graders using the original rubric ($\kappa$ reported); analysis is ANCOVA with baseline covariates (experience, screening score), cluster-robust standard errors, multiple imputation (50 datasets, Rubin's rules) and delta-adjusted MNAR tipping-point sensitivity.

**Power and framing.** At $n = 30$/arm, the replication contrast has ~80% power for the published $d = 0.738$; the intervention contrast is powered only for large recovery effects, so the pilot is preregistered as **estimation plus gate** — report the confidence interval on $\Delta_{\text{content}}$ and proceed to scale-up iff the point estimate exceeds 40% of the base gap with guardrails clean — never as a confirmatory test of small effects. Scale-up ($n \approx 120\text{–}160$, micro-randomized timing) inherits the frozen machinery.

### 5.5 Ethics, IRB, and timeline

Two protocols, pipelined: (i) transcript collection (minimal risk; consented logging; needed only for Source B), submitted immediately; (ii) the live RCT (mild information asymmetry — participants know sessions are logged and an AI assistant is involved, not which arm or that nudges are algorithmically triggered until debrief), submitted 2026-11-15 for a Feb 2027 start, assuming 2–3-month expedited review [verify local IRB latency]. Recruitment: Prolific/Upwork, 1+ yr Python, no Trio experience, screening quiz with embedded familiarity traps; over-recruit 20% (144 recruited for 120 across four arms); compensation $100–150 per 2–2.5 h session. Budget envelope: $35–50k participants + annotation, ~6–8 person-months, negligible compute. Calendar: offline dataset + G0 (labels, $\kappa$) by 2026-10-31; classifier + human ceiling + gate G1 by 2027-01-15 (one re-gate absorbing to Mar 1; CHI 2028 slack covers it); freeze-and-hash + OSF preregistration before first live participant; pilot Feb–May 2027; G2 (replication + guardrails) 2027-05-31; scale-up Jun–Aug 2027; CHI 2028 submission Sep 2027, with CSCW/AIES as fallbacks. A data-free FAccT 2027 position form (deadline 2026-11-03) argues the normative case — skill erosion is invisible at decision time to the person incurring it, and post-hoc measurement structurally favors the vendor — using the published evidence base and this gated design as its constructive proposal; any early offline curves are a bonus appendix, never load-bearing.

## 6. Experiments

**RQ0 (ceiling).** How early are erosive patterns humanly visible? Annotators label 30 development-session prefixes at $k \in \{1,3,5,10\}$ blind to endings; agreement with terminal labels is the prefix ceiling, reported per $k$. *Metric:* human precision/recall vs. terminal labels. *Role:* upper reference for RQ1 and a standalone finding.

**RQ1 (detection).** Does the leakage-controlled procedure identify erosive-terminal sessions at half-session with useful precision? Nested LODO cross-fitting on development sessions; cluster-bootstrap (developers as resampling units, full pipeline re-run per replicate) lower bounds on precision vs. prevalence + 0.10 enrichment; success = enrichment bound met at $k \leq$ half the median session. *Metrics:* precision, recall, activation count, enrichment, at all $k$ and time checkpoints.

**RQ2 (probabilistic prognosis).** Do risk scores beat fold-train prevalence-only predictors on session-weighted Brier score at every locked checkpoint (10/20/30 min)? Success: relative Brier reduction bound > 0.10, Holm-adjusted. *Ablations:* deterministic-only features vs. + intent tags; regularization on/off; calibration on/off; checkpoint-by-index vs. by-time.

**RQ3 (gate).** Does the sealed gate set support authorization? Frozen $(f,c)$ scored once; decision by the preregistered $(a,t)$ pass table under the §3 rule. *Metrics:* alert count, true positives, precision, one-sided 90% Clopper–Pearson bound, FPB. Only a pass opens RQ4–RQ6.

**RQ4 (replication).** Does $\mu_U - \mu_H$ directionally replicate the published −17-point gap? Governs the interpretation tree; either outcome is informative given the base paper's prominence.

**RQ5 (intervention, primary).** Does the active nudge beat placebo on the original-rubric quiz? ITT and pre-trigger flagged-subgroup ANCOVA contrasts with MI + tipping-point sensitivity; guardrails (completion, time-on-task within 10% of $U$) must hold for an efficacy claim.

**RQ6 (reactivity & lag).** $\Delta_{\text{react}}$, $\Delta_{\text{policy}}$, detection-lag distribution, and the exploratory lag × benefit relation. Six-way pattern breakdowns are descriptive appendix material.

**Failure products (pre-committed).** G1 hard-fail → "How early are erosive AI-interaction patterns visible? A prefix-labeling ceiling study" (CSCW note, built from RQ0–RQ2). G2 replication-fail → "A preregistered replication of AI-assisted skill-formation effects" — valuable either way.

## 7. Related Work

**Lane 1 — the causal premise and its neighbors.** Shen & Tamkin (arXiv:2601.20245) provide the base RCT, taxonomy, quiz instrument, and the 50%/67% baseline; the labels are post-hoc codings of complete sessions, and the paper's own future work looks at longitudinal dissipation, not within-session detection. Kosmyna et al. (arXiv:2506.08872, preprint) corroborate the direction in essay writing via EEG, recall, and ownership measures — a different domain and instrument, cited as convergent direction only. Becker et al. (arXiv:2507.09089, METR) show experienced maintainers were *slowed* by early-2025 AI tools on real issues — a productivity outcome on experts, contextual here: it undercuts "AI assistance is an unqualified productivity win" but says nothing about learning or prefixes. Aouad, Lykouris & Zhong (arXiv:2605.11350) supply the theoretical "why early matters": heterogeneous AI literacy compounds into skill polarization, and if the compounding runs through early interaction differences, early intervention is the lever the model predicts (their model, our motivating inference).

**Lane 2 — behavioral signatures of AI reliance.** Yao (arXiv:2606.06253) detects an "AI-prompt signature" (high first-attempt acceptance, few debug retries) in competitive-programming platform data and shows AI-prohibited gates screen for durable skill — the closest existence proof that the signal lives in behavior, but observational, cohort-level, retrospective, and without a closed loop. Cao & Huang (PACMHCI CSCW2 2022; verification incomplete — ACM DL inaccessible this cycle) find a real-time behavioral proxy (gaze) beats retrospective self-report for reliance; different sensor, different outcome — whether zero-hardware query-log features match gaze-grade signal is exactly our C2 risk, untested by anyone.

**Lane 3 — JITAI: the loop template, credited.** The mobile-health JITAI literature (Nahum-Shani et al., Health Psychology 2018; Klasnja et al.'s micro-randomized HeartSteps trials [verify exact refs]) formalized decision points, tailoring variables, proximal vs. distal outcomes, and timing-randomization. No JITAI work senses AI-interaction patterns or targets skill formation; we import the vocabulary — tailoring variable $r_k$, proximal outcome = post-nudge pattern shift, distal outcome = quiz score — rather than reinventing the loop, and scope our novelty accordingly.

**Lane 4 — appropriate reliance and cognitive forcing.** Buçinca et al.'s cognitive forcing functions [verify ref] show interruption-based nudges can improve decision quality at a UX cost — the nearest intervention class with RCT evidence, and the direct precedent for our placebo worry: their effects partly run through friction, which is precisely what our matched placebo isolates. The automation-bias/appropriate-reliance tradition (Lee & See) frames why delegation is the product's happy path.

**Lane 5 — ITS and learner modeling; and the design foil.** Intelligent tutoring systems have run closed-loop learner modeling for decades (cognitive tutors; Bayesian knowledge tracing, Corbett & Anderson 1995) — but they model mastery of curricular skills inside purpose-built tutors. Nobody has ported that loop to AI-assistant usage patterns during authentic work, where the "tutor" is the same tool causing the erosion; naming this lineage preempts "knowledge tracing, rediscovered." Finally, Agents-That-Teach (Mehra et al., arXiv:2607.06101) is the current design-principles answer — build incidental learning back into AI-assisted development via pedagogical scaffolding applied open-loop, to all users, unconditioned on any measured learner state. That stance taxes users already in protective patterns and cannot tell whether its principles are working mid-session. We are the closed-loop complement: measure first, intervene conditionally, verify on the same quiz that established the harm; if their designs turn out adaptive, we supply the measurement layer their adaptivity would require.

## 8. Limitations and Threats

The base effect rests on one lab and $n = 52$; our replication arm re-tests rather than assumes it, and a replication failure is preregistered as a headline outcome. The human prefix ceiling may be low — if humans cannot call the pattern from half a session, no classifier can; the ceiling is therefore a primary measurement, not a nuisance. Crowd-recruited populations may drift from the base paper's; screening traps and documented deviations bound, but do not remove, this comparability threat. The pilot cannot separate comprehension mechanisms from engagement mechanisms beyond the placebo contrast; mechanism claims are capped at "a content effect exists." The setup inherits the base paper's own caveat that it differs from agentic coding products; results are a likely lower bound for settings with richer delegation affordances. ## 9. Conclusion

Skill erosion under AI assistance is real, pattern-mediated, and — so far — visible only after the fact. This paper's wager is that the drift is catchable in the query stream before the quiz, and that catching it is only worth claiming under discipline: prefix-only sensing anyone can deploy, a sealed decision-analytic gate before any human is intervened on, a placebo that eats the reactivity story, and an internal replication that keeps the baseline honest. Either branch of the gate produces knowledge the field lacks: how early the patterns are visible, or whether a one-shot, additive, just-in-time nudge can buy back part of a 17-point comprehension loss without taxing the users who never needed it.
