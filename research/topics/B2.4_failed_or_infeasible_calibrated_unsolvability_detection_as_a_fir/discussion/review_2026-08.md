### Framing-strip check (framing firewall)

Claim 1 wears "identification and formalization" framing; stripped, the surviving object is the B0-a label-restricted control plus the formal statement that the counterfactual-correction estimand is undefined on certified-unsolvable instances — a real object, but the *dichotomy itself* is prior art (AIProbe), so 🟡 not 🟢. ### Overall novelty stance

**🟢 overall, earned on the estimand row, with mandatory concessions on the framing and construction rows.** The combination that carries the paper — *third-party* false-blame/cop-out rates of deployed LLM-agent attribution systems on *certificate-certified* unsolvable instances in *matched pairs*, plus the design-vs-ceiling dissociation (claim 3) — was verified absent from every checked neighbor, including the entire 2026 attribution wave (all ten checked systems presuppose a culpable step; Who&When Pro's 12,326 trajectories guarantee one by injection). But two flanks the draft treated as empty are now occupied: AIProbe owns the agent-vs-environment attribution dichotomy (for non-LLM agents, planner-referenced), and FeasiGen owns the mask-a-critical-tool construction (first-party, human-verified). Honest one-sentence delta: **relative to AIProbe (nearest framing) and FeasiGen (nearest construction), this paper's contribution is measuring whether the *attribution systems the LLM-agent field actually deploys* recover certificate-grade unsolvability from traces, in matched pairs, with a calibration-boundary and design-vs-ceiling readout — not the discovery that tasks can be infeasible.**

## 2. Feasibility review (se-paper-reviewer)

**Verdict: FEASIBLE at the stated budget class, with an honest and unusually complete power/validity story.**

- **Compute/cost.** Pilot: 100 pairs × 2 agents ≈ 400 runs + ~400 judge calls → $150–500 class. Scale-up: 600 pairs (1,200 instances) × 3 agents = 3,600 runs, ~15–20k attribution calls → $2k–5k class; optional tracer fine-tune <100 A100-hours. These are consistent with the per-call arithmetic and with current API pricing; nothing hidden.
- **Annotation.** 20–40 annotator-hours (10% pair audit, ≥2 raters, κ≥0.70 gate) — the only human labeling; ground truth is otherwise script-certified, which is the design's whole economy.
- **Power arithmetic.** Present and honest: the CoR denominator's dependence on agent competence is worked (failed-solvable n ≈ 180–1,530 under A3's t⁺ ∈ [0.15, 0.90]; power > 0.9 only when n ≳ 400; under-powered cells pre-declared INCONCLUSIVE, never pooled as nulls). This survives scrutiny.
- **Assumption load.** Heaviest: no-substitute decidability outside SQL. Schema-graph reachability (Spider/BIRD) is genuinely decidable; τ-bench/Terminal-Bench certification rests on restricting operators to a closed sub-language and excluding non-certifiable instances — workable, but per-domain oracle bring-up (the draft's PG-gates at 20-pair scale) is correctly on the critical path. Second heaviest: the leakage gate (text-only probe AUROC < 0.60 after one repair) — pre-registered with a positive-control power check, and F-G2 makes a total failure publishable. Both risks are owned, not hidden.
- **Minimum-viable kill-test.** Exists: the 20-pair, 1-domain, ~$5–20 walking skeleton (paper.md Step 9) kills the project cheaply if the oracle or the leakage gate fails at toy scale.
- **Feasibility tailwinds since drafting.** FeasiGen demonstrates automatic critical-tool identification works at high label accuracy (94% human-verified) — de-risks the tool-domain mutation operator; Who&When Pro demonstrates injection-at-scale (12,326 trajectories) is practical engineering.
