## Stage 4 — "Experiments" (Analysis/Finding variant: falsification, not correlation)

**STAGE 4 VERDICT: REVISE** *(fired, fixed — see below)*

**Why, quoting the draft:** The one question this stage asks in every archetype — is the claimed cause isolated from its strongest alternative — is well-served structurally (RQ6 is a genuine falsification design: recomputing the selective-allocation gain under realistic, non-oracle verifiers specifically to see whether it collapses, not just whether it correlates with the oracle result; RQ2 is likewise a falsification design, testing whether confident-but-wrong instances are under-selected at *below*-chance rates rather than merely reporting an aggregate AUROC). But the first assembled draft's RQ subsections stopped at an "**Expected outcome**" clause without ever stating the converse — what specific result would *refute* that expectation. Per the Analysis/Finding bar: "the strongest finding papers construct a case where, if the claim were false, the result would differ... demand at least one such test" stated as a check, not implied. An expected outcome alone reads as a hope; a stated falsification condition is what makes it a pre-registered, breakable claim — and the underlying `pipeline.md`/`paper.md` material already had this discipline (`paper.md`'s Step 6 fallback wording, and digest N9's explicit per-result falsification conditions) that had not yet been carried into the first assembled `paper_draft.md`.

**THE ONE FIX (applied):** Add an explicit **Falsification condition** line to all seven RQ subsections, stating the specific observation that would refute each expected outcome — grounded in the concrete statistical thresholds already pre-registered in `paper.md`'s Step 6 (e.g., RQ1's chance-level AUROC threshold, RQ6's damaging-if-it-happens-under-execution-verification note) and digest N9's falsification conditions, not a vague restatement of the outcome's negation.
