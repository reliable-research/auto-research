# Axis-Selective Breakage: Measuring Which Behavioral Probes Predict LLM Version-Drift Failures

> Assembled from the six confirmed stage outputs in `pipeline.md`, post-audit (`taste_audit.md`: 🟢). Status: **specification / paper draft — no experiments have been run.** All numbers below are pre-registered design targets, not results.

## Abstract

Teams that build on a hosted or self-hosted large language model must decide, every time a new version ships, what to re-test — yet whether a cheap per-version probe actually forecasts real breakage depends on which behavioral axis it probes, not on how much the update changed overall. We show that the decisive part of this question is still open: a recent black-box change-detection line can already tell *whether* a model changed across a version pair, and single-axis instruments measure refusal boundaries, calibration, or safety scope precisely at one point in time — but none of them connects axis-resolved drift to a downstream-task breakage ground truth, so none says which axis, if any, is worth watching. We build a retrospective auditing pipeline that administers five behavioral-probe families — capability, refusal-boundary, format-preference, calibration, and safety-behavior — identically to both versions of already-shipped version pairs, jointly regresses functionally-defined downstream breakage on all five drift scores to identify which axis, if any, governs failure net of the others, disentangles the variance behavioral axes share via partial correlation, gates every reported drift against a within-version noise floor and paraphrase-set rank-stability, and ablates toward the smallest family subset that retains most of the full battery's predictive power within a pre-registered margin. We evaluate this design on retrospective open-weight and dated-API version pairs against sanity-check baselines, including a no-probe base rate and a family-agnostic total-drift scalar, and report which axis, if any, is worth continuously auditing after every version bump.

## Introduction

Large language model providers ship new versions routinely, and every team with a downstream integration built on top of one faces the same practical choice each time: re-test everything, or trust a cheaper signal that a new version probably still behaves the same way it used to. The second option is attractive because comprehensive re-testing is expensive and repeats with every version, every provider, and every integration a team maintains — so a small, fast probe run once per version, rather than a full downstream re-test, is the only auditing strategy that scales with how often models actually change.

Yet a central question about this practice remains unexamined: a probe is only useful if the axis it measures is the axis that actually governs whether a downstream integration breaks, and that is not something teams currently have any evidence for. A team might re-test raw capability because that is the most familiar benchmark to run, while the update that actually breaks their pipeline shifted a refusal boundary or a safety-scope decision the capability probe never touches. Existing instruments make each individual axis measurable with real precision, but none of them says which axis, among several plausible candidates, is the one worth watching — and worse, several axes tend to drift together, so even a team that probes more than one axis has no principled way to tell which one is doing the real predictive work.

The nearest existing work splits across two recent lines, and this paper's question sits between them. A black-box change-detection line already compares model behavior across version pairs without weight access: longitudinal evidence that hosted-model behavior drifts across dated snapshots [ChenDrift2307], statistical audits of behavioral shift and silent endpoint modification [AuditBehaviorShift2410; YouveChanged2504; RankUniformity2506; ModelEquality2410; LogProbTracking2512; ModelSubstitution2504], multi-behavior fingerprints of endpoint stability and identity [BehavioralFingerprints2603], and construct-specific cross-release audits such as a bias-similarity signature [BiasSimBSM2607]. Its estimand, however, is *whether the model changed* — none of these audits validates its change signal against a downstream-breakage ground truth, or asks which behavioral axis carries the predictive signal. An update-governance line answers the breakage question from the other end, by re-running direct downstream tests on every update [TestBeforeDeploy2604; SkillDriftContract2605] — exactly the per-bump cost a cheap probe protocol exists to avoid, and one that carries no cheap-probe prediction layer. Complementary instruments make individual axes measurable with real precision: a rank-invariant refusal metric [RefusalIndex2607], a semantic notion of calibration native to free-form generation [BCalibration2607], an accept/refuse test for whether an agent stays inside its intended scope [OffTopicEval2607], and a documented account of systematic false refusals on harmless requests [ProSafePrune2607]. Each is a strong single-axis diagnostic evaluated at one point in time; none is compared against another axis, or against a real downstream-task outcome, across a version pair. A related line of work selects a minimal subset of benchmark items that preserves a score or a ranking under one fixed metric [EssenceBench2607] — the same "how small can the sufficient set be" question this paper asks, but applied to preserving fidelity to an existing score rather than to predicting a separate downstream target. And a study of paraphrase sensitivity shows that point-wise, single-phrasing measurements are brittle enough to flip a reported result [TARE2607] — a warning this paper has to take seriously about its own drift measurements, not just about the prompts other papers optimize.

Turning this gap into a measurable question surfaces genuine difficulties. A version pair can drift on several behavioral axes at once with no ground truth saying a priori which axis governs a specific downstream failure, and any attempt to credit one axis risks simply crediting whatever it happens to share with a correlated axis. Downstream "breakage" itself is not a single well-defined quantity: the same raw output difference can be a harmless rewording for one task and a fatal structural violation for another, so a definition that does not check task-specific functional consequences will conflate the two. A retrospective design needs version pairs that remain reliably queryable on demand, but providers offer no guarantee of stable historical access, so a corpus assembled by best effort alone risks silently shrinking toward whichever versions happen to still be reachable. And any reported drift-to-breakage correlation is only as trustworthy as the probe items it was computed from — a correlation that appears under one phrasing and disappears under another says more about instrument choice than about the model being audited.

This paper turns each of those obstacles into a specific design decision. Rather than computing five separate correlations and reading off whichever looks largest, all five families' drift scores enter one joint model at once, so the question of which axis governs breakage and the question of whether any axis carries real signal at all come from a single, shared fit instead of five uncoordinated tests. Downstream breakage is defined per task, from functional checks the task actually depends on, before any pooling across tasks — so a harmless rewording and a broken output are never scored as equivalent just because they moved the same number of characters. The retrospective corpus is built around an explicit reachability gate rather than best-effort collection, and pairs lost to deprecation during the study are checked directly for whether they differ systematically from the pairs that survived. Each family's own marginal contribution is separated from what it merely shares with the other four via a collinearity-checked partial correlation, so a correlated bundle of axes cannot be mistaken for one axis doing all the work. And every reported drift is required to clear a within-version noise floor and to keep the same family ranking across independently reworded probe sets before it is trusted at all.

We evaluate this design on retrospective version pairs spanning immutable open-weight checkpoint successions and dated API snapshots, comparing the resulting family-level predictions against a battery of sanity-check comparators — a no-probe base-rate predictor, a family-agnostic total-drift scalar matched on query budget, single-family predictors, and an item-subset compression baseline scoped to the capability family alone — to establish not just a ranking, but whether that ranking is real, isolated from a confound, and small enough to run continuously.

In summary, we contribute:
- A retrospective measurement design that identifies, from one joint model rather than five uncoordinated correlations, which behavioral-probe family's drift — if any — predicts real downstream breakage across LLM version pairs, together with an explicit test for whether probe drift carries any signal at all.
- A functionally-grounded definition of downstream breakage, computed per task from schema-validity and executability checks before cross-task pooling, that keeps a harmless rewording from being scored the same as a structural failure.
- A collinearity-checked attribution procedure that separates a family's own marginal contribution to the breakage prediction from what it merely shares with the other four, plus a paired noise-floor-and-paraphrase gate that a reported drift-to-breakage correlation must clear before it counts.
- A retrospective corpus built around an explicit reachability gate, with a direct test for whether version pairs lost to provider-side deprecation during the study differ systematically from the pairs that remained queryable throughout.
- An empirical account of which probe family, or minimal subset of families, is worth running continuously as a version-drift audit protocol for LLM-backed integrations.

## Problem Definition

**Formal setup.** Consider a version pair p = (v_old, v_new), p ∈ P, drawn from already-occurred model updates. A probe battery spans five behavioral families F = {cap, ref, fmt, cal, saf} — capability, refusal-boundary, format-preference, calibration, and safety-behavior — each with an item set I_f administered identically to both versions of every pair. A downstream-task suite T, with a shared input set X_t for each task t ∈ T, is likewise run through both versions. The unit of analysis is the cell (p, t). For each family f and pair p, D_f(p) denotes a signed drift-magnitude score computed from I_f's outputs under v_old versus v_new. For each cell (p, t), B(p, t) ∈ [0, 1] denotes downstream breakage, and B̄(p) = mean_t B(p, t) is the pair-level breakage average. The object of study is π_f, the **out-of-sample** predictive power of D_f(p) for B̄(p) across P — estimated by grouped leave-one-pair-out evaluation, with pairs from the same model lineage always assigned to the same fold so that no pair is ever scored by a model fit on its own lineage-mates' update events — and the minimal-sufficient subset S* ⊆ F such that a breakage classifier trained on S*'s drift features achieves held-out AUC(S*) ≥ AUC(F) − δ for a pre-specified margin δ, with both AUCs computed on held-out pairs under the same grouped scheme. In-sample fits are diagnostics, never the estimand: a π_f read off the same pairs used to fit the model and select S* would be validated against the very drift events it claims to predict.

**Why this is hard.** A version pair's drift is not confined to one axis: several families can move together on the same update, so a raw correlation between one family's drift and B̄(p) cannot distinguish that family's own contribution from whatever it merely shares with a correlated family — attributing predictive power requires isolating each family's marginal signal, not just observing that a signal exists somewhere in the bundle. Downstream breakage B(p, t) is not a single well-behaved quantity either: the same magnitude of output difference can leave one task's functional behavior untouched while breaking another task outright, so a definition based on raw output distance rather than task-specific functional consequence would conflate genuinely different outcomes under one score. Because P is retrospective, both versions of every included pair must remain queryable on the full I_f ∪ X_t input set under identical decoding — a condition providers do not guarantee, so a corpus built by best-effort collection risks quietly losing exactly the pairs most informative for a given family, biasing P itself before any analysis begins. And because D_f(p) is computed from a specific choice of probe items and decoding samples, any measured correlation between D_f(p) and B̄(p) is only as trustworthy as that choice is stable — a family ranking that reorders under an equally valid rewording of the same probes reflects an artifact of instrument choice, not a property of the versions being audited.

## Method Overview

At a conceptual level, the study is a single measurement pipeline with five cooperating parts, built around one central move: instead of computing five independent family-drift-to-breakage correlations and reading off whichever looks largest, every family's drift score enters one shared analysis at once, so questions about which axis matters, whether any axis matters, and whether a reported correlation is real rather than an artifact of collinearity, noise, phrasing, or corpus attrition are all answered from the same coordinated instrument rather than five uncoordinated ad hoc checks.

The **joint family-drift regression** takes the five families' per-pair drift scores and regresses the downstream-breakage average on all of them simultaneously, reading off both which family's coefficient is largest and whether any coefficient is distinguishable from zero at all. The **functional downstream ground truth** module takes each pair's outputs on the shared downstream-task suite and turns them into a per-task, per-pair breakage score using functional checks specific to each task, before any cross-task pooling. The **attrition-gated retrospective corpus** module governs which version pairs are even admitted to the study, requiring a reachability check before any pair contributes data, and directly testing whether pairs later lost to deprecation differ from the ones that survived. The **collinearity-aware partial attribution** module takes the same five drift scores and computes each family's contribution net of the other four, flagging when a family's raw correlation and its disentangled contribution disagree. The **noise-and-paraphrase stability gate** takes every reported drift score and requires it to clear a within-version noise floor and to preserve the same family ranking across independently reworded probe sets before that score is allowed to inform any of the other four modules' conclusions. All headline conclusions these modules produce are additionally scored under a **held-out-pair evaluation and axis-matching guard** (Methodology, final subsection): predictive claims are never read off the same pairs they were fit on, and never off probe–task cells that share the same behavioral axis.

## Methodology

### Joint Family-Drift Regression

**Challenge.** A version pair can drift on several behavioral axes at once, and a raw, one-family-at-a-time correlation between that family's drift and downstream breakage cannot tell whether the family is doing real predictive work or merely riding along with a correlated axis.

**Insight.** Fitting all five families' drift scores in one regression, rather than running five separate univariate tests, turns two questions that would otherwise need separate ad hoc procedures into two estimands read off a single shared fit: which family's coefficient is largest, and whether any coefficient is distinguishable from a no-signal null at all.

**Method.** For each version pair p, the five drift scores D_cap(p), ..., D_saf(p) are regressed jointly against the pair-level breakage average B̄(p). The resulting standardized coefficients are compared to identify the largest, and each coefficient's bootstrap confidence interval is checked against zero to determine whether that family carries statistically distinguishable predictive power. The same fit therefore answers both the ranking question and the existence-of-signal question without requiring a separate procedure for each.

### Functional Downstream Ground Truth

**Challenge.** The same magnitude of raw output difference between two versions can be functionally harmless for one downstream task and fatal for another, so a breakage definition based on undifferentiated output distance would conflate genuinely different outcomes.

**Insight.** Anchoring breakage to functional checks a task actually depends on — rather than to how much text changed — is what keeps a harmless rewording from being scored the same as a broken schema, and computing that check per task before pooling is what lets heterogeneous task sensitivities show up instead of being averaged away.

**Method.** For each (pair, task) cell, breakage B(p, t) is computed as one minus the fraction of the task's shared input set that passes deterministic functional-equivalence checks (schema validity, tool-call executability) combined with a semantic-diff threshold specific to that task. Per-task breakage scores are reported individually before being averaged into the pair-level B̄(p) used by the regression above, so a dominant task cannot silently swamp the signal from tasks with different sensitivities.

### Attrition-Gated Retrospective Corpus

**Challenge.** A retrospective corpus assembled by best-effort collection can silently shrink or skew toward whichever version pairs happen to remain reachable, since providers do not guarantee stable historical access — turning ordinary provider-side deprecation into an unexamined source of sample bias.

**Insight.** Gating a pair's admission on an explicit reachability check, and then directly comparing pairs that later become unreachable against pairs that remain reachable throughout, converts a hidden survivorship risk into a checkable property of the corpus rather than an assumption about it.

**Method.** A version pair is admitted to the corpus only after both versions are confirmed queryable on the full probe-plus-downstream input set under identical decoding. Open-weight checkpoint successions, whose checkpoints are immutably archived, form the corpus's backbone; dated closed-API snapshots are included as a secondary stratum precisely because they are the pairs most likely to become unreachable mid-study. Pairs that do become unreachable during the study are compared against pairs that remained reachable throughout on mean per-family drift magnitude, directly testing for systematic attrition bias rather than assuming the retained sample is representative.

### Collinearity-Aware Partial Attribution

**Challenge.** Behavioral axes are not statistically independent — a safety-behavior shift often co-occurs with a refusal-boundary shift — so a family's raw correlation with breakage can simply be borrowing a correlated family's signal rather than reflecting its own contribution.

**Insight.** A family's own marginal contribution is only visible once the variance it shares with the other four is accounted for; a raw pairwise correlation, considered alone, cannot distinguish a family's independent signal from a collinear echo of a different family's signal.

**Method.** For each family, a partial correlation with downstream breakage is computed net of the other four families, after a variance-inflation-factor check confirms the partials are not themselves ill-conditioned. Each family's raw pairwise correlation and its partial correlation are compared for rank-order agreement; a disagreement flags that the family's apparent predictive power in the joint regression above was, at least in part, borrowed from a correlated family rather than being independently its own.

### Noise-and-Paraphrase Stability Gate

**Challenge.** A drift-to-breakage correlation is only as trustworthy as the probe items and decoding samples it was computed from; if the correlation appears under one phrasing and disappears under another, it reflects instrument choice rather than a property of the audited versions.

**Insight.** A reported drift score should not be trusted as signal until it is shown to exceed ordinary within-version decoding noise, and a reported family ranking should not be trusted until it is shown to survive an independent rewording of the same probes — both are checkable properties of the instrument, not assumptions about it.

**Method.** For each family, a within-version null distribution is estimated by re-querying the same version under fixed and re-sampled decoding; a cross-version drift score is only credited toward the ranking if it exceeds the 95th percentile of that null. Separately, the full family predictive-power ranking is recomputed under at least three independently reworded paraphrase sets of the same probe items, and the ranking is only trusted if it remains stable (by Kendall rank correlation) across those sets. Only drift scores and rankings that clear both gates are allowed to inform the joint regression and partial-attribution modules above.

### Held-Out-Pair Evaluation and Axis-Matching Guard

**Challenge.** The probe battery is validated against the same already-occurred drift events it is meant to predict. That substrate is circular in two concrete ways: a predictive-power estimate scored in-sample on the pairs used to fit the model and select S* proves nothing about prospective use, and a probe family that is structurally a miniature downstream task — format-preference probes built from IFEval/JSON-schema items versus an extraction-to-JSON downstream task — would "predict" that task's breakage by self-similarity rather than by transfer.

**Insight.** Both circularities are checkable by construction rather than arguable after the fact: predictive claims can be scored exclusively on pairs held out of the fit, and the probe-versus-task cells where instrument and target share the same behavioral axis can be labeled in advance and quarantined from headline claims instead of discovered post hoc.

**Method.** All headline predictive-power estimates — every π_f and every subset AUC — are computed by grouped leave-one-pair-out cross-validation, grouped by model lineage so that pairs sharing update events never straddle a fold boundary; in-sample fits are reported only as diagnostics. Every (probe family, downstream task) cell is pre-labeled *same-axis* or *cross-axis*; same-axis diagonal cells (e.g., format-preference probes against format-sensitive extraction tasks, safety-behavior probes against moderation tasks) are reported for completeness but excluded from headline predictive claims, which must be carried by cross-axis transfer. As a construction constraint, probe item banks and downstream-task input sets are drawn from disjoint sources, sharing no items or generation templates. Finally, the temporal forward check (Experimental Setup) is elevated from disclosure to gate: if the forward-fitted subset's held-out performance falls far below the grouped-CV estimate, the prospective-use claim is voided, not merely caveated.

## Experiments

### Experimental Setup

**Datasets.** An open-weight version-pair corpus (successive checkpoints such as Llama-2→Llama-3, Qwen1.5→2→2.5, Mistral v0.1→v0.2→v0.3) forms the study's backbone, since these checkpoints are immutably archived and satisfy the reachability gate by construction. Dated closed-API snapshot pairs — restricted to dated snapshots still being served at study start — are included specifically as the stratum whose reachability is most at risk of provider-side deprecation during the study, so that the corpus-attrition-bias check has real at-risk pairs to test rather than only immutable ones. (Earlier snapshot generations such as gpt-3.5-turbo-0301/0613/1106 and gpt-4-0314/0613 were retired by mid-2024 and can no longer pass the reachability gate; they are named here only as examples of the attrition-risk *class*, not as admissible pairs — a retired snapshot could enter only via a previously released cached-output corpus, which fails the gate's identical-decoding re-query requirement and is therefore excluded.) Probe item banks are drawn one per family: capability (MMLU / GSM8K / BIG-Bench-Hard), refusal-boundary (XSTest / OR-Bench-style borderline requests), format-preference (IFEval-style constraints plus a JSON-schema-compliance set), calibration (a semantic-calibration item set with known correctness labels), and safety-behavior (harm-adjacent and out-of-scope prompts in the style of established over-refusal and operational-scope benchmarks). A downstream-task suite of roughly twenty real tasks (extraction-to-JSON, classification, retrieval-augmented answering, tool-call argument filling, moderation) supplies the shared input set used to compute functional breakage, chosen specifically to span format-sensitive, calibration-sensitive, and safety-sensitive tasks so that task heterogeneity (below) is testable rather than assumed.

**Compute budget.** At the confirmatory floor of |P| = 60 pairs, the full design costs roughly 60 pairs × (~1,500 probe items + ~4,000 downstream inputs) × 2 versions × ≥3 decoding repeats ≈ 2M generations — an order of magnitude above the pilot tier — budgeted as multi-GPU-weeks on the open-weight backbone plus a low-thousands-USD dated-API stratum. The pilot (3–5 pairs, ~500 probes, ~10 tasks, ≥3 repeats) is ~20–50k generations and serves as the go/no-go gate before that spend is committed.

**Placebo, checked before any research question.** Before any RQ below is scored, a null-by-construction placebo (P0) is run through the identical predictor and cross-validation pipeline used for RQ1/RQ2: the family-drift feature vector D(p) and the breakage target B̄(p) are randomly permuted across pairs (destroying the pair-level correspondence while preserving both marginals), and the same joint regression and grouped-CV subset search are re-fit on the shuffled data. Because the correspondence is broken by construction, P0's predictive power *must* be null: each family's shuffled coefficient CI must include 0 and the shuffled AUC's 95% CI must include 0.5, over at least 1,000 shuffles. A non-null result here does not mean a probe family predicts breakage — it means the pipeline itself leaks (e.g. a cross-validation grouping leak, or a per-pair nuisance such as prompt length or decode temperature shared by D and B̄), and the run is invalid: diagnosis and a re-run from the walking skeleton follow, not a scored verdict on any RQ.

**Baselines** (sanity-check comparators, not competitors to beat — this is a measurement study, and no research question here is framed as "our procedure beats baseline X"). Single-probe-family predictors (each family's drift alone) are the necessary comparator for any claim about which family dominates in the joint model. A constant base-rate predictor (B0), using no probe features at all, is the arm that justifies the problem exists: if no family or subset beats it, no probe carries usable signal — note that B0 is a performance floor to beat, not a placebo, since it cannot come out non-null by pipeline artifact the way P0 above can. A family-agnostic total-drift scalar (B-alt) — raw output difference across the entire probe bank, ignoring family structure, at the same query budget — is the rival explanation that any observed prediction is really tracking how large the update was overall rather than which axis it moved; the axis-attribution claims below only mean what they claim to mean if the family-structured predictor beats this scalar. An item-subset compression baseline, scoped to the capability family alone and modeled on existing benchmark-compression designs [EssenceBench2607], tests whether a cheaper within-family selection already captures most of the capability family's own signal. A random/uniform drift-score baseline anchors the lower end of the comparison.

**Metrics.** Standardized regression coefficients and their bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; partial correlations and variance-inflation factors; the ratio of cross-version drift to the within-version noise floor; Kendall rank correlation of the family ranking across paraphrase sets (minimum pairwise value across all paraphrase-set comparisons, not the mean, so one unstable pairing cannot be averaged away); cross-validated AUC for the subset-ablation criterion; and a TOST equivalence-test statistic comparing drift magnitude between retained and attrited pairs against a ±0.5 SD margin.

**Forward validation (a gate on the prospective-use claim) and cost accounting.** Because the audit protocol's intended use is prospective — run the minimal subset once per version bump — while the family ranking and subset are estimated on already-occurred pairs under lineage-grouped cross-validation, a temporal-split forward-validation check is additionally run: the family ranking and minimal subset are fit on the chronologically earlier version pairs and evaluated (subset AUC and ranking Kendall τ) on the latest held-out pairs, with the forward numbers reported alongside the grouped-CV ones. This check is a **gate on the prospective-use claim**, not merely a disclosed robustness axis: if the forward-evaluated subset AUC falls below the grouped-CV estimate by more than the pre-registered sufficiency margin δ, the prospective-use claim is voided and the protocol is reported as validated only retrospectively — the thin temporal fold (few pairs) reduces the gate's sensitivity, and that reduced sensitivity is itself reported, but it does not reduce the gate's bindingness. Alongside all results, a deterministic cost-accounting table reports queries, monetary cost, and wall-clock time per version bump for the selected minimal subset versus a full downstream re-test, quantifying the audit-overhead saving that the minimality argument rests on.

**Research questions:**
1. RQ1 — Which probe family's drift, if any, is the strongest predictor of downstream breakage once all five are considered jointly?
2. RQ2 — Does at least one probe family carry statistically distinguishable predictive power at all?
3. RQ3 — Does downstream breakage vary meaningfully across tasks with different sensitivities, rather than uniformly across the downstream suite?
4. RQ4 — Do version pairs lost to corpus attrition during the study differ systematically in drift magnitude from the pairs that remained reachable?
5. RQ5 — Does a family's disentangled (partial) contribution to breakage prediction disagree in rank order with its raw correlation, revealing collinearity-driven misattribution?
6. RQ6 — Does cross-version drift exceed within-version noise and remain rank-stable across independently reworded paraphrase sets?

#### RQ1: Which probe family's drift, if any, is the strongest predictor of downstream breakage once all five are considered jointly?

**Setup.** Pilot stage: 3–5 open-weight version pairs with immutable checkpoints, a probe bank of roughly 500 items across the five families, and a downstream suite of roughly 10 tasks. Scale-up target: **at least 60 version pairs** spanning both open-weight and closed-API strata (more archived open-weight successions plus dated-API snapshots, added as a config change, never as a reduction in per-pair task coverage), a probe bank of roughly 1,500 items, and a downstream suite of roughly 20 tasks — this floor is set by the power analysis below, not chosen for convenience. Joint regression: B̄ ~ Σ_f D_f, with bootstrap resampling (10,000 resamples) over version pairs to construct each coefficient's confidence interval; pairwise coefficient differences tested via paired bootstrap at α = 0.05, Holm-corrected across the ten family pairs.

**Power gate.** The effective sample size for this test is the number of version pairs |P|, not the ~400 (pair, task) cells — cells collapse into one B̄(p) per pair before the regression, so they are not independent replicates. By Fisher-z at two-sided α=0.05, detecting a true ρ = 0.30 (the effect floor below) has power ≈0.25 at |P|=20, ≈0.47 at |P|=40, and reaches 0.80 only at |P|≈85; the confirmatory paired-bootstrap test on π_top − π_cap (Holm-corrected across 10 family pairs) needs more pairs still. Consequently: at |P| < 40, RQ1 is reported as **exploratory** (point estimates and CIs only, no confirmatory verdict); at 40 ≤ |P| < 60 the ranking is pre-declared **inconclusive**, never a fallback-triggering failure; the confirmatory PASS/FAIL verdict below is scored only at |P| ≥ 60 (power ≥0.65). One further correction: version pairs within a single lineage (e.g., Qwen1.5→2→2.5 across multiple sizes) share update events and are not independent — all bootstrap resampling and cross-validation folds are therefore grouped by lineage, and when lineages contribute multiple pairs the power arithmetic above is read against the number of lineage-grouped update events, not the raw pair count, so the effective sample size is never overstated by lineage duplication.

**Expected outcome.** At |P| ≥ 60: the family with the highest bootstrap-median standardized coefficient is refusal-boundary or safety-behavior, not capability, with π_top ≥ 0.30, and the 95% confidence interval on the top-family-minus-capability coefficient difference lies strictly above zero.

**Falsification condition.** At |P| ≥ 60: refuted if capability's standardized coefficient is the largest, or if the top family's CI over capability includes zero — either would mean policy/behavioral drift is not, in fact, what governs downstream breakage more than raw capability regression does, contrary to the motivating premise that familiar capability re-testing under-covers the real risk. Below |P|=60, a miss is reported as inconclusive-for-power, not as a falsification of the hypothesis.

**Hypothesis (plain language).** The behavioral axis a team is most likely to already re-test (capability) is not the axis that actually governs whether their integration breaks.

**Metrics.** Standardized regression coefficients per family with bootstrap 95% CIs; paired bootstrap difference between the top family and capability.

#### RQ2: Does at least one probe family carry statistically distinguishable predictive power at all?

**Setup.** Same joint-regression fit as RQ1; each family's coefficient CI checked individually against zero, Holm-corrected across the five families.

**Expected outcome.** At least one family's coefficient has a 95% CI that excludes zero, establishing that probe drift carries real predictive signal for downstream breakage rather than all five being indistinguishable from a no-signal null.

**Falsification condition.** Refuted if every family's coefficient CI includes zero — that would mean none of the five behavioral axes, individually or in this joint specification, predicts downstream breakage, undercutting the premise that any observable probe is worth running at all (in which case the study's own B0 comparison in the Setup above should also show no probe beating the base rate).

#### RQ3: Does downstream breakage vary meaningfully across tasks with different sensitivities, rather than uniformly across the downstream suite?

**Setup.** Per-task breakage scores B(p, t) computed for every (pair, task) cell before pooling; compare the per-family drift-to-breakage correlation ρ_f(t) = Spearman ρ(D_f(p), B(p,t)) across two pre-declared task-sensitivity contrasts, examined and not searched post hoc: (i) a format-sensitive task (e.g., extraction-to-JSON) vs. a calibration-sensitive task (e.g., an answer-confidence task), and (ii) a safety-sensitive task (e.g., moderation) vs. a capability-sensitive task (e.g., a reasoning task), across all pairs, with Holm correction across the two contrasts examined.

**Expected outcome.** For at least one of the two pre-declared contrasts, the per-task correlations differ by at least 0.15 in absolute Spearman ρ, with the paired-bootstrap 95% CI on the difference excluding zero — i.e. they differ meaningfully in magnitude or in which family correlates most strongly, rather than tracking one shared, task-agnostic pattern.

**Falsification condition.** Refuted if neither pre-declared contrast clears the ≥0.15 bar — that would mean a single pooled breakage score loses little information relative to per-task scoring, undermining the case for defining breakage per task before aggregating; any heterogeneity would then be smaller than this design can resolve, narrowing rather than eliminating the claim.

**Hypothesis (plain language).** Downstream breakage is genuinely task-specific, not a single scalar quantity that happens to be measured in twenty places.

**Metrics.** Per-task drift-to-breakage correlation and its confidence interval; comparison test between task pairs.

#### RQ4: Do version pairs lost to corpus attrition during the study differ systematically in drift magnitude from the pairs that remained reachable?

**Setup.** Track, over the study's duration, which dated closed-API snapshot pairs become unreachable; for each family, compute the standardized mean difference in |D_f(p)| between the attrited subset and the subset that remained reachable throughout. This is tested as **equivalence, not as failure-to-reject**: a plain two-sample significance test is deliberately not used here, since with few attrited closed-API pairs a non-significant result would rubber-stamp "unbiased" from an underpowered test rather than from real evidence. Instead, a two-one-sided-test (TOST) equivalence test checks whether the standardized difference falls within a pre-declared margin Δ_eq = 0.5 SD, at α=0.05, Holm-corrected across the five families.

**Expected outcome.** Equivalence is established (both one-sided tests reject) for all five families — the standardized mean difference in |D_f(p)| between attrited and retained pairs falls within ±0.5 SD for every family, supporting that the retained corpus is not systematically biased by which pairs happened to remain queryable.

**Falsification condition.** Refuted (for a given family) if the TOST equivalence test rejects — i.e. the standardized difference exceeds the ±0.5 SD margin — meaning that family's retained sample is attrition-biased; that would mean the retrospective corpus systematically under-samples exactly the version pairs most informative about drift for that family, biasing headline rankings that involve it. If the attrited stratum has too few pairs to power the TOST at all, RQ4 is reported **inconclusive** for that family (an instrument/power failure, not a substantive null), and every headline ranking is reported both pooled and on the attrition-free open-weight-only subset as a robustness check.

**Hypothesis (plain language).** Losing access to some old API snapshots during the study does not quietly bias the corpus toward "boring" version pairs, by more than a pre-declared small-effect margin.

**Metrics.** Per-family standardized mean difference in |D_f(p)| between attrited and retained pairs; TOST equivalence test statistic against ±0.5 SD, per family.

## Related Work

**Cross-version change detection and update governance.** The closest prior work is a black-box change-detection line that already compares model behavior across version pairs without weight access: longitudinal evidence that hosted-model behavior drifts substantially across dated snapshots [ChenDrift2307]; sequential and two-sample audits that detect behavioral shift or silent modification of a deployed endpoint [AuditBehaviorShift2410; YouveChanged2504; RankUniformity2506]; distribution-equality and log-probability tests that decide which model an API is actually serving [ModelEquality2410; LogProbTracking2512; ModelSubstitution2504]; and multi-behavior fingerprints of endpoint stability and identity [BehavioralFingerprints2603]. All of these answer "did the model change?" — several with within-model sampling-noise nulls that this paper's noise floor deliberately parallels [ModelEquality2410; RankUniformity2506] — but none validates its change signal against a functionally-defined downstream-breakage ground truth, attributes predictive power to behavioral axes, or asks how few probes suffice. A complementary update-governance line answers the breakage question from the opposite end: by re-running direct downstream tests on every update [TestBeforeDeploy2604], by treating agent-skill drift as contract violation [SkillDriftContract2605], or by task-level output-drift checks for regulated workflows [LLMOutputDrift2511]. These establish that version updates do break integrations, but at exactly the per-bump cost a cheap probe protocol exists to avoid — and none asks which behavioral axis predicts the breakage they measure. The estimand claimed here — out-of-sample, axis-resolved probe-drift-to-breakage predictive power, with a minimal-sufficient family subset — is posed by none of these lines; conversely, this paper's gap claim is deliberately narrowed to that estimand, since cross-version behavioral comparison per se is now well covered.

An earlier, construct-specific member of the same audit family compares whether a tuned model release has departed from its base model along a shared behavioral construct, computing a multi-view similarity signature from prompts common to both — call it the cross-release behavioral-similarity line [BiasSimBSM2607]. It already establishes that a rigorous, weight-free audit of behavioral change between two model releases is possible, without needing to observe either model's parameters. But it audits one construct (fairness/bias) at a time and stops at a pairwise similarity score; it never asks whether the measured departure predicts whether any downstream application actually breaks, nor compares which behavioral axis is the better predictor when several are available — precisely the comparative, breakage-facing question this paper is built to answer.

**Benchmark compression and minimal-subset design.** Work in this space selects a small subset of benchmark items that jointly reconstructs full-benchmark scores and preserves cross-model rankings, modeling both semantic redundancy and per-item ranking redundancy to find the smallest set that still tells the same story a full benchmark would [EssenceBench2607]. This is the closest existing methodological cousin to this paper's own "how small can the sufficient set be" question, but it is answered for a different target: preserving fidelity to an existing score under one fixed metric, not predicting a separate downstream-breakage outcome across a version pair. A complementary line establishes that the capability/generation axis specifically is measurable and monitorable across releases with relatively few examples, grounding why a capability probe is a sensible member of the battery in the first place [CondGenBench0511].

**Classical antecedents, conceded.** The abstract shape of the minimality question — a minimal subset of cheap tests that retains fault-detection power — is not new: it is the core of the regression-test-selection and test-suite-minimization literature in software engineering [RothermelHarrold1997; YooHarrold2012], and "a cheap monitor predicts expensive degradation" is likewise the core of concept-drift detection in stream learning [GamaDrift2014]. This paper does not claim first-mover status on the minimality criterion itself; that criterion is inherited from these classical threads and credited to them. What breaks in the transposition is what those methods assume: a deterministic pass/fail oracle and white-box coverage information (test-suite minimization), or a streaming label feed (concept-drift detection) — none of which exists here, where the "fault" is a probabilistic behavioral drift on a chosen axis, observed black-box, across two discrete version checkpoints. Axis-resolved, oracle-free, black-box breakage prediction is the residual gap this paper targets.

**Single-axis behavioral instruments.** A cluster of recent work makes individual behavioral axes precisely measurable: a rank-invariant refusal metric computed as the correlation between a model's refusal probability and its error probability [RefusalIndex2607]; a semantic notion of calibration that collapses full generations into equivalence classes rather than requiring single-token confidence [BCalibration2607]; a benchmark for whether a policy-conditioned agent accepts in-scope requests while refusing out-of-scope ones, stress-tested under adversarial and multilingual conditions [OffTopicEval2607]; and a documented account of systematic, widespread false refusals concentrated in specific model layers [ProSafePrune2607]. Each is a strong, single-axis diagnostic evaluated at one point in time; none is compared against another axis, or regressed against a downstream-breakage ground truth, across a version pair — which is exactly the composite comparison this paper runs. Two further single-axis results ground the same point from opposite directions: pre-trained language model calibration is itself dynamic and state-dependent rather than monotonically improved by scale [PLMCalib9999], and LLM capability self-assessment is measurably overconfident and not monotonically better in newer or larger models [CapableOf4661] — both consistent with this paper's premise that behavioral axes genuinely drift across model changes rather than being fixed properties of "how capable" a model is. On the construction side of the same families, a linguistically grounded taxonomy of instruction conflict, embedded commands, and policy ambiguity supplies seed material for refusal-boundary and safety-behavior probe items whose labels are designed to remain meaningful as models change [AdversarialPragmatics1153], and a query-efficient behavior-elicitation method — motivated explicitly by static safety benchmarks saturating on newer model releases — offers a route to keeping such item banks diagnostic over time [ElicitBehaviorsMultiTurn3701]; both are single-snapshot instruments this paper's probe-item design can borrow discipline from, not cross-version breakage predictors. A same-archetype measurement study of one behavioral axis — agent loyalty under multi-party pressure, with a demonstrated leak/over-refusal trade-off frontier — exemplifies the axis-specific rigor this design aims to apply battery-wide [PrincipalLoyalty0383].

**Instrument brittleness and the training-side view of model updates.** A study of prompt sensitivity shows that semantically equivalent paraphrases of the same prompt can induce large behavioral swings, arguing that a single scalar measurement taken from one phrasing is an unreliable target for optimization [TARE2607] — a warning this paper takes seriously about its own drift measurements, not only about the prompts other work optimizes. In the same spirit, a standardized protocol for measuring evaluator preference dynamics documents that proprietary models silently update — causing measurements to decay within weeks — and responds with versioned reference snapshots [EvaluatorPrefDynamics0297]; it shares this paper's motivating phenomenon of silent version churn, but its estimand is evaluator win-rate coupling across versions, not axis-resolved downstream breakage. A related causal-intervention study shows that surface-form perturbations of a math problem can flip a model's correctness independent of the underlying reasoning required, a complementary robustness concern for the capability probe family specifically [CausalRobust6745]. From the opposite side of the interface, a study of parameter-space differences between a pretrained model and its finetuned variant makes model updates interpretable as structured vectors in weight space [WeightDiff2607] — but that route requires white-box access to both checkpoints, which an external API consumer auditing a version bump from the outside never has, motivating why an observable, black-box probe battery is the only lever available to that consumer at all. Finally, model-level modifications other than provider version bumps — e.g. compression via pruning, quantization, or distillation — have also been shown to shift a specific behavioral axis (fairness) differentially by technique, with intrinsic and extrinsic fairness metrics diverging [CompressFair0878], reinforcing that axis-specific behavioral drift under model change is a general phenomenon this paper's design must isolate from, not merely an artifact of provider-side version updates specifically.

## Conclusion

This work turns a governance assumption practitioners already rely on — that a cheap per-version probe can substitute for a full downstream re-test — into a measurable question with a stated identifiability structure, rather than treating any single probe family as self-evidently sufficient. The central methodological wager is that which axis governs breakage, whether any axis carries signal at all, and whether an observed correlation is real rather than a collinear echo or an instrument artifact are only answerable together, from one coordinated joint model with its own noise-floor and paraphrase-stability gates — not from five independent single-axis checks run in isolation. Whichever family or subset survives that gating, the finding is directly actionable: a team choosing what to re-test after a version bump would have evidence, rather than intuition, for which axis to watch and how small the watch-list can safely be. Four limitations bound this claim from the outset and point to concrete next steps rather than standing as afterthoughts: the retrospective design can only characterize version pairs that already occurred and remained reachable long enough to probe, leaving prospective prediction of a not-yet-shipped version's breakage for follow-up work with its own validation design; relatedly, because the probe set is validated against the same population of already-occurred drift events it is meant to predict, every headline predictive claim is scored on held-out pairs under lineage-grouped cross-validation with same-axis probe–task cells excluded, and the temporal forward check is binding — a large in-sample-versus-forward gap voids the prospective-use claim outright rather than surviving as a caveat; the per-family probe instruments borrow their refusal, calibration, and safety-scope constructions from cited single-axis work, and the judgment-based labels inside them (e.g., whether a given output counts as a refusal) do not receive the full inter-rater validation plan reserved for the downstream breakage classifier — their label reliability is a stated assumption to be reported, not a property certified here; and the collinearity-disentanglement step (RQ5) may itself reveal that several axes carry inseparably shared signal for some corpora, in which case the honest report is a correlated bundle rather than a single governing axis — a real possible outcome this design is built to detect and report, not to explain away.
