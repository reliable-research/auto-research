## Stage 1 — Problem Definition

Memory-pinning mechanisms for LLM agents keep governance constraints (safety rules, user directives, policy boundaries — 治理约束) resident and enforced while the rest of the context is truncated, summarized, or compressed. Every such mechanism presupposes an upstream step: deciding **which spans of the context are the rules**. This topic isolates that step and asks whether it is adversarially robust.

Formally: given a raw multi-turn agent context, classify each span as *governance constraint* (pin) vs. *non-constraint* (no-pin), under two adversarial pressures that operate purely through phrasing (semantic register), not through structural forgery:

- **Evasion (漏保 direction)**: a genuine constraint is rewritten into casual chit-chat register while its binding (deontic) force is preserved — the attacker wants the rule silently dropped (miss-pin).
- **Spoofing (错保 direction)**: ordinary chit-chat is rewritten into imperative/authoritative register without acquiring any real binding force — the attacker wants junk pinned (false-pin: pin-budget exhaustion, authority laundering).

Outputs: per-span pin decisions plus two direction-specific reliability rates — miss-pin rate (漏保率 = FN/(FN+TP) on true constraints) and false-pin rate (错保率 = FP/(FP+TN) on non-constraints) — and the robustness gap Δ = error(adversarial) − error(original). The study is a measurement study: it characterizes whether current identifier families survive register disguise, not a new defense.

**Critic:** Three weaknesses. (1) "Span" is undefined — is the unit a sentence, a turn, or an arbitrary character range? A pre-segmented formulation quietly deletes the localization half of the problem, and deployed pinning consumes unsegmented context. (2) The definition says "purely through phrasing, not structural forgery," but does not say why the structural channel is excluded — a reviewer will ask whether ChatInject-style role-tag forgery already subsumes this. (3) The [边界] label warns that constraint-hood is indexical ("let's keep this between us" is binding or phatic depending on speaker authority and conversational state); a problem definition that treats labels as crisp is ill-posed at exactly the boundary the topic is named after. (4) The scope relative to sibling T3 topics (pinning/repair mechanisms) is asserted but not stated as a claim boundary.

**Refined:** The problem is defined in two nested forms. *Pilot form*: binary classification over pre-segmented, length/topic-matched span pairs — this deliberately brackets localization to isolate the register-vs-force question first. *Deployment form*: joint localization + classification over continuous, unsegmented multi-session transcripts (span-level F1 with IoU ≥ 0.5), which is the form pinning actually consumes; both forms are in scope, sequenced. The structural channel is excluded *by design, as the complement*: role/privilege forgery is already covered (ChatInject, Instruction Hierarchy), whereas the semantic-register channel — content earning or losing constraint status through phrasing alone, with all channel metadata honest — is unmeasured; this study is the controlled isolation of that residual channel. Constraint-hood ambiguity is not assumed away but budgeted for: ground truth is annotator-adjudicated (κ ≥ 0.7 gate), and a boundary audit decomposes measured "errors" into genuine identifier failures vs. contested labels, so the headline rates are reported both with and without contested items. Claim boundary vs. siblings: T3.3 measures the identification precondition only; it makes no claim about pinning, retention, or repair mechanisms downstream (those are T3.1/T3.2 territory), and its deliverable is the reliability envelope those mechanisms must assume.

## Stage 2 — Motivation

1. **The precursor gap.** The pinning-repair line of work — including this project's own Tier-1 topics — assumes constraints are already identified: pinning preserves *what it has recognized as a rule*. If identification is not adversarially robust, every downstream guarantee is vacuous against an adversary who never attacks the pin, only the recognizer. No existing work isolates or measures this step (digest N3: triangulated absence across instruction/data-separation, injection, and memory-retention literatures).
2. **The framing shift makes it timely.** The field has moved from RAG (retrieve-then-answer, stateless) to **agent memory** (persistent, self-curated memory stores: MemGPT-style memory tools, session summarization, long-horizon agents). In RAG, a mis-classified span costs one answer; in agent memory, a mis-classified constraint is *silently dropped from the persistent store and stays dropped* — the error compounds across every future session. The identification problem graduated from a retrieval nuisance to a governance-integrity problem. [VERIFY: characterize the shift with 1–2 concrete memory-system citations (e.g., MemGPT, Letta, or 2025–26 agent-memory survey) before writing the intro claim.]
3. **Both attack directions are already live in adjacent forms.** Spoofing is demonstrated structurally: ChatInject shows low-privilege content wrapped in forged role tags gets treated as high-authority instruction. Evasion is demonstrated behaviorally: SeqMonitor's decomposition attacks show harmful intent hiding in benign-looking surface form. The semantic-register analogs for *constraint identification* are the untested composition of the two.
4. **Even without an adversary, salience misjudgment is documented.** MemoryCallback's "premature pruning" motivation (early content overwritten before its relevance is clear) is the benign version of miss-pinning; a rule stated once at turn 3 that governs turn 300 is its worst case. An adversary who additionally disguises register turns a benign weakness into an exploitable channel.

**Critic:** (1) Motivation 2 is the audit's strongest card but is currently vibes — "compounds across sessions" needs either a citation to a real memory system whose write-policy makes the one-shot-drop claim true, or a one-paragraph mechanical argument from how summarization-based memory actually works. (2) Motivation 1 proves the problem exists but not that it is *unsolved* — a reviewer may say "an LLM judge obviously solves this"; the motivation must pre-empt by noting that whether a frontier LLM judge survives *validated* register disguise is precisely the open empirical question (and the pilot's pre-registered primary hypothesis). (3) Nothing quantifies stakes: how large is a realistic pin budget, and does false-pin pressure actually bind? (4) The [VERIFY] discipline is right, but the doc should also mark which motivation survives if the verification fails.

**Refined:** Motivation 2 is tightened to a mechanism: in summarization/curation-based memory systems, context enters the persistent store only via a write-time salience judgment; a constraint judged non-salient at write time is not merely deprioritized but *absent from all future reads*, and — unlike RAG, where the source document persists and can be re-retrieved — there is no later correction signal until the moment of violation (this is C4-of-MemoryCallback made adversarial). This mechanical argument stands on its own even if specific system citations shift ([VERIFY] tags retained for the named systems). Motivation 1 is sharpened into a falsifiable stance: the study does not presume fragility — if the LLM-judge identifier proves robust (McNemar null retained), that is a publishable *positive* precondition result for the pinning line, so the measurement is valuable under both outcomes. Stakes for the spoofing direction are made concrete: pin budgets are finite (pinned tokens occupy every future context window), so false-pins are not cosmetic — the spoofer's win condition is crowding real rules out of a bounded budget; the experiment reports false-pin rate against an explicit assumed budget envelope rather than as an abstract rate.

## Stage 3 — Related Work

Three literatures triangulate the gap; none contains the task (all corpus entries below are real papers read via the local OpenReview corpus; see digest N3 for URLs).

- **Instruction/data separation & privilege.** *ASIDE* (OpenReview:C81TnwHiRM): architectural separation of instruction vs. data tokens via embedding rotation — but the role label is *given* at ingestion, not identified from content. *Instruction Hierarchy* (Wallace et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.13208): privilege by channel role (system > user > tool). Boundary: both relocate the authority label; neither computes it from content. A rule arriving in a low-privilege channel gets nothing; filler in a privileged channel is over-trusted. [VERIFY: also check StruQ / SecAlign as additional separation baselines — not in corpus digest, cite only after reading.]
- **Injection attacks & robustness benchmarks.** *ChatInject* (WVhgFSKniL): spoofing via forged role tags — structural, not semantic-register. *TensorTrust* (fsW7wJGLBd): human-authored attack/defense corpus; its defense prompts are real governance rules and serve as our constraint source pool. *SeqMonitor* (HQuboWvFA1): evasion via benign-looking decomposition, but for harm-halting, not retention. Boundary: all measure end-behavior compromise, never the span-level identification error that caused it.
- **Memory retention & constraint handling.** *PrefEval* (QWunLKbBGF): long-session preference following with *benign* LMSYS-Chat-1M distractors — conflates identification/retention/application, no adversarial look-alikes. *MemoryCallback* (1cymflI2Lh): premature-pruning fix, benign forgetting only, no adversary. *RECAST* (90tCp2KszA): constraint mining under fully cooperative conditions. *R2-Guard* (CkgKSqZbuC): robust classification over a *fixed, enumerable* safety taxonomy — governance constraints are open-ended and deployment-specific, so the MLN-over-categories strategy does not transfer.

Positioning: this study sits at the intersection — span-level identification (not end-behavior), semantic register disguise (not structural forgery), governance constraints for retention (not harm categories), adversarial (not benign distractors).

(3) The sibling-topic boundary (T3.x pinning papers) is described in prose but not in the related-work table where reviewers look for it.

## Stage 4 — Sub-Problems

1. **SP1 — Ground truth under indexicality.** Build a paired constraint/non-constraint corpus with defensible labels despite constraint-hood being context-dependent ([边界]). Deliverable: ~300 constraint spans (TensorTrust defense prompts + real agent policy/system-prompt rules) + ~300 length/topic-matched non-constraint spans (LMSYS-Chat-1M), dual-annotated, κ ≥ 0.7 gate, adjudication trail.
2. **SP2 — Validity-gated bidirectional adversarial rewriting.** *This is the named hard part.* Evade(c) must destroy register cues while *provably preserving* the obligation; Spoof(s) must inject authority register while *provably not creating* an obligation. The tension is intrinsic: the stronger the disguise, the more likely the obligation itself is damaged (or smuggled in), corrupting ground truth. Everything downstream is an artifact if this gate fails — it is the single biggest validity risk (digest t1).
3. **SP3 — Identification in continuous context.** Move from pre-segmented pairs to joint localization + classification in 8k–32k-token multi-session transcripts with items embedded at controlled depths; separate localization failure from classification failure.
4. **SP4 — Two-adversary asymmetric evaluation.** The evader wants miss-pins, the spoofer wants false-pins; threshold-shifting hardens one direction while softening the other. Sub-problem: report the frontier (per-direction rates + AUROC), then test whether an *adaptive* attacker (query access to the identifier) can exploit whichever direction the operating point leaves soft.
5. **SP5 — Failure decomposition.** Blind re-annotation of all errors of the best identifier to partition genuine identifier failure vs. contested-label cases, yielding both cleaned headline rates and a taxonomy of boundary cases (the [边界] deliverable).

**Critic:** (1) SP2's gate is named as the hard part but the gate's own failure mode is circular: the NLI entailment back-check is itself an LLM-family judgment — if the entailment checker shares blind spots with the identifiers under test, "validated" rewrites may systematically be the easy ones, biasing Δ downward. (2) SP1 and SP2 interact: annotators validating rewrites see the original (unblinded), which inflates agreement. (3) SP4's adaptive attack (≤20 queries/item) is underspecified — query access to *decisions* or *confidences*? (4) No sub-problem covers cross-lingual register (the seed and parent topic are bilingual; Chinese chit-chat/imperative register conventions differ), which is either a scoped-out threat or a missed factor.

**Refined:** SP2 gains a two-sided gate protocol: entailment back-check from a model family disjoint from both the rewriter and all identifiers, *plus* human validation where the annotator sees the rewrite alone (blinded) and must independently state whether it carries an obligation — agreement with the intended label is the gate; unblinded comparison is used only for adjudication. A held-out "gate audit" subsample is validated by a third annotator to estimate gate leakage, and the paper reports Δ under both the full set and the audit-clean subset. SP4 specifies two adaptive tiers: decision-only access (realistic black-box) as primary, confidence access as an upper-bound condition. Cross-lingual register is explicitly scoped out of the pilot and listed as a threat (external validity) with an English-only claim boundary; a small zh probe set (~50 pairs) is optional Stage-2 garnish, not load-bearing. SP list order = execution order; SP2 sits on the critical path before any identifier is run.

## Stage 5 — Methods

**5.1 Adversarial paired-dataset construction (GovSpan-Adv).**
- *Source pools*: constraint spans from TensorTrust released defense prompts (real, human-written access-control rules) + curated agent policy/system-prompt rules (second, structurally different pool; results reported per-source); non-constraint spans from LMSYS-Chat-1M single turns filtered for zero directive content, then matched to constraints on token length (±20%) and topic (embedding-cluster matching).
- *Rewriting*: LLM attack operators Evade/Spoof with the two-sided validity gate from SP2 (disjoint-family entailment check + blinded human validation; expected 20–30% rejection with regeneration; per-item validation metadata shipped). Rewriter family disjoint from every identifier family.
- *Forms*: (i) isolated pairs (pilot); (ii) in-context form — ~100 multi-session transcripts (8k–32k tokens, LMSYS filler), 3–5 items embedded per transcript at early/middle/late depths, no boundaries given.

**5.2 Identification models under test (5 families).** (a) keyword/deontic-cue heuristic (regex over modals/imperatives); (b) fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-base span classifier trained on original (non-adversarial) pairs only; (c) zero-shot frontier LLM-as-judge (pre-registered primary); (d) role/privilege-signal baseline (ASIDE-style: does channel metadata alone suffice? — expected to fail by construction, included to quantify how much the privilege paradigm leaves on the table); (e) cumulative-context prompted monitor (SeqMonitor-style: does trajectory context help?). Each emits pin/no-pin + confidence.

**5.3 Robustness evaluation protocol.**
- *Paired design*: same underlying item, original vs. adversarial phrasing; McNemar exact test per identifier × direction (10 tests, Holm–Bonferroni), discordant-pair odds ratios with 95% CIs; miss-pin, false-pin, Δ, AUROC reported per direction — never a single aggregate accuracy (keeps SP4's asymmetry visible).
- *In-context stage*: span-level F1 (IoU ≥ 0.5), depth-stratified miss-pin rates; localization vs. classification error decomposition.
- *Adaptive round*: rewriter gets ≤20 decision-only queries/item against the best Stage-1 identifier; report adaptive Δ vs. static Δ (paired bootstrap).
- *Failure decomposition*: SP5 blind re-annotation; all headline numbers reported with/without contested items; contested fraction with Wilson 95% CI.
- *Transfer probe*: run the best identifier over PrefEval's implicit preference statements as a bridge to an existing end-to-end retention benchmark.

**Critic:** (1) Training identifier (b) on original pairs only then testing on adversarial is the right fragility measurement, but a reviewer will demand the obvious next bar: adversarially-trained (b′) on half the rewrites — without it the paper reads "we didn't try the standard fix." (2) The LLM-judge prompt is a huge uncontrolled df; prompt variance across 3 paraphrases should be reported or the (c) result is one prompt's anecdote. (3) n=300/direction gives ~8pp McNemar detectability — fine for large gaps, but the *interesting* possible result (judge is nearly robust, Δ≈3–5pp) is underpowered; say so and pre-commit to interpreting it as an equivalence question (TOST margin) rather than chasing significance. (4) The five families conspicuously omit an embedding-similarity/retrieval-style identifier, which is what real memory systems actually use for write-time salience.

**Refined:** Add (b′) adversarial fine-tune arm (train on 50% of rewrites, test on held-out 50% + a fresh rewriter family) — turns the paper from "fragile" to "fragile, and here is how far the cheap fix goes," strictly stronger under any outcome. Add family (f): embedding-based salience scorer (cosine similarity to a constraint prototype set / memory-write heuristic), motivated as the deployed-practice baseline. LLM-judge results averaged over 3 pre-registered prompt paraphrases with per-prompt dispersion reported. Power: pilot's primary hypothesis stays McNemar-on-(c); additionally pre-register a TOST equivalence margin (Δ < 3pp counts as "robust for practical purposes") so a near-null is an interpretable positive finding, not a shrug. Compute per digest N6 remains: <4 GPU-h fine-tuning, ~$300–800 API total, ~30 annotator-hours — unchanged by the two added arms (+~$150 API, +8 annotator-hours).

## Stage 6 — Experiment Design

**Arms.** {6+1 identifier families: a, b, b′, c, d, e, f} × {original, evade, spoof} × {isolated-pair, in-context} + adaptive round on the best family + PrefEval transfer probe. Controls: original-phrasing condition is its own within-item control (paired design); per-source (TensorTrust vs. policy-pool) breakdown guards single-source artifacts.

**Scale.** Stage 1: 600 base spans, ~600 validated rewrites (~1,800 rewrite calls incl. regeneration), ≈ 17k classifier decisions. Stage 2: 100 transcripts × 7 identifiers; adaptive ≤ 20×600 queries. Annotation: ~40 annotator-hours (2 annotators + gate-audit third).

**Compute estimate.** Fine-tuning (b, b′): < 8 GPU-hours total on one A100/4090. All API (rewriting, gates, judge, long-context Stage 2, adaptive): ≈ $500–1,000. No cluster required. Effort: Stage 1 ≈ 3 person-weeks; Stage 2 ≈ 2–3 person-weeks; overlappable with writing.
